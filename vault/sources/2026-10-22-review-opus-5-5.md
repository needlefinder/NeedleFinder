## 0. Read this first: what is blocked on you

The research program is strong. However, **several headline claims in the brief don't survive a check against the primary sources**: the PMB 2019 full text and our own raw benchmark JSON. ### 0.1 The "3.2× shaft-accuracy improvement over PMB 2019" compares different metrics *(critical)*

- **PMB 2019's 2.0 ± 3.4 mm is not a shaft error.** It is **D_avg**: the 3D symmetric mean distance between voxel sets, averaged over both directions. The paper defines no "shaft" metric and uses no per-slice in-plane distance. I checked this against the PMC6726393 full text. Its companion metrics were HD 15.9 ± 20.5 mm and 95thD 6.9 ± 9.5 mm.
- **Our 0.62 mm is `mean_shaft_xy`.** That is a per-slice, in-plane (x, y) distance measured only over the slices where both curves exist, after matching at ≤ 3.5 mm. It ignores longitudinal (z) error by construction.
- **Our closest analogue to D_avg is `msd_3d`.** In the raw 54-case JSON (2026-10-21 physics run) it is **2.19 ± 2.94 mm (median 1.04)**, which is essentially equal to PMB 2019's 2.0 ± 3.4 mm. Our `hd_3d` is 12.5 ± 17.7 mm against PMB's 15.9 ± 20.5 mm.
- **The PMB "light" model is the ~2-minute one in the brief.** It reported D_avg = 1.6 ± 2.8 mm (FP 13.8%), which beats our current `msd_3d`.
- **What to do:** delete "3.2×" everywhere. Re-measure *every* method, including a PMB-2019-style 3D U-Net that we re-train or re-run, with one metric suite on one ground truth (see §3, Q1). The honest result today is: **in-plane shaft accuracy is at the inter-observer floor; 3D/longitudinal accuracy is not yet better than 2019.** That is still publishable, and §2 shows how to frame it.

### 0.4 Tip / longitudinal error is the real clinical gap, and the brief omits it

Needle-level numbers from the JSON, all 54 cases, physics run:

| Metric | Algorithm vs CY (mean / median / p95) | Inter-observer CY vs LB (mean / median / p95) |
|---|---|---|
| In-plane shaft (`mean_shaft_xy`) | **0.63 / 0.48 / 1.64 mm** | 0.85 / 0.59 / 2.47 mm |
| Slice-wise max HD (xy) | 1.44 / 0.97 / 4.09 mm | 1.43 / 1.00 / 3.78 mm |
| 3D mean surface distance | 2.19 / 1.04 / 9.34 mm | 3.10 / 1.68 / 13.07 mm |
| **3D tip error** | **11.8 / 3.5 / 49.7 mm** | 6.75 / 3.39 / 22.0 mm |
| Tip z error | 11.2 / 3.05 / 48.0 mm | 6.16 / 3.00 / 21.0 mm |

- **Where we are good:** in-plane, the algorithm is **at or below the inter-observer floor**. That is a genuinely strong, publishable result.
- **Where we fail:** median tip error matches the observers, but the **tail is roughly 2× worse** (p95 50 mm vs 22 mm). Some cases have *median* tip-z errors of 40–43 mm (Case 025, Case 065) or 19 mm (Case 066). These are truncations or over-extensions.
- **Why it matters:** in HDR planning the tip defines the first dwell position. A longitudinal error shifts *every* dwell position along the catheter. This error is dosimetrically far more important than 0.6 vs 2.0 mm in-plane (§3, Q4).
- **The paper must report tip error as a primary metric.** The 10-22 tables don't report it at all.

## 1. Why this is still relevant in 2026

### 1.1 Clinical drivers

1. **Interstitial needles are now routine in image-guided adaptive brachytherapy (IGABT), not exotic.**
   - EMBRACE II made combined intracavitary/interstitial (IC/IS) implants a protocol objective, targeting **≥ 20% of patients per centre**. The rationale is that dose-escalation aims and OAR constraints are hard to meet with intracavitary-only applicators in large or asymmetric HR-CTVs.
   - RetroEMBRACE showed IC/IS improves local control for HR-CTV ≥ 30 cm³ without added morbidity.
   - An industry summary claims EMBRACE I used IC/IS in 43% of patients and EMBRACE II in > 70%. *These are unverified secondary-source figures; cite the primary EMBRACE II reports instead.*
   - So the number of patients with 6–25+ catheters per fraction keeps rising.
2. **MR-only workflows remove the CT crutch.**
   - On CT, catheters (with metal obturators or markers) are bright and easy. On T2W MRI they are 1.5–2 mm signal voids that mimic vessels, fascia, rectal air and partial-volume edges.
   - Centres that adopt MR-only planning (one imaging session, no registration error, no CT/MR transfer under anaesthesia) must digitise directly on MRI. That is exactly this problem, and commercial auto-digitisation in MRI remains limited.
3. **Manual digitisation is the bottleneck while the patient is under anaesthesia.**
   - Shaaer et al. (JACMP 2021, MR-only GYN) measured **46 ± 10 min** of manual planner reconstruction, against 11 ± 1 min with their 2D U-Net-assisted pipeline.
   - Every minute adds anaesthesia time and the risk of applicator motion before treatment.
4. **Reconstruction error is a top-ranked uncertainty in interstitial BT.**
   - GEC-ESTRO/AAPM uncertainty reviews (Kirisits et al., Radiother Oncol 2014; Hellebust et al., Radiother Oncol 2010) rank applicator and catheter reconstruction among the largest contributors to delivered-dose uncertainty.
   - Interstitial-specific numbers are scarce. A head-and-neck analysis estimated **~5% PTV dose per mm** of catheter reconstruction error, and mis-reconstructed *abutting* needles produced D90 changes from −17.5% to +13.4%. *Both come from search snippets I did not read in full: verify before citing.*
   - The vault's "2 mm → > 10–20% dose deviation" has **no citation**. Replace it with our own dosimetric simulation (Q4) plus a properly cited per-mm sensitivity.

### 1.2 Technical drivers: why it isn't "solved by nnU-Net"

1. **Wrong output type.** A voxel mask isn't a catheter. Planning needs *N ordered, labelled 3D curves with tips*, one per source channel. Every voxel method, PMB 2019 included, needs a post-hoc instance step: connected components, skeletonisation or clustering. That step is where touching or crossing needles merge and where dropouts fragment tracks. PMB 2019 itself names "non-continuous segmentation" and "voxel classification without catheter identification" as limitations.
2. **Thin-structure geometry vs voxel losses.** Catheters are 3–6 voxels wide in-plane and 1 voxel (or less) through-plane at 3–6 mm slices.
   - Dice is dominated by the boundary voxels of a fixed-radius label cylinder, so Dice ≈ 0.6 is a **ceiling set by the label definition**, not by detection quality.
   - Topology losses such as clDice help continuity but still don't produce instances.
3. **Anisotropy.** Isotropic 1 mm resampling (PMB 2019) discards 2–4× in-plane information. Native-grid processing keeps it, but then the network must cope with 1.2–6.0 mm slice spacing in one model.
4. **Dense packing, asymmetric neighbourhoods and blooming.** Spacing can be < 4 mm. Needles hug the dark obturator or rectal air, and tips fade into fascia or blooming.
   - These are *instance-level geometric* ambiguities. Collinearity (second-order continuity) and mutual exclusion (two tracks can't occupy the same void) resolve them.
   - Neither is expressible in a per-voxel cross-entropy.
5. **Deployment reality.** BT suites rarely have a GPU workstation in the planning loop. A < 1 s CPU, ~10 MB, ONNX-in-3D-Slicer tool is a real deployment advantage over 2–9 min (PMB 2019, P6000 GPU). The claim needs a reproducible timing protocol (Q7).

## 2. Narrative and continuity (2013 → 2017 → 2019 → 2026)

### 2.1 Recommended thesis

> **Catheter digitisation is a structured *curve-set* estimation problem, not a voxel-labelling problem.** Each generation of this cohort's history solved one factor of that structure and exposed the next:
> - **2013** solved *local curve following* but required a human for *detection* (one click per tip).
> - **2017** showed that local following generalises across 54 patients and multiple observers, but still lacked *global detection*.
> - **2019** solved *global detection* with a 3D CNN, but lost *in-plane resolution* (1 mm resampling) and had *no notion of instances* (mask → skeleton), causing merges, fragments and FPs.
> - **2026** restores all three factors explicitly: native-resolution physics evidence plus dense geometric fields (local evidence), a slice graph with triplet collinearity (continuity), and set-level attention over candidate trajectories (mutual exclusion and identity).

This gives a principled, almost probabilistic decomposition that reviewers can check:

$$
p(\{\gamma_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\mid I)\;\propto\;\underbrace{\prod_i \prod_{z} \phi_{\text{local}}(\gamma_i(z)\mid I)}_{\text{Stage 0/1: physics + multi-head U-Net}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\prod_i \prod_{z}\psi_{\text{cont}}(\gamma_i(z\!-\!1),\gamma_i(z),\gamma_i(z\!+\!1))}_{\text{Stage 2A: triplet MPNN}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\Omega(\{\gamma_i\})}_{\text{Stage 2B: bundle set transformer}}
$$

- **Voxel segmentation models only the first factor.**
- **The Kalman / MICCAI-2013 tracker models the second factor, but for one needle at a time.**
- **Connected components implicitly set Ω to "non-touching"**, which is false here.
- **The ablation table (Fig. 7) then *is* the scientific claim:** each factor is switched off, and its contribution is measured by error type.

**Don't call it "the principled mathematical solution".** Call it "a structured decomposition that makes each failure mode addressable". The factorisation is a modelling choice, and reviewers will punish the absolute claim.

### 2.2 Retire the "five paradigms" framing

Five co-equal systems read as a bake-off and invite "what is the contribution?". Present **one system** (Stage 0 → 1 → 2A → 2B) and make the other paradigms **ablations or baselines**:

| Paper role | Brief's paradigm |
|---|---|
| Baseline A (classical, ours) | Physics 3D CV + Kalman/Radon (no learning) |
| Baseline B (2019 lineage) | 3D U-Net at 1 mm iso + skeletonisation (PMB-style, re-trained on our folds) |
| Baseline C (2026 SOTA voxel) | **nnU-Net (3d_fullres, native spacing) ± clDice** + skeleton/CC instance step. *Must be added; a reviewer at any venue will require it.* |
| Ablation: Stage 1 variants | NeedleUNet3D vs AxialTransUNet3D vs ensemble (supplement; the gain is small, so keep the simpler one) |
| Ablation: Stage 2 variants | tangent-tube tracking only → + MPNN → + set transformer |
| Ablation: input channels | image only vs + physics channels (answers "do the physics features matter?") |

If the Transformer gives no consistent gain (93.79% vs 93.32% Hungarian recall, Dice 0.554 vs 0.579), say so and **drop it from the main system**. A smaller, honest model wins review.

## 3. Top reviewer questions and how to pre-empt them

### Q1. "Is the comparison to PMB 2019 apples-to-apples?" (*certain to be asked*)

**Current state:** no. Different metric (in-plane shaft vs 3D D_avg), different GT annotator (CY vs HZ), different split (patient-level vs catheter-level), and a 2019 runtime measured on 2019 hardware.

**Pre-emption:**
1. **Re-train the 2019 architecture** (3D U-Net, 1 mm iso, Dice loss, skeleton/CC post-processing) on *our* patient-level folds against *our* GT. If the TF checkpoint is the published model, also run it as-is, with a caveat about split overlap.
2. Evaluate *all* methods with one metric suite: D_avg as PMB defined it, HD, HD95, in-plane shaft, tip 3D, tip z, angle, and FP/FN under PMB's "dilated-GT search volume" rule *and* our Hungarian rule.
3. Quote the published PMB numbers only in a separate "historical" table with a clear footnote on the protocol differences.
4. Expected honest headline: "at matched FP rate, the 2026 system reduces in-plane error from X to Y and 3D D_avg from A to B, while producing instance-labelled curves."

### Q2. "Is there data leakage or test-set tuning?"

**Risks:**
- The physics pipeline was iterated on cohort-wide error analysis.
- `minConfidence` (25–60%) is chosen post hoc on OOF predictions.
- Stage 2 models trained on Stage 1 outputs can leak if Stage 1 predictions on Stage 2 training cases aren't themselves out-of-fold.
- The ±10° rotation-augmented copies in PMB "valid" must not cross folds.
- The 2019 catheter-level split leaks patients, so reusing its checkpoint on any of its patients is invalid.

**Pre-emption:**
1. **Patient-level folds, fixed and published** (case IDs in the supplement).
2. **Nested selection.** Choose the operating threshold inside each training fold (e.g., maximise F1 on inner-OOF) and report the resulting outer-fold performance. Also show the full FROC so thresholds aren't cherry-picked.
3. **Stacked OOF.** State explicitly that Stage 2 is trained only on Stage 1 *out-of-fold* outputs.
4. **Freeze-then-test lock-box.** Before running more experiments, sequester ~10 cases (stratified by slice thickness and needle count) and touch them once at the end. If the cohort is already "burnt", say so and use external or phantom data as the lock-box (§5).
5. Report the physics-only baseline with hyperparameters frozen at the Case 050/016 values, alongside the final tuned version, so the effect of cohort-wide tuning is visible.

### Q3. "How good is the ground truth, and are you below the inter-observer floor?"

**Data available:** CY vs LB on 349 needles in 27 cases. Inter-observer in-plane shaft is 0.85 mm mean / 0.59 median, 3D MSD 3.10 mm, tip 3D 6.75 mm mean / 3.39 median.

**Pre-emption:**
1. **Main geometry figure = algorithm vs CY plotted against LB vs CY** on the *same* 349 needles. Use ECDFs and a non-inferiority test with a pre-stated margin, e.g. 0.25 mm in-plane.
2. Add a **three-way** analysis (algorithm, CY, LB): is the algorithm closer to the "consensus" (mean curve) than either observer is to the other?
3. Add HZ (PMB 2019 GT) as a third observer if recoverable.
4. **Annotation provenance.** State how CY/HT/LB annotated: manual polyline, or NeedleFinder 1-click tracking followed by correction. If annotation used the same intensity-minimum principle as Stage 0, disclose it and add a sensitivity analysis on a subset re-annotated fully manually.
5. **Short obturator needles have a single observer (HT).** Get a second reader on at least 10 cases (~60 needles).
6. **GT completeness.** Count 760 vs 826, and HT 304 vs JSON 315. Run a blinded FP adjudication: an expert reviews every unmatched prediction as "real catheter missing from GT", "artifact" or "duplicate". Report precision both raw and adjudicated. Precision of 68–84% is otherwise uninterpretable.

### Q4. "Does 0.6 vs 2.0 mm matter clinically? Show dose."

**Pre-emption:** a dosimetric simulation on every case, or on the subset with contours or plans.

1. **Plan on GT.** Generate dwell positions on the GT catheters: 2.5 or 5 mm step, active length from the GT tip, or the clinical plan if available. Compute TG-43 dose with an open Ir-192 source model; an open-source TG-43 engine is sufficient.
2. **Move the plan to predicted catheters.** Transfer the same dwell times to dwell positions on the *predicted* catheters, using the same arc-length offset from the predicted tip and the matched channel. Unmatched GT catheters stay as "missed", which is the clinically relevant failure.
3. **Report the dose differences:**
   - ΔD90 and ΔD98 of the HR-CTV if contours exist. Otherwise use a surrogate CTV: the GT-plan 100% isodose volume.
   - ΔV100, ΔV150 and ΔV200.
   - ΔD2cc for bladder, rectum and sigmoid if contoured. Otherwise use "dose at 5 mm outside the implant envelope" as an OAR surrogate.
4. **Decompose the error:** in-plane-only (tip z forced to GT), tip-only, and full. This is the figure that tells physicists *what to fix*. Expect tip/longitudinal error to dominate, consistent with the § 0.4 data.
5. **Compare each method on the same dose pipeline:** inter-observer (LB catheters vs CY), the 2019 baseline, nnU-Net, and ours.
6. **Pre-registered tolerance.** Use, for example, |ΔD90| < 5% and |ΔD2cc| < 10%. Report the % of patients within tolerance and the % that would need one-click tip correction.

### Q5. "Long interstitial vs short obturator: are you hiding a weak class?"

**Pre-emption:**
- **Always report per class** (long / short / pooled), with class-specific matching thresholds justified by length.
- Report class *assignment* accuracy as a confusion matrix, since planning needs the channel type.
- The physics-only baseline is 74% recall on the pooled 1,063 because it is long-tuned. Say this plainly and show the learned stages' gain is concentrated on short needles. The pooled number alone looks like a regression.
- State the obturator needle geometry (groove positions are mechanically fixed). Note a strong prior baseline: "applicator-model registration" (fit the known obturator template), which reviewers may suggest. Consider it as a baseline or prior.

### Q6. "Single site, single scanner. Does it generalise?"

**Pre-emption:**
1. **Within-cohort heterogeneity, actually measured:** 2D TSE vs 3D SPACE, 0.31–0.875 mm in-plane, 1.2–6.0 mm slices. Report a leave-one-protocol-out experiment, e.g. train on 1.6 mm, test on ≥ 3 mm.
2. **Controlled synthetic degradation:** downsample z to 3, 4.8 and 6 mm; vary SNR and bias field; show the curves.
3. **External data:** phantom, a public-background synthetic set, and partner-site data (§5). Even 5–10 external cases from one other centre transform the paper.
4. Keep the claim to "single-centre, 3T, multi-protocol", and state it as a limitation.

### Q7. "Runtime claims: on what, measuring what?"

**Pre-emption:**
- Specify the CPU model, threads, RAM, OS, ONNX Runtime version, and whether timing is cold or warm.
- State exactly what is included: DICOM/NRRD load, the filter, the networks, the graph build and the Slicer node creation.
- Report mean ± SD over all 54 cases.
- Reconcile the 0.27 s / 0.61 s claims with the raw JSON's 7–9 s.
- For 2019, re-time the re-trained baseline on the *same* machine; don't quote 2019-era P6000 numbers.
- Note that PMB's ensemble was **9 ± 2.5 min** and the light model 2 ± 0.5 min. The brief's "~1–2 min" is the light model only.

### Q8. "Your errors are conditioned on detection. Isn't the best model just the one that drops hard needles?"

The MPNN's 0.55 mm is measured over its own 91.8% recall set, against 95.2% for others. That is survivorship bias.

**Pre-emption:**
- Report geometry on the **intersection** of needles matched by all compared methods.
- Also report "error with misses penalised", e.g. a capped 3D distance for unmatched needles, or a FROC-style "fraction of GT needles reconstructed within d mm" curve over d. The latter is the single best summary plot.
- Use **patient-cluster bootstrap CIs** and paired per-patient Wilcoxon / mixed-effects tests. Never pool needles as i.i.d.

### Also prepare (shorter answers)

- **Why Dice at all?** It is label-radius-dependent, and our Dice 0.58 vs 2019's 0.60 isn't comparable across resolutions or radii. Move Dice to the supplement and use clDice or centreline metrics instead.
- **Is the 3.5 mm matching threshold generous given < 4 mm needle spacing?** Show the sweep over 1–5 mm.
- **Reproducibility:** release code, weights (10.5 MB), the evaluation script and fold files. Follow CLAIM 2024 / TRIPOD+AI reporting.
- **Failure-case honesty:** add a gallery of the 5 worst cases (Case 014, 029, 017, 025, 065) with the cause of each failure.

## 5. Public datasets and SOTA strategy

### 5.1 What exists (and what doesn't)

- **No public MRI brachytherapy catheter dataset with needle annotations.**
  - I searched for TCIA collections, open-data reviews and data-availability statements and found none.
  - The 2024 open-data review (Fechter et al.?, "*The use of deep learning in interventional radiotherapy (brachytherapy): a review with a focus on open source and open data*", PMC11156786 / arXiv 2205.07516) lists only gland/organ datasets (PROMISE12, NCI-ISBI 2013, ProstateX), not needles.
  - A 2024 CT-HDR paper states that no models are publicly available for CT-based HDR autosegmentation.
  - *I did not exhaustively check every paper's data statement; treat this as "none found" rather than "none exist".*
- **Closest related cohorts (all apparently private; data release unconfirmed):**
  - **MRI GYN:** Shaaer et al. 2021 (Sunnybrook; 20 patients, 360 catheters, 5 planners).
  - **MRI prostate:** Kostoulas, Guijt, Kerkhof, Pieters, Bosman, Alderliesten (SPIE MI 2025; Amsterdam UMC; 261 test needles). Tip median 1.07 mm, shaft median 0.75 mm, 0 FP/FN.
  - **MRI prostate HDR catheters:** Dai, Lei, Yang et al. (Emory, Med Phys 2020). *From memory; verify.*
  - **CT GYN:** Wang et al. 2023 (Adv Radiat Oncol; attention-gated 3D CNN; 56 plans / 17 patients).
  - **CT interstitial:** Xiao et al. 2026 (arXiv 2607.11290; 38 patients / 203 fractions; synthetic-to-real).
  - **US / pelvic seed implants:** arXiv 2509.17931 (tip–handle detection and matching).
- **Public data adjacent to the problem:**
  - **X-ray catheter/guidewire:** CathAction (~25–40k masks; phantom and porcine). It is 2D fluoroscopy, so only useful to show that the graph or set stage generalises to thin-curve instance problems. *Optional; probably distracting for PMB.*
  - **Public pelvic MRI as synthetic backgrounds:** PI-CAI (large prostate T2W), Prostate158, LUND-PROBE (432 prostate MRI + sCT + dose, found in this search), SynthRAD2023 pelvis (MR/CT pairs), the Gold Atlas pelvis. *Licences unverified per dataset.*

### 5.2 Recommended strategy, ranked by impact per effort

1. **Release the BWH cohort as the first public MRI interstitial-BT catheter benchmark.** This is the highest impact by far.
   - Contents: 54 de-identified volumes, CY/HT/LB curves, a fixed patient-level split, the evaluation code (Hungarian + tip + 3D + dose-surrogate), and a hidden lock-box served through Grand-Challenge.
   - This converts a "method paper" into a **lasting benchmark** and makes the 2013→2026 lineage citable forever.
   - *Needs BWH IRB / data-use approval (question 3 in §0.6).* Even a partial release (e.g., the RSNA-2016 Case 050 plus 10 cases) helps.
2. **Phantom with a CT gold standard (repeat and extend the 2013 gel-wax phantom).**
   - Image it on 3T and 1.5T with 2D TSE, 3D SPACE and bSSFP at 1.6, 3 and 6 mm slices; add crossing, touching and deflected needles.
   - It gives *non-annotator* ground truth, which directly answers Q3 and Q6, and is cheap.
   - Release it publicly with no IRB barrier.
3. **Physics-based synthetic MRI needles on public pelvic T2W backgrounds.**
   - Insert simulated voids (proton-density zero within the catheter radius, partial-volume convolution through the slice profile, optional obturator susceptibility dipole, Rician noise) along realistic template trajectories (Syed-Neblett geometry).
   - Uses: (a) controlled ablations of spacing, SNR and density; (b) a public synthetic benchmark anyone can regenerate; (c) pretraining. This mirrors Xiao 2026's synthetic-to-real idea, but for MRI.
4. **One external clinical site, 5–20 cases.** Candidates: Sunnybrook (Ravi/Shaaer), Amsterdam UMC (Alderliesten/Pieters; prostate MRI needles, a cross-anatomy test), or an EMBRACE centre with MR-only IC/IS. Even zero-shot external testing without fine-tuning is valuable.
5. **Open baselines inside the benchmark:**
   - nnU-Net (native spacing), nnU-Net + clDice;
   - the 2019 U-Net re-implementation;
   - a centreline-regression + tracking baseline (Kostoulas-style post-processing);
   - the physics-only tracker.
   Ship all of them as Docker or ONNX so others can reproduce the leaderboard.

### 5.3 Venue strategy

- **PMB:** the natural home (Zaffino 2019 lineage), and it values the dosimetric analysis.
- **Medical Physics:** an equally good fit, and the strongest audience for MR-only clinical deployment.
- **MedIA / TMI:** need external validation plus methodological novelty beyond the system. The factorised curve-set formulation with a public benchmark could qualify, but not with single-site data alone.
- **Short-term option:** a dataset or benchmark paper (e.g., *Scientific Data* or MICCAI datasets track), plus the method paper in PMB, maximises citations and impact.
