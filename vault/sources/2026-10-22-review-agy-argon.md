## 1. Referee Report (*Physics in Medicine & Biology*)

### Summary of the Contribution

This manuscript presents **NeedleFinder 2026**, an open-source 3D Slicer module and three-stage hybrid physics-and-deep-learning pipeline for automatic reconstruction of interstitial brachytherapy catheters on intraoperative 3T T2-weighted (T2W) pelvic MRI. Addressing the time-critical bottleneck of manual catheter digitization while the patient is under anesthesia in MRI-only high-dose-rate (HDR) gynecologic brachytherapy, the authors combine:
1. **Stage 0**: a multi-threaded native Rust 3D dark-core radial-symmetry filter (`NeedleFinderRust/src/lib.rs`) that computes a circular dark-core response $S_0$ across four physical radii ($1.35\text{–}3.3\text{ mm}$) and six diameters;
2. **Stage 1**: a 6-channel anisotropic 3D residual U-Net (`NeedleUNet3D`, 2.38M parameters) predicting catheter mask ($P_{\text{mask}}$), sub-pixel centerline peaks ($P_{\text{peak}}$), local trajectory slopes ($V_x, V_y$), and an obturator channel ($P_{\text{obt}}$);
3. **Stage 2A**: native C++17/Rust bidirectional Kalman seed tracking coupled with a 4-layer message-passing graph neural network (`NeedleSliceMPNN`) that scores inter-slice links using incoming/outgoing chord context and predicts sub-pixel offsets; and
4. **Stage 2B**: a bundle-level graph transformer (`TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer` pre-trained on 300 procedural synthetic trajectory bundles, **SynthBrachy3T**, and blended with a clinical `DenseGraphTransformer`), followed by a geometric implant-region gate, cranial tip refinement, and cubic polynomial smoothing (`Poly3`).

Evaluated on 54 intraoperative 3T T2W MRI scans (1,063 reference trajectories: 759 full-depth `CY` interstitial catheters and 304 short obturator/partial-field-of-view tracks) from Brigham and Women's Hospital (AMIGO suite), the full pipeline reports a pooled 5-fold out-of-fold 1-to-1 Hungarian F1 of **88.2%** (recall **91.3%**, precision **85.3%**, median shared-shaft in-plane error **0.46 mm**, mean **0.58 mm**; median 3D tip error **2.61 mm**, mean **7.90 mm**) in **2.74 s** on an 8-core CPU (**1.25 s** on a GPU), alongside a 40-scan dual-observer comparison (`CY` vs. `LB`/`AMe`) and a negative-control false-alarm evaluation on 48 prostate MRI scans from the Medical Segmentation Decathlon (`Task05_Prostate`).

**Overall Assessment**: The clinical motivation is strong, the combination of an analytical dark-core prior with slice-level and bundle-level graph neural networks is well-suited to *Physics in Medicine & Biology*, and the recent audit pass logged in `vault/wiki/log.md` has already fixed many earlier citation, table-formatting, and disclosure issues. However, my independent verification of the current manuscript (`manuscript.md`), JSON sources, and benchmark scripts confirms **critical unresolved methodological and data-integrity defects**—most notably that **6 of the 54 "out-of-fold" Stage 1 maps (including featured Case 017 in Figures 1 and 5a) were overwritten by an all-patient production training run (`fold99`) and none of the downstream results have been re-run on the regenerated clean OOF maps**, that the **few-shot ($k \in \{0, 1, 5, 10, 20\}$) experiment still blends in a 43-patient Stage 2B model and compares two different architectures/stages in Figure 4b**, and that **`2026-10-22-unified-apples-to-apples-benchmark.json` still contains incompatible baseline runs between Table 2 and Figure 3**. **Major revision with full re-execution of the downstream evaluation chain on clean, nested out-of-fold predictions is required before publication can be considered.**

### Major Points

#### Major Point 1: Unresolved In-Sample Data Leakage in 6 of 54 Stage 1 "Out-of-Fold" Test Cases (Including Featured Qualitative Case 017 in Figures 1 and 5a)
- **Manuscript location**: Abstract (lines 10–12), Key Terms (line 48), Figure 1 (line 37), Section 3.1 (lines 167–181), Section 3.2 (lines 191–193), Section 3.3 (lines 199–203), Table 2 (`tab2_main_results.tex`), Table 3 (`tab3_interobserver.tex`), Figures 3, 4, and 5 (`fig5_qualitative_mri_3d.pdf`).

Inside `train_one_fold()` (lines 234–245), the script wrote `oof_case_{v_idx:02d}.npz` for every index in `val_indices`, **overwriting the out-of-fold Stage 1 maps for 6 of the 10 cases** (`case_00`, `case_01`, `case_02`, `case_05`, `case_06`, `case_07`, corresponding to Cases 002, 007, 008, 016, 017, and 021) with in-sample predictions from a network trained on all 54 patients.

#### Major Point 2: Undisclosed 43-Patient Stage 2B Leakage and Apples-to-Oranges Comparison in the Few-Shot ($k \in \{0, 1, 5, 10, 20, 43\}$) Experiment
- **Manuscript location**: Abstract (line 12), Section 3.2 (lines 185, 189), Conclusion (line 235), and Figure 4b (`fig4_pareto_and_fewshot.pdf`).
- **Finding**: While the revised manuscript (line 189 and Figure 4b caption) now discloses that $k$ applies to Stage 2B while Stages 1 and 2A keep their full training folds, and that $k=0$ uses no learned bundle scoring, **two severe code-level defects remain in the $k \ge 1$ curves** plotted in Figure 4b and cited on lines 12, 189, and 235:
  1. **Undisclosed 43-patient Stage 2B blending at $k = 1, 5, 10, 20$**: In `finalize_synthbrachy3t_benchmark.py` (lines 207–245), the $k$-shot `TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer` predictions (`fs_outputs`) are decoded via `decode_hybrid_synth_nf2026_preds(bundle_items, base_oof_scores, fs_outputs, ..., w_synth=w_syn_k)` with $w_{\text{syn},k} = 0.73 + 0.004\,k$ for $k < 43$. Inside `decode_hybrid_synth_nf2026_preds()` (lines 111–116), the trajectory confidence blends `w_synth * c_syn + (1.0 - w_synth) * c_real`, where `c_real` comes from `base_oof_scores`—the clinical `DenseGraphTransformer` trained on **all 43 (or 44) clinical patients of the fold** (`run_synthbrachy3t_full.py` lines 405–429)! Thus, at $k=1$ (82.0% F1), $26.6\%$ of the Stage 2B score itself comes from a Stage 2B transformer trained on all 43 clinical patients.
  2. **The "without SynthBrachy3T pre-training" curve in Figure 4b (78.1% F1 at $k=1$) does not train Stage 2B at all**: Line 189 claims that at $k=1$, *"the fine-tuned Stage 2B reaches 82.0% F1, which is 3.9 points better than the same stage without synthetic pre-training."* In reality, the dashed comparator curve in Figure 4b (`few_shot_clinical_annotation_budget` in `2026-10-22-unified-apples-to-apples-benchmark.json`, generated by `run_xiao2026_and_fewshot_benchmark.py` lines 461–608) **never trains a Stage 2B graph transformer on $k$ patients**. Instead, it trains a 3-layer 3D convolutional network (`FewShotPhysicsAdapter3D`, 16 channels) on $k$ voxel volumes, blends its voxel probabilities with the **full 43-patient Stage 1 U-Net** (`w_full * p_full + (1 - w_full) * p_fs`), and uses that to re-score a candidate pool containing `nf2026_all_tau50` (the **full 43-patient Stage 2B output**)! Therefore, claiming that $82.0\%$ vs. $78.1\%$ ($+3.9$ F1 points) measures *"the same stage without synthetic pre-training"* is factually unsupported by the code.
- **Concrete fix**: Re-run the few-shot experiment in `finalize_synthbrachy3t_benchmark.py` by comparing (a) `TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer` pre-trained on SynthBrachy3T and fine-tuned on $k$ clinical bundles with **`w_synth = 1.0`** (zero blending with 43-patient `base_oof_scores`), (b) the **exact same `TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer` architecture** trained from random initialization on those same $k$ clinical bundles with **`w_synth = 1.0`**, and (c) at $k=0$, evaluating the SynthBrachy3T-pretrained `TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer` directly (`w_synth = 1.0`) alongside the unlearned Stage 0 + gate baseline.

#### Major Point 5: Hungarian Assignment Pre-Thresholding Defect, Shared-Length Matching Bias, and Undisclosed Wilcoxon Imputation
- **Manuscript location**: Section 2.5 (lines 144–151), Section 3.1 (line 179), Section 4.2 (line 231).
- **Finding**:
  1. **Global cost minimization up to 8.0 mm before 3.5 mm thresholding** (`evaluate_unified_apples_to_apples.py` lines 169–179): `cost_m[gi, pi]` is set to `mean_shaft_xy` for all pairs with `mean_shaft_xy <= 8.0` mm (`1e6` above 8.0 mm), `linear_sum_assignment(cost_m)` is solved globally, and pairs with `cost_m[gi, pi] > 3.5` mm are discarded *afterward*. Because costs in $(3.5, 8.0]\text{ mm}$ are not forbidden (`1e6`) prior to optimization, `linear_sum_assignment` can assign a pair at $3.8\text{ mm}$ to lower global cost and leave a valid $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ catheter unmatched.
  2. **Minimal shared-length requirement ($> 1.0\text{ mm}$)** (`evaluate_unified_apples_to_apples.py` lines 97–115): While line 231 now acknowledges that matching uses shared length only, `compute_unified_pair_metrics()` requires only `z_max_sh - z_min_sh > 1.0` mm of axial overlap. A 2 mm fragment overlapping any segment of a 70 mm catheter is scored as a true positive if its in-plane distance on that 2 mm overlap is $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$.
  3. **Undisclosed constant imputation on 0-match cases in paired Wilcoxon tests** (`evaluate_unified_apples_to_apples.py` lines 293–308): If a method has zero matched catheters on a patient, per-patient `mean_shaft_xy`, `msd_3d`, `hd95_3d`, and `tip_3d` are imputed as `3.5`, `10.0`, `20.0`, and `20.0` mm, and Wilcoxon $p$-values for tip error (`4.96e-4`, `1.06e-5`, `5.16e-9`) are computed on **per-patient means** while line 179 and Table 2 attach those $p$-values to **pooled medians** (`2.61 mm vs. 3.35 / 6.02 / 2.78 mm`).
- **Concrete fix**: Threshold `cost_m` at `3.5` mm (`1e6` for `> 3.5` mm) before calling `linear_sum_assignment`, report a length-completeness metric (or require $\ge 10\text{ mm}$ / $\ge 30\%$ overlap), disclose the 0-match imputation rule, and align the Wilcoxon test statistic (per-patient median vs. per-patient mean) with the summary statistic reported in the text.

#### Major Point 6: Unverified Runtime Claims (`2.74 s` CPU / `1.25 s` GPU) and Arithmetic Inconsistency in Cohort-Weighted Means
- **Manuscript location**: Abstract (lines 12, 14), Figure 1 (line 37), Section 2.2 (line 93), Section 3.4 (lines 211–213), Section 4.2 (line 229), Section 5 (line 235), Table 2 (`tab2_main_results.tex`), Table 5 (`tab5_literature.tex`).
- **Finding**:
  1. **Weighted-mean arithmetic contradiction**: Lines 12, 37, and 213 state that full-volume reconstruction takes **2.74 s** on average across the 54 scans on an 8-core CPU, consisting of **1.20 s** on standard $320 \times 256$ scans (20 scans according to `vault/wiki/log.md` line 35) and **3.47 s** on high-resolution $640 \times 416$ / $768 \times 768$ scans (34 scans). However, $\frac{20 \times 1.20 + 34 \times 3.47}{54} = \mathbf{2.629\text{ s}} \ne 2.74\text{ s}$ (indeed, $20 \times 1.20 + 34 \times 3.47$ only equals $2.74 \times 54$ if there were $17.4$ standard and $36.6$ high-res scans!). Similarly, `generate_pmb_figures.py` (line 258) hard-codes Stage 1 CPU times of `0.55 s` (standard) and `1.02 s` (high-res), whose 20/34 weighted mean is **0.846 s**, contradicting the **0.96 s** Stage 1 mean reported on lines 37 and 213.
  2. **Hard-coded literals in `generate_pmb_figures.py` without a 54-case timing log**: In `generate_pmb_figures.py` (lines 257–262), the stage runtimes (`0.41, 0.96, 1.22, 0.15` s CPU and `0.41, 0.22, 0.54, 0.08` s GPU) and baseline runtimes in Table 2 (`14.80, 3.85, 3.42, 2.68, 2.74` s) are hard-coded literals; `vault/reports/figures/data/runtime.csv` contains older numbers (`0.61 s`), and no per-case 54-scan timing JSON/CSV exists in `vault/sources/`. Moreover, claiming in the Abstract (line 14), Plain-Language Summary (line 25), and Discussion (line 229) that the method *"runs in 1 to 3 seconds"* or *"under three seconds"* is contradicted by its own reported **3.47 s** mean on the 34 high-resolution scans (63% of the cohort).
- **Concrete fix**: Save the actual per-case 54-scan timing benchmark output to `vault/sources/`, compute the exact cohort and subgroup means from those 54 measurements, reconcile the Stage 1 and total weighted means, and revise *"1 to 3 seconds"* / *"under three seconds"* to *"1 to 4 seconds (2.6–2.7 s on average)"*.

### Minor Points

1. **Stage 1 Architecture and Loss Description in Section 2.2 Still Match `AxialTransUNet3D` Instead of `NeedleUNet3D`** (Section 2.2, lines 99–106; Figure 2a):
   - *Issue*: Line 99 states that `NeedleUNet3D` *"starts with a $1 \times 3 \times 3$ in-plane residual block"*, and lines 103–105 state that the loss is *"binary cross-entropy with a positive weight of 12 plus soft Dice"* for $P_{\text{mask}}$, *"mean-squared-error loss up-weighted 25-fold at the peaks"* for $P_{\text{peak}}$, and *"mean-squared-error loss inside the mask"* for $(V_x, V_y)$. Those specifications come from `train_transunet3d.py` (`AxialTransUNet3D`, lines 139–140, 207–225).
