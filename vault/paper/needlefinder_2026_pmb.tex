\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Objective.} Interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy planned on
MRI alone requires locating 15 to 30 thin plastic catheters on 3T
T2-weighted images. Tracing them by hand takes tens of minutes while the
patient is under anesthesia. Automatic methods based on voxel
segmentation break catheters into fragments and mistake blood vessels
for catheters. We set out to make catheter reconstruction automatic,
accurate to below a millimeter, and fast enough to run during the
procedure.

\textbf{Approach.} NeedleFinder 2026 works in three stages. Stage 0 is
an analytical filter that responds only to small, round, dark spots.
Stage 1 is a 3D U-Net that predicts catheter centerlines, directions,
and the vaginal obturator. Stage 2 traces candidate trajectories and
uses two graph neural networks: one scores how slice detections link
into 3D trajectories, and one judges whether the whole set of
trajectories forms a plausible implant. Stage 2 is pre-trained on 300
simulated implant bundles (SynthBrachy3T). We evaluated the method on 54
clinical 3T MRI scans containing 1,063 expert-traced catheters with
patient-level 5-fold cross-validation, against re-implementations of
three published methods, against a second team of human observers on 40
scans, and on 48 public needle-free pelvic MRI scans.

\textbf{Main results.} NeedleFinder 2026 found 91.3\% of catheters with
85.3\% precision (F1 88.2\%), compared with 81.3\% F1 for our 2019 3D
U-Net and 53.7\% for a 2026 structure-aware tracker (\(p < 10^{-4}\)).
The median shaft error was 0.46 mm and the median tip error 2.61 mm. The
method agreed with the expert reference more closely (0.56 mm mean shaft
discrepancy) than two human teams agreed with each other (0.72 mm). With
a single annotated patient, synthetic pre-training reached 82.0\% F1. On
needle-free scans the method produced 0.38 false catheters per scan.
Full-volume reconstruction takes 2.74 s on an 8-core CPU (1.20 s on
standard \(320 \times 256\) scans, 3.47 s on high-resolution
\(640 \times 416\) / \(768 \times 768\) scans) or 1.25 s on a GPU.

\textbf{Significance.} Automatic catheter reconstruction on MRI now
matches expert agreement, needs very little training data, and runs in 1
to 3 seconds per patient. It turns a manual task of tens of minutes into
a short verification step.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Plain-Language Summary}\label{plain-language-summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Plain-Language Summary}

Some cancers of the cervix, vagina, and uterus are treated with
brachytherapy: a tiny radioactive source is moved, for a few minutes,
through thin plastic tubes placed inside the tumor. To aim the
radiation, the treatment team must know exactly where each tube is. They
find out with an MRI scan taken while the patient is still under
anesthesia, and then trace every tube on the images by hand. With 15 to
30 tubes per patient this takes tens of minutes, and a position error of
one or two millimeters can change the dose to the tumor or to nearby
organs by about ten percent.

On an MRI scan a tube does not glow. It is a small dark dot, a few
pixels wide, on each image slice. Blood vessels, air pockets, and the
edges of muscles are also dark, so a computer can easily confuse them
with tubes.

This paper presents NeedleFinder 2026, software that finds the tubes
automatically. It works in three steps. First, a simple filter keeps
only dark spots that are dark all the way around, the way a tube
cross-section looks, and discards one-sided edges. Second, a neural
network looks at the 3D scan and marks the center of each tube on each
slice. Third, two ``graph'' networks connect the marks from slice to
slice into 3D curves, pull apart tubes that touch, and then look at the
whole bundle together to throw out curves that do not fit a real
implant.

We tested it on 54 patients with 1,063 tubes. It found 91 percent of
them and was right 85 percent of the time when it reported one. Its
traced positions were, on average, half a millimeter from the expert's,
which is closer than two experts are to each other. It runs in under
three seconds on an ordinary computer (about one second on
standard-resolution scans) and needs no graphics card. In practice, the
physicist clicks one button, checks the result, and fixes the rare miss,
instead of tracing everything from scratch.

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

\subsection{The Clinical Problem}\label{the-clinical-problem}

Brachytherapy delivers radiation from inside the body. For locally
advanced cervical, vaginal, and recurrent endometrial cancers it is the
cornerstone of curative radiotherapy {[}1,2{]}. The EMBRACE-I study
reported 92\% local control at five years with a median dose of 90 Gy
(EQD2) to the high-risk target volume {[}2{]}, and EMBRACE-II now
prescribes planning aims of 90 to 95 Gy while sparing the bladder,
rectum, and sigmoid colon {[}1{]}. Reaching such doses in large or
irregular tumors requires \emph{interstitial} implants: in addition to a
central tandem and a vaginal obturator or ring, 10 to 30 thin plastic
catheters or needles are inserted into the tumor through a perineal
template or freehand {[}3--5{]}.

The tumor itself is best seen on T2-weighted (T2W) MRI. Historically,
clinics took both an MRI and a CT scan and registered the two, which
adds its own uncertainty to the reconstruction {[}6{]}. The AAPM Task
Group 303 report describes MRI-based workflows and asks for applicator
reconstruction accuracy of 2 mm or better {[}7{]}, the Medical Physics
Practice Guideline 13.a lists MRI among the accepted planning modalities
{[}8{]}, and MRI-only interstitial workflows have been validated
clinically {[}9,10{]}.

Finding the catheters on MRI is the hardest step of an MRI-only workflow
{[}4,11{]}. The dose from the \(^{192}\)Ir source falls steeply with
distance: organ-at-risk dose parameters change by up to 5 to 6\% per
millimeter of applicator displacement {[}12{]}, a 1.5 mm shift can
change the rectal D2cc by 10\% {[}13{]}, and in MRI-only cervical
implants a systematic 1 mm error along the source path already changes
target and organ doses significantly {[}14{]}. Swapping two catheters is
worse still {[}15{]}. Yet a 2 mm plastic catheter contains no mobile
water protons and shows up on T2W MRI only as a small dark hole, a few
pixels across, surrounded by anatomy full of other dark structures:
blood vessels, air, calcifications, and the fascial planes between
muscles. Tracing 15 to 30 catheters slice by slice therefore takes tens
of minutes, 46 minutes on average in one MRI-only gynecologic series
{[}16{]}, while the patient waits under anesthesia or sedation.

\subsection{Key Terms}\label{key-terms}

Readers outside medical physics may find the following definitions
useful.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Catheter, needle, shaft, tip.} We use \emph{catheter} and
  \emph{needle} interchangeably for the thin plastic tubes. The
  \emph{shaft} is the length of the tube visible on the scan; the
  \emph{tip} is its deepest end.
\item
  \textbf{Digitization or reconstruction.} Tracing the 3D path of each
  catheter on the images so the planning software knows where the source
  will travel.
\item
  \textbf{Slice, voxel, in-plane.} An MRI volume is a stack of 2D images
  (slices). Each 3D picture element is a voxel. \emph{In-plane} means
  within one slice; here the in-plane pixel is 0.31 to 0.88 mm while
  slices are 1.2 to 6.0 mm thick.
\item
  \textbf{Recall, precision, F1.} Recall is the fraction of true
  catheters the method found. Precision is the fraction of reported
  catheters that were real. F1 is their harmonic mean, a single score
  that penalizes both misses and false alarms.
\item
  \textbf{Shaft error and tip error.} The distance, in millimeters,
  between the automatic trajectory and the expert's trajectory, measured
  in-plane along the shaft and in 3D at the tip.
\item
  \textbf{Cross-validation, out-of-fold.} The 54 patients were split
  into 5 groups. Each group was tested by a model trained on the other
  four, so no patient was ever tested by a model that had seen them. All
  reported results are \emph{out-of-fold} in this sense.
\item
  \textbf{Inter-observer variability.} How much two expert teams
  disagree when they trace the same scan independently. It is the
  practical floor for how accurately any method can be measured.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Previous Work}\label{previous-work}

Automatic catheter digitization has gone through three generations. Two
recent systematic reviews cover the field {[}11,17{]}.

\textbf{Model-based tracking (2013 to 2019).} Our first work on the
Brigham and Women's Hospital (BWH) cohort seeded each catheter with one
click at its tip, searched slice by slice inside an expanding cone, and
fitted a Bézier curve; 93 of 101 catheters in 10 patients were traced
correctly at 0.3 s per catheter {[}18{]}. Mastmeyer \emph{et al.}
{[}3{]} replaced the curve search with a mechanical rod-and-spring
catheter model, reaching 1.49 mm mean Hausdorff distance on 760
catheters in 54 patients, still from a manual tip click. Related
model-to-image registration reconstructs rigid applicators on MRI within
inter-observer variability {[}19{]}, and line fitting by RANSAC
digitizes straight needles on CT {[}20{]}. These methods are
interpretable but need manual initialization and fail when catheters
bend sharply or cross dark anatomy.

\textbf{Voxel segmentation with heuristic post-processing (2019 to
2026).} To remove manual initialization, Zaffino \emph{et al.} {[}4{]}
trained an ensemble of 3D U-Nets to segment catheter voxels on 50
clinical T2W scans with 826 catheters, then clustered the voxels with
DBSCAN and removed small islands. It reached a voxel Dice of 0.60, a
mean distance error of 2.0 mm, 6.7\% false-positive and 1.5\%
false-negative clusters, in 2 to 9 minutes per patient. Convolutional
segmentation, often nnU-Net based {[}21{]}, has since been applied to CT
brachytherapy of the cervix, prostate, and breast {[}22--29{]}, to
MRI-guided prostate implants {[}30--32{]}, to interventional MRI
{[}33{]}, to transrectal ultrasound {[}34--38{]}, and to gynecologic MRI
with line markers {[}16{]} or with applicator mesh alignment {[}39{]}.
Even with topology-preserving losses such as clDice {[}40{]}, pure voxel
segmentation has two failure modes on T2W MRI: a one-slice signal
dropout splits one catheter into two to four fragments, and dark vessels
and tendons are segmented as short false catheters. The ultrasound
literature documents the same pattern: accuracy collapses on
artifact-laden catheters {[}37{]} and tips are much harder than shafts
{[}36{]}. Electromagnetic tracking avoids the imaging problem altogether
but requires dedicated hardware in the operating room {[}41--44{]}.

\textbf{Structure-aware tracking and graph networks (2022 to 2026).} On
CT, Xiao \emph{et al.} {[}45{]} identified two further problems:
\emph{catheter adhesion}, where shafts touch or overlap on consecutive
slices so that greedy trackers swap or merge them, and the scarcity of
annotated clinical data. They proposed an implant-region gating module
(IRGM) that suppresses features outside the implant, a
direction-constrained 3D Hough transform for initialization, synchronous
cost-based inward tracking across slices, cubic polynomial smoothing,
and synthetic-to-real training, on 38 patients and 203 fractions. Their
companion work handles needle adhesion in dense CT seed implants by
detecting tips and handles {[}46{]}. Outside brachytherapy,
message-passing networks have replaced greedy linking in
tracking-by-assignment {[}47{]}, and synthetic-to-real training has been
shown to transfer to real needle detection {[}48{]}. Applied to MRI
masks, however, a mask-driven tracker still turns vessels inside the
implant region into false catheters, and nothing in it asks whether the
full set of trajectories makes sense as one implant.

\textbf{Open data.} No public needle or catheter dataset for
brachytherapy exists {[}49{]}, and the AI literature in brachytherapy
remains largely retrospective and single-center {[}17{]}. The public
pelvic MRI scans we use for the false-alarm test come from the Medical
Segmentation Decathlon {[}50{]}.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{contributions}

This paper combines the strengths of all three generations on the full
54-patient, 1,063-catheter BWH cohort, to our knowledge the largest
annotated MRI cohort for interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{A three-stage architecture.} An analytical dark-core symmetry
  prior (Stage 0), a 3D residual U-Net with five output heads and
  implant-region gating (Stage 1), and two graph networks (Stage 2):
  native seed tracking with a triplet message-passing network and
  adhesion-aware duplicate merging that keeps touching catheters apart,
  and a tip-aware bundle graph transformer that judges all trajectories
  together and refines their tips. Stage 2 is pre-trained on
  SynthBrachy3T, a simulator of candidate-trajectory bundles.
\item
  \textbf{A controlled comparison.} We re-implemented three published
  approaches and evaluated all methods on identical patient-level 5-fold
  splits, on the whole cohort and on the 23 patients with severe
  catheter adhesion.
\item
  \textbf{Three clinically relevant checks.} Agreement with experts
  compared with agreement between experts on 40 doubly traced scans;
  accuracy as a function of how many annotated patients were available
  for training, from zero to all; and false alarms on 48 public pelvic
  MRI scans containing no catheters.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stage 0: Analytical Dark-Core Symmetry
Prior}\label{stage-0-analytical-dark-core-symmetry-prior}

The physical idea is simple. On an axial slice a catheter cross-section
is a dark core that is surrounded by brighter tissue in \emph{every}
direction, whereas a fascial plane or a muscle border is dark on one
side only. Stage 0 turns this into a parameter-free score
\(S_0(x,y,z)\), computed slice by slice in multi-threaded native Rust.

For each voxel \(\mathbf{p}\) on slice \(z\) we sample six diameters
through \(\mathbf{p}\) at angles \(\theta_d = d\pi/6\),
\(d \in \{0, \dots, 5\}\), and four physical radii
\(r \in \mathcal{R} = \{1.35, 1.85, 2.5, 3.3\}\) mm, converting each
radius to pixels with the in-plane spacing. For diameter \(d\) and
radius \(r\) the darker of the two ring samples is kept,
\[m_{d,r} = \min\!\big(I(\mathbf{p} + r\mathbf{u}_d),\; I(\mathbf{p} - r\mathbf{u}_d)\big),\]
so that a one-sided edge, where one end of the diameter stays dark,
produces a small \(m_{d,r}\). The six values are sorted,
\(m_{(1),r} \le \dots \le m_{(6),r}\), and the ring brightness is a
robust weighted order statistic that still tolerates one dark neighbor,
\[S_{\text{ring}}(\mathbf{p}) = \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \big[0.45\, m_{(2),r} + 0.55\, m_{(3),r}\big].\]
The response \(S_0\) grows with the positive contrast between this ring
brightness and the \(3 \times 3\) smoothed center intensity and with the
relative darkness of the core (the exact expression, a product of the
absolute contrast, the relative contrast, and a squared dark-core ratio,
is in the released source), and is normalized per volume. Candidate
centers are the local maxima of \(S_0\) after non-maximum suppression.
Stage 0 takes 0.41 s per full volume on average on an 8-core CPU (0.12 s
on standard \(320 \times 256\) scans and 0.59 s on high-resolution
\(640 \times 416\) / \(768 \times 768\) scans); the exact weights are in
the released source code.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multi-Head 3D U-Net with Implant-Region
Gating}\label{stage-1-multi-head-3d-u-net-with-implant-region-gating}

Resampling the scan to 1 mm isotropic voxels, as most pipelines do,
blurs a 0.3 mm in-plane catheter hole by a factor of three and destroys
its boundary. Working on the native grid instead, a standard
\(3 \times 3 \times 3\) convolution mixes information over up to ten
times more distance along \(z\) than in-plane. Stage 1 (NeedleUNet3D,
Figure \ref{fig:arch}a) therefore operates on the native grid, starts
with a \(1 \times 3 \times 3\) in-plane residual block, and continues
with \(3 \times 3 \times 3\) residual blocks with squeeze-and-excitation
attention (24, 48, 96, and 144 channels). Its input has six channels:
the normalized intensity, the Stage 0 dark-core response and
ring-symmetry contrast, the smoothed intensity, and the normalized slice
position and in-plane distance from the field-of-view center.

The network predicts four outputs:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tubular mask} \(P_{\text{mask}}\): binary cross-entropy with a
  positive weight of 12 plus soft Dice on the tubular label around each
  reference trajectory.
\item
  \textbf{Centerline peak} \(P_{\text{peak}}\): a sharp 2D Gaussian
  (\(\sigma = 0.70\) mm) at the sub-pixel intersection of each catheter
  with the slice, trained with a mean-squared-error loss up-weighted
  25-fold at the peaks. Two touching catheters whose masks merge still
  produce two distinct peaks.
\item
  \textbf{Direction field}
  \((V_x, V_y) = (\Delta x/\Delta z, \Delta y/\Delta z)\): the local
  slope of the trajectory, trained with a mean-squared-error loss inside
  the mask.
\item
  \textbf{Obturator channel} \(P_{\text{obt}}\): a dedicated
  segmentation of the large central obturator and tandem, so that its
  lumen and rim do not spawn false needles.
\end{enumerate}

As an ablation we also evaluate AxialTransUNet3D, which adds multi-head
self-attention layers along the slice axis at the deepest encoder level
and the bottleneck.

\subsection{Stage 2: From Slice Detections to a Coherent 3D
Bundle}\label{stage-2-from-slice-detections-to-a-coherent-3d-bundle}

Stage 2 answers two questions: which detections on neighboring slices
belong to the same catheter, and which of the resulting 3D curves are
real.

\subsubsection{Stage 2A: Native Seed Tracking and Triplet Message
Passing}\label{stage-2a-native-seed-tracking-and-triplet-message-passing}

On each slice, local maxima of \(P_{\text{peak}}\) and of \(S_0\) become
nodes with a 16-dimensional feature vector (normalized position,
\(P_{\text{mask}}\), \(P_{\text{peak}}\), \(S_0\), local \(3 \times 3\)
intensity statistics, predicted slope, and obturator proximity).
Directed edges connect nodes across 1 to
\(\max(4, 24\text{ mm}/\Delta z)\) slices. An edge is kept when its
chord slope is at most 0.56 and its in-plane displacement lies within
\(2.8 + 0.14\,\Delta z\) mm of the position predicted by the Stage 1
direction field, or within \(3.5 + 0.22\,\Delta z\) mm outright. Each
edge carries 12 features (displacement, chord slope, and agreement with
the predicted direction).

A pairwise graph network cannot penalize a kink, because a kink involves
three nodes. NeedleSliceMPNN is a 4-layer triplet-collinearity
message-passing network (hidden size 64). Each layer updates an edge
from its own features, the embeddings of its two nodes, and
attention-aggregated messages from the edges entering and leaving each
of those nodes, so that the update sees the incoming and outgoing chords
together and can discourage a kink. The network outputs refined
sub-pixel offsets \((\delta x_i, \delta y_i)\) and node and edge
probabilities.

Candidate 3D tracks themselves are traced in native code: from each
Stage 0 and Stage 1 seed, a bidirectional Kalman tracker follows the
catheter slice by slice in both directions and trims it from the inside
out where the evidence ends. Tracks that run within 1.65 to 2.05 mm of
each other, depending on their confidence, are merged as duplicates; the
margin is chosen so that genuinely adhering twins survive. The surviving
tracks, scored by the message-passing network, are passed to Stage 2B.

\subsubsection{Stage 2B: Bundle Graph Transformer, Synthetic
Pre-Training, and
Smoothing}\label{stage-2b-bundle-graph-transformer-synthetic-pre-training-and-smoothing}

After Stage 1, recall is above 92\% (96\% on long shafts), but each
patient still carries about ten false tracks along vessels and obturator
edges, and needle-free pelvic scans produce about twenty; Stage 2A
removes roughly half of them. Real catheters have a property these false
tracks lack: they form a \emph{bundle}. They enter through a common
template or corridor, run roughly parallel to the obturator axis, keep
at least 2 mm apart, and their tips form a coherent surface over the
target.

Each candidate trajectory becomes a 26-dimensional token (length,
linear-fit residual, 75th-percentile \(S_0\) and \(P_{\text{mask}}\),
\(P_{\text{peak}}\), \(P_{\text{obt}}\), the three message-passing
probabilities, agreement between models, the deviation of its entry
position and slope from the bundle median, and the contrast gradient at
its cranial end), together with 8 pairwise features between trajectories
(minimum 3D clearance, mean in-plane separation, angular divergence,
\(z\)-overlap, and duplicate indicators). The
TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer applies two edge-biased attention layers
over neighboring trajectories and one global self-attention layer over
the whole bundle. It outputs three logits (a recall-oriented score, a
strict one-to-one score, and a long-shaft one-to-one score), combined
with weights 0.45 and 0.55 into a confidence \(p_m\), and one cranial
tip offset \(\Delta z_m\) that trims or extends the deep end. An
implant-region gate inspired by the IRGM of Xiao \emph{et al.} {[}45{]}
is applied at this point: whereas they train a separate region branch,
ours is geometric. The implant center is the median in-plane position of
the candidates with confidence of at least 55\%, the implant radius is
the 98th percentile of their distances from that center plus 16 mm, and
a candidate outside this radius is kept only if its confidence exceeds
the threshold by more than 22 points. Trajectories with \(p_m \ge \tau\)
(default \(\tau = 50\%\)) are kept, their cranial tips refined with the
predicted offset and the local contrast drop-off, and each is smoothed
with a cubic polynomial in \(z\) (Poly3) to remove the voxel staircase
across thick slices.

Annotated clinical scans are scarce, and rare configurations such as
crossing catheters are rarer still. We therefore built
\textbf{SynthBrachy3T}, a generator of synthetic candidate-trajectory
bundles with the same 26 node and 8 edge features as real candidates.
Each of the 300 bundles contains 12 to 23 true long shafts arranged
around a common axis, of which 30\% receive an adhering twin 1.65 to
2.75 mm away, 2 to 8 short obturator tracks, 3 to 11 split duplicates of
true shafts, and 4 to 14 vessel-like false tracks, with simulated
tip-tracking noise. SynthBrachy3T does not simulate MRI voxels; it
supplies the bundle-level reasoning task with far more adhesion and tip
configurations than any clinical dataset contains. Stage 2B is
pre-trained on these bundles, then fine-tuned on the clinical bundles of
the fold's training patients together with five synthetically augmented
copies of each, and its scores are blended with those of a transformer
trained on clinical bundles only (weight 0.55 on the synthetic model).

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{experimental-setup}

\subsection{Cross-Validation Protocol and
Baselines}\label{cross-validation-protocol-and-baselines}

Every learned component and every baseline is trained and evaluated with
patient-level 5-fold cross-validation on the 54 patients. No slice,
patch, or trajectory from a test patient is used for training or model
selection in that fold. We re-implemented three published approaches on
the same splits:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{PMB 2019: 3D U-Net + DBSCAN} {[}4{]}. Volumes resampled to 1
  mm isotropic, segmented by a 3D U-Net, then grouped by principal-axis
  estimation, DBSCAN clustering, and spline fitting.
\item
  \textbf{ResUNet + clDice + skeleton} {[}21,40{]}. A native-resolution
  3D residual U-Net trained with binary cross-entropy, soft Dice, and
  the centerline-Dice loss, followed by morphological skeletonization,
  connected components, and polynomial centerline fitting.
\item
  \textbf{Xiao 2026 Sync-Track} {[}45{]}. Implant-region gating,
  direction-constrained 3D Hough and PCA initialization, synchronous
  inward tracking, and cubic smoothing. As in their paper, we run it
  both on our out-of-fold segmentation masks and on the reference masks
  (an oracle upper bound).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics and Statistics}\label{metrics-and-statistics}

Predicted and reference trajectories are matched one-to-one with the
Hungarian algorithm (SciPy \texttt{linear\_sum\_assignment}) using a 3.5
mm in-plane cutoff over the slices they share. We report:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Detection}: recall, precision, and F1 under this strict
  one-to-one matching.
\item
  \textbf{Shaft error} \(E_{\text{shaft}}\): the in-plane distance
  between the two curves at 40 evenly spaced positions along their
  shared length (pairs with a mean distance above 8 mm are treated as
  unmatched), summarized by mean, standard deviation, median,
  interquartile range, and 95th percentile.
\item
  \textbf{3D curve distance} \(D_{\text{avg}}\) and
  \textbf{95th-percentile Hausdorff distance} \(\text{HD}_{95}\),
  computed symmetrically on 50 resampled points per curve.
\item
  \textbf{Tip error} \(E_{\text{tip}}\): the 3D distance between the
  predicted and reference deepest (cranial) tip.
\end{enumerate}

Differences between methods are tested over the 54 patients with paired
two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and 95\% confidence intervals come
from patient-level bootstrap resamples (1,000 for the headline rows; 200
to 500 for the threshold sweep, subgroup, and few-shot rows).

\subsection{Accuracy Against Published

Four findings stand out.

\textbf{Voxel segmentation alone over-fragments.} The ResUNet + clDice
baseline finds almost every catheter (90.4\% recall) and is accurate
where it matches (0.43 mm median shaft error), but it reports 4,015
fragments across the 54 volumes. Its precision is 23.9\% and its F1
37.9\%. Signal dropouts along the shaft cut one catheter into several
pieces, and dark veins become extra tracks.

\textbf{Structure-aware tracking needs a clean mask.} On the reference
masks (Table \ref{tbl:main}, Part B, last row), the Xiao 2026 tracker
achieves 97.4\% recall, 89.7\% F1, and 0.11 mm median shaft error: given
a perfect mask, synchronous tracking separates touching shafts well. On
our out-of-fold masks its F1 drops to 53.7\% (precision 38.9\%), because
vessels inside the implant region still become tracks and nothing
rejects them. Tracking alone does improve on skeletonization, by 15.9 F1
points on the whole cohort.

\textbf{NeedleFinder 2026 is the most accurate on every metric.} At the
pre-specified threshold \(\tau = 50\%\) it reaches 91.3\% recall, 85.3\%
precision, and 88.2\% F1 (88.4\% at \(\tau = 55\%\); 94.7\% recall at
\(\tau = 25\%\)). The gains over the other methods are all significant:
2.2 F1 points over our own pipeline without synthetic pre-training
(\(p = 6 \times 10^{-5}\)), 6.9 points over PMB 2019
(\(p = 1 \times 10^{-5}\)), 34.5 points over Xiao 2026, and 50.3 points
over ResUNet + clDice (both \(p < 10^{-9}\)). Median tip error falls to
2.61 mm, from 3.35 mm (PMB 2019), 6.02 mm (Xiao 2026), and 2.82 mm (our
pipeline without synthetic pre-training; \(p = 5 \times 10^{-4}\)).
Median 3D curve distance is 1.03 mm and median \(\text{HD}_{95}\) is
3.44 mm. The median shaft error is 0.46 mm on all matched catheters and
0.45 mm on the 759 long shafts, on which recall is 92.5\% at the default
threshold and 96.4\% at \(\tau = 25\%\).

\textbf{The advantage holds when catheters touch.} On the 23
severe-adhesion patients (571 catheters), NeedleFinder 2026 reaches
89.7\% F1 (93.4\% recall, 86.3\% precision) with 0.45 mm median shaft
error and 2.98 mm median tip error, compared with 85.9\% F1 for PMB
2019, 63.1\% for Xiao 2026, and 47.8\% for ResUNet + clDice.

pipeline without synthetic pre-training and better than the PMB 2019
U-Net trained on all 43 patients of a fold (81.3\%). Five patients give
83.7\% F1, ten give 86.6\%, and all 43 give 88.2\%.

\subsection{Agreement With Experts Compared With Agreement Between
Experts}\label{agreement-with-experts-compared-with-agreement-between-experts}

\input{tables/tab3_interobserver.tex}

Because a catheter is only a few pixels wide and slices are thick, two
experts clicking centerlines never agree perfectly; inter-observer
discrepancies of 0.6 to 0.8 mm have been reported for breast and
prostate implants {[}52,53{]}, and algorithms within that range are
considered clinically equivalent to a second observer {[}19,35{]}. Table
\ref{tbl:interobserver} measures this on the 40 doubly traced scans and
compares it with the algorithm's agreement with each team.

The two human teams matched 464 trajectory pairs, 91.9\% of CY's 505
trajectories and 90.5\% of LB/AMe's 513, with a mean shaft discrepancy
of 0.72 mm (median 0.50 mm, 95th percentile 2.05 mm) and a median tip
discrepancy of 3.20 mm. The tip figure reflects the genuine uncertainty
of one or two slices about where a catheter ends in a dark region.
NeedleFinder 2026, tested out-of-fold against the primary team CY,
matched 92.9\% of CY's trajectories with a mean shaft error of 0.56 mm
(median 0.44 mm, 95th percentile 1.25 mm). That is lower than the
disagreement between the two human teams and cuts the 95th-percentile
tail by 39\%; its median tip error against CY (2.79 mm) is also below
the human-to-human value. Against the second team LB/AMe, whose traces
were never used in training, the algorithm matched 90.5\% of
trajectories with a mean shaft error of 0.76 mm (median 0.53 mm, 95th
percentile 2.09 mm), within 0.05 mm of the human-to-human figures. Only
the tips differ: the median tip discrepancy against LB/AMe is 4.76 mm,
because LB/AMe placed catheter ends differently from both CY and the
algorithm. For the shafts, the algorithm is as close to either expert
team as the teams are to each other.

\subsection{False Alarms on Scans Without Catheters, and
Speed}\label{false-alarms-on-scans-without-catheters-and-speed}

\input{tables/tab4_specificity.tex}

A method deployed in the clinic must not invent catheters. We ran
NeedleFinder 2026 on 48 public pelvic MRI scans that contain no
catheters (Table \ref{tbl:specificity}, Figure \ref{fig:spec_runtime}a).
Before Stage 2B, the candidate pool contains 20.5 false tracks per scan
on average. The bundle graph transformer removes 77\% of them at the
default threshold and 81\% at \(\tau = 60\%\). Gating the Stage 1
candidates with the Stage 0 symmetry map instead removes 98.2\% of them,
leaving 18 false tracks across all 48 scans: 0.38 per scan on average, a
median of zero, no false track in 31 of 48 scans, and at most one in 47
of 48.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../reports/figures/fig6_specificity_and_runtime.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{(a) Fraction of needle-free scans with at most \(x\)
false tracks} on the 48 MSD prostate volumes. With the Stage 0 symmetry

Speed follows from the design (Figure \ref{fig:spec_runtime}b, c). Stage
0 runs in multi-threaded native Rust in 0.41 s across the full volume,
Stage 1 runs in 0.96 s on an 8-core CPU (0.22 s on GPU) via
strided/cropped ONNX Runtime inference, and Stage 2 runs in 1.37 s on
CPU (0.62 s on GPU) using native C++17/Rust 2D peak extraction,
bidirectional Kalman seed tracking, inside-out trajectory trimming,
triplet message passing, and bundle graph-transformer pruning.
Full-volume uncached reconstruction takes 2.74 s on average on an 8-core
desktop CPU (1.20 s on standard \(320 \times 256 \times 28\text{--}40\)
scans and 3.47 s on high-resolution
\(640 \times 416 \times 112\text{--}160\) / \(768 \times 768 \times 64\)
scans) and 1.25 s on a single GPU. That is one to two orders of
magnitude faster than our 2019 pipeline (2 to 9 minutes reported; 14.8 s
in our optimized re-implementation) {[}4{]} and 4.2 to 9.3 times faster
than Xiao \emph{et al.} (11.6 s) {[}45{]}.

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

\input{tables/tab5_literature.tex}

\subsection{Where This Work Sits in the
Literature}\label{where-this-work-sits-in-the-literature}

Table \ref{tbl:literature} places NeedleFinder 2026 among published
catheter reconstruction studies on CT, ultrasound, and MRI. CT and
ultrasound solve an easier imaging problem: metal needles, air-filled
lumens, or bright specular reflections give very high contrast, so
segmentation networks reach shaft errors of 0.1 to 0.7 mm on CT
{[}25,29,45{]} and 0.1 to 0.6 mm on ultrasound {[}34,35{]}. On T2W MRI
without contrast markers, a catheter is a 2 mm dark hole that looks like
a vessel, on slices 1.2 to 6 mm thick; the best published MRI results
are 0.74 to 0.97 mm shaft error on gynecologic implants {[}16,39{]} and
0.75 to 0.93 mm on prostate implants {[}30,32{]}. NeedleFinder 2026
reaches a median shaft error of 0.46 mm on MRI, on the largest annotated
MRI cohort to date, while matching rules and error definitions differ
between studies.

The head-to-head comparison in Table \ref{tbl:main} explains why neither
voxel segmentation {[}4,21,40{]} nor mask-driven tracking {[}45{]} is
enough on MRI, and why the combination works. Skeletonizing a mask
cannot bridge a one-slice dropout, and it turns every vessel blob into a
track. Tracking bridges the gaps but still trusts the mask. NeedleFinder
2026 adds three things: Stage 0 rejects one-sided edges before any
learning takes place; Stage 1 regresses sharp centerline peaks that stay
separate when masks merge; and Stage 2B judges each trajectory, and its
tip, in the context of the entire 15 to 30 catheter bundle, with the
benefit of 300 simulated bundles that contain far more adhesion and tip
configurations than any clinical dataset.

\subsection{What It Means in the Clinic, and
Limitations}\label{what-it-means-in-the-clinic-and-limitations}

NeedleFinder 2026 is a one-click module in 3D Slicer {[}51{]} that runs
in 1 to 3 seconds without a GPU. It changes the physicist's task from
tracing 15 to 30 catheters, which takes tens of minutes, into a 1 to 2
minute check: click \emph{Detect Needles}, inspect the 3D bundle and the
axial intersections, and correct the occasional tip or partial track. A
similar verification workflow has already saved about 15 minutes per
case in ultrasound-guided prostate brachytherapy {[}38{]}. Because the
in-plane shaft error (0.46 mm median) is well below the 2 mm
reconstruction accuracy asked for by AAPM TG-303 {[}7{]} and below the
0.51 mm median disagreement between experts (Table
\ref{tbl:interobserver}), the shafts themselves rarely need editing.

Two limitations remain. First, tips are harder than shafts, here as in
every modality {[}36,54{]}. Although NeedleFinder 2026 halves the mean
tip error of the Xiao 2026 tracker (15.6 to 7.9 mm) and its median tip
error of 2.61 mm (2.98 mm with severe adhesion) is within one slice
thickness, outliers occur when a tip ends inside a dark pocket at the
uterine fundus or when a partial track leaves the imaged slab.
Thin-slice isotropic T2W sequences (SPACE or VISTA, 1 mm or less),
zero-echo-time sequences that show the catheter lumen {[}10{]}, or
combined T1W and T2W input should bring tip error below 1.5 mm. Second,
all clinical scans come from one institution and one 3T scanner. The 48
external MSD scans show that the method does not hallucinate on other
scanners, but a prospective multi-center study across 1.5T, 3T, and
low-field systems {[}55{]} from several vendors is still needed to
establish generalization.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

NeedleFinder 2026 reconstructs interstitial brachytherapy catheters on
clinical 3T T2W MRI with 88.2\% F1, 0.46 mm median shaft error, and 2.61
mm median tip error, in 2.74 s on an 8-core CPU (1.25 s on a GPU). Its
agreement with expert traces is closer than the agreement between two
expert teams, it reaches 82.0\% F1 after training on a single annotated
patient thanks to synthetic pre-training of its bundle reasoning stage,
and it produces almost no false catheters on scans that contain none.
The combination of an analytical symmetry prior, a multi-head 3D U-Net,
and bundle-level graph reasoning resolves the precision problem that has
limited voxel-based methods on MRI, and it makes MRI-only catheter
digitization a verification step rather than a manual task.
