\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Objective.} Magnetic resonance (MR)-only adaptive high-dose-rate
(HDR) interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy has become the
international clinical standard following the EMBRACE II protocol and
the 2025 AAPM Task Group 317 recommendations. However, manual
digitization of 15--30 polymeric interstitial catheters on
intraoperative 3T T2-weighted (T2W) MRI remains a major clinical
bottleneck requiring 15--40 minutes under procedural anesthesia, while
pure voxel-wise 3D convolutional segmentation networks suffer from
trajectory fragmentation across signal dropouts and catastrophic
false-positive hallucinations along pelvic vessels and fascial
boundaries.

\textbf{Approach.} Continuing our multi-institutional research lineage
on catheter digitization at Brigham and Women's Hospital (Pernelle
\emph{et al.} MICCAI 2013; Mastmeyer \emph{et al.} 2017; Zaffino
\emph{et al.} \emph{Phys. Med. Biol.} 2019), we present
\textbf{NeedleFinder 2026}, a two-stage physics-informed deep learning,
synthetic data pre-training (\textbf{SynthBrachy3T}), and relational 3D
graph architecture evaluated across 54 clinical 3T T2W MRI datasets
containing 1,063 expert-contoured trajectories (759 full-depth
interstitial shafts and 304 short/obturator reference tracks) plus 48
public needle-free 3T pelvic MRI volumes
(\texttt{MSD\ Task05\_Prostate}). Stage 0 computes an analytical 3D
multi-radii circular dark-core symmetry prior (\(S_0\)) in native
Rust/C++. Stage 1 feeds the T2W volume and \(S_0\) into an anisotropic
3D residual U-Net equipped with an Implant-Region Gating Module (IRGM)
and five dense prediction heads (tubular mask, 2D Gaussian centerline
peak, directional tangent slopes
\(\Delta x/\Delta z, \Delta y/\Delta z\), and vaginal obturator
channel). Stage 2 constructs a 3D multi-slice candidate graph and
applies a 4-layer Triplet Message-Passing Neural Network
(\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN}) with synchronous Hungarian cost-matrix
adhesion separation, followed by a global
\texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer} (2-layer Edge-Biased Graph
Attention Network + 2-layer Set Transformer with cranial/caudal endpoint
boundary refinement) pre-trained on 1,500 physics-based synthetic 3T
brachytherapy bundles (\textbf{SynthBrachy3T}) and 3rd-order polynomial
trajectory regularization (\texttt{Poly3}).

\textbf{Main results.} Under strict patient-level 5-fold out-of-fold
cross-validation across all 1,063 catheters, \textbf{NeedleFinder 2026 +
SynthBrachy3T + Tip-Aware} achieves \textbf{91.3\% one-to-one Hungarian
recall, 85.3\% precision, and 88.2\% F1} at \(\tau=50\%\) (reaching
\textbf{88.4\% F1} at \(\tau=55\%\) and \textbf{94.7\% recall} at
\(\tau=25\%\), with \textbf{96.4\% recall} and \textbf{88.3\%
obturator-adjusted F1} on the 759 long interstitial shafts),
statistically significantly outperforming our non-synthetic
\texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026\ +\ IRGM\ +\ Poly3} baseline (\texttt{86.0\%}
F1, \(p = 6.42 \times 10^{-5}\)), re-implemented isotropic 3D U-Net +
DBSCAN (\texttt{PMB\ 2019}: 81.3\% F1, \(p = 1.15 \times 10^{-5}\)),
native-resolution 3D ResUNet + \texttt{clDice} + skeletonization (37.9\%
F1, \(p = 2.85 \times 10^{-10}\)), and structure-aware 3D Hough +
synchronous inward cost-matrix tracking (\texttt{Xiao\ et\ al.\ 2026}:
53.7\% F1, \(p = 2.28 \times 10^{-10}\)). On the 23-patient
\textbf{Severe Catheter-Adhesion Subgroup} (\(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\)
spacing), our method achieves \textbf{93.4\% recall, 86.3\% precision,
and 89.7\% F1} (\textbf{90.3\% F1} on long shafts) with \textbf{2.98 mm
median tip error}. Across all matched shafts, \textbf{NeedleFinder 2026}
achieves a mean in-plane localization error of
\textbf{\(0.55 \pm 0.36\text{ mm}\) (\(0.45\text{ mm}\) median)} and
reduces 3D distal-tip error by 49.5\% (\(15.64 \to 7.90\text{ mm}\)
mean, \textbf{\(2.61\text{ mm}\) median}, \(p = 5.16 \times 10^{-9}\)
vs.~\texttt{Xiao\ 2026} and \(p = 4.96 \times 10^{-4}\)
vs.~\texttt{IRGM\ +\ Poly3}). On a 40-patient dual-observer cohort
(\texttt{CY} vs.~\texttt{LB}/\texttt{AMe}, 481 paired trajectories),
algorithm-to-human agreement (\(0.55 \pm 0.36\text{ mm}\), median
\(0.45\text{ mm}\), P95 \(1.19\text{ mm}\)) surpasses human
inter-observer agreement (\(0.71 \pm 0.58\text{ mm}\), median
\(0.51\text{ mm}\), P95 \(2.02\text{ mm}\), \(p < 10^{-4}\)). In
few-shot Sim-to-Real clinical training, \textbf{SynthBrachy3T}
pre-training enables \textbf{75.1\% zero-shot F1 (\(k=0\))} and
\textbf{82.0\% one-shot F1 (\(k=1\) single training
patient)}---surpassing the full 43-patient \texttt{PMB\ 2019\ 3D\ U-Net}
(\texttt{81.3\%}) with just one annotated scan---and reaches
\textbf{84.5\% F1 at \(k=5\)}, while achieving \textbf{0.38
false-positive tracks per volume (median 0.0)} on 48 out-of-distribution
needle-free pelvic MRI volumes in \textbf{\(0.61\text{ s}\) on CPU
(\(0.35\text{ s}\) on GPU)}.

\textbf{Significance.} Combining analytical 3D circular dark-core
symmetry priors with multi-head 3D convolutional feature learning,
synchronous adhesion-aware triplet message passing, and global
bundle-level graph attention resolves the precision--recall bottleneck
of voxel-only segmentation in MR-guided interstitial brachytherapy,
delivering sub-millimeter, sub-second catheter digitization within the
human inter-observer floor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

\subsection{Clinical Evolution of MR-Guided Interstitial Gynecologic
Brachytherapy
(2013--2026)}\label{clinical-evolution-of-mr-guided-interstitial-gynecologic-brachytherapy-20132026}

Image-guided adaptive brachytherapy (IGABT) is the cornerstone of
definitive radiotherapy for locally advanced cervical, vaginal, and
recurrent endometrial malignancies (Pötter et al. 2018, 2021). Over the
past decade, multi-institutional prospective outcomes from the EMBRACE I
and EMBRACE II studies (Pötter et al. 2018, 2021) have demonstrated that
escalating the dose to the high-risk clinical target volume
(\(\text{CTV}_{\text{HR}} D_{90} > 85\text{--}90\text{ Gy }\text{EQD2}_{10}\))
while respecting strict organ-at-risk (OAR) constraints for the bladder,
rectum, and sigmoid colon yields 5-year local control rates exceeding
92\%. Achieving this therapeutic window in patients with large,
asymmetric, or parametrial-invading tumors requires combined
intracavitary and interstitial (IC/IS) applicators---where a central
tandem and vaginal obturator or ring are supplemented by 10 to 30
titanium or polymeric interstitial needles inserted through perineal
templates (such as the Syed-Neblett or Venezia/Geneva templates) or
freehand (Mastmeyer et al. 2017; Zaffino et al. 2019; Beaulieu et al.
2025).

While computed tomography (CT) provides high Hounsfield contrast for
metallic stylets or air-filled catheter lumens, soft-tissue delineation
of the gross tumor volume and parametrial extension requires T2-weighted
(T2W) magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Historically, many clinics
acquired both a post-implant MRI and a planning CT, relying on
deformable or rigid image registration that introduces
\(1\text{--}3\text{ mm}\) systematic fusion uncertainties (Schindel et
al. 2013). Consequently, recent consensus guidelines---including the
2025 American Association of Physicists in Medicine (AAPM) Task Group
317 report on high-dose-rate (HDR) brachytherapy treatment planning
(Beaulieu et al. 2025) and Medical Physics Practice Guideline (MPPG)
13.a (Richardson et al. 2025)---strongly endorse transitioning toward
\textbf{pure MR-only adaptive brachytherapy workflows} (Deufel et al.
2024; Moradi et al. 2025).

However, catheter reconstruction on clinical 3T pelvic T2W MRI remains
the single most error-prone and labor-intensive step in an MR-only
workflow (Zaffino et al. 2019; Xiao, Liu, et al. 2026). Because
High-Dose-Rate \(^{192}\text{Ir}\) sources exhibit an inverse-square
dose gradient of approximately
\(6\%\text{--}12\%\text{ per millimeter}\) within the target volume
(Tanderup et al. 2008; Schindel et al. 2013), a
\(1.5\text{--}2.0\text{ mm}\) catheter shaft or tip reconstruction error
translates into a \(10\%\text{--}20\%\) dosimetric deviation to
\(\text{CTV}_{\text{HR}}\) and adjacent OARs. On spin-echo T2W MRI, 6F
(\(2.0\text{ mm}\) diameter) plastic catheters contain no water protons
and manifest only as small signal voids (``dark holes'' of 3--5 pixels
in-plane) embedded within heterogeneous pelvic anatomy containing signal
dropouts, chemical-shift artifacts, periprostatic/parametrial blood
vessels, calcifications, air pockets, and dark muscular fascial planes.
Manually digitizing 20--28 catheters slice-by-slice takes an experienced
medical physicist 15 to 40 minutes while the patient remains immobilized
under general anesthesia or conscious sedation (Zaffino et al. 2019; Xie
et al. 2023; Xiao, Zhou, et al. 2026).

\subsection{Related Work and Scientific Lineage
(2013--2026)}\label{related-work-and-scientific-lineage-20132026}

Automated catheter and needle digitization has evolved through three
distinct algorithmic generations over the past decade:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Analytical Tubular Filters and Model-Based Tracking
  (2013--2017):} Early approaches relied on geometric image processing
  and Sequential Monte Carlo tracking. In our initial work on the
  Brigham and Women's Hospital (BWH) Advanced Multimodality Image-Guided
  Operating (AMIGO) suite cohort, Pernelle \emph{et al.} (2013)
  introduced an elliptical template-matching likelihood coupled with a
  particle filter initialized from the Syed-Neblett perineal template,
  achieving \(1.05\text{ mm}\) mean shaft error across 187 catheters in
  15 patients. Mastmeyer, Pernelle \emph{et al.} (2017) extended this
  physical modeling with multi-step directional compass filters and
  dynamic programming across 19 patients (260 catheters). While
  geometrically interpretable, pure model-based trackers require manual
  ROI cropping or template initialization and degrade when catheters
  deflect sharply or cross dark anatomical boundaries.
\item
  \textbf{Voxel-Wise 3D Convolutional Segmentation + Heuristic
  Post-Processing (2019--2025):} To eliminate manual initialization,
  Zaffino, Pernelle \emph{et al.} (2019) (\emph{Physics in Medicine \&
  Biology}, 2019) pioneered 3D U-Net voxel segmentation on 50 clinical
  3T gynecologic MRI scans (41 training/validation cases with 760
  catheters and 9 test cases with 153 catheters), followed by 2D
  Hough-transform axis alignment and DBSCAN density clustering,
  achieving \(88.2\%\) recall, \(69.8\%\) precision, and
  \(2.00 \pm 1.50\text{ mm}\) mean shaft error in \(\sim 90\text{ s}\)
  per patient. Related 3D CNN and nnU-Net (2021) pipelines were
  subsequently investigated for transperineal prostate MRI biopsies
  (Mehrtash et al. 2019), CT brachytherapy (Jung et al. 2019; Dai et al.
  2020; Weishaupt et al. 2022; Aleong et al. 2024; Cantin et al. 2024;
  Quetin et al. 2025), and gynecologic MRI (Shaaer et al. 2022; Xie et
  al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024; Kostoulas et al. 2025).
  Despite architectural advances such as centerline-Dice
  (\texttt{clDice}) topology-preserving losses (Shit et al. 2021), pure
  voxel segmentation followed by morphological skeletonization suffers
  from two fundamental failure modes on clinical T2W MRI: (i)
  \textbf{trajectory fragmentation}, where a single-slice MRI signal
  dropout splits one physical catheter into 2--4 disconnected segments,
  and (ii) \textbf{anatomical false-positive hallucinations}, where
  blood vessels, rectovaginal septa, and obturator internus tendons are
  segmented as short tubular fragments.
\item
  \textbf{Structure-Aware Synchronous Tracking and Graph Neural Networks
  (2025--2026):} Most recently, Xiao \emph{et al.} (2026; 2026)
  (\texttt{arXiv:2607.11290}, July 2026) highlighted two critical
  challenges that degrade standard deep learning pipelines in
  multi-catheter brachytherapy: \textbf{catheter adhesion} (where
  \(\ge 2\) adjacent shafts approach within \(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\) or
  cross on axial slices, causing greedy nearest-neighbor trackers to
  swap identities or merge shafts) and \textbf{clinical annotation
  scarcity}. To address these on multi-catheter datasets, Xiao \emph{et
  al.} (2026) proposed an \textbf{Implant-Region Gating Module (IRGM)}
  to suppress out-of-implant background features, a
  \textbf{direction-constrained 3D Hough transform + intensity-weighted
  local PCA} for shaft direction estimation, and \textbf{synchronous
  inward cost-matrix tracking} via the Hungarian algorithm followed by
  \textbf{3rd-order polynomial trajectory fitting}. However, when
  applied directly to 3T T2W MRI segmentation masks, purely mask-driven
  inward tracking remains vulnerable to persistent false-positive
  vascular blobs inside the implant bounding box and lacks a learned
  relational mechanism to evaluate whether an entire set of candidate 3D
  trajectories forms a physically coherent interstitial implant bundle.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions of This
Work}\label{contributions-of-this-work}

In this paper, we bridge the gap between analytical physics priors,
dense 3D convolutional feature learning, synchronous adhesion-aware
tracking, and relational 3D graph reasoning on the expanded 54-patient
(\textbf{1,063-catheter}) BWH clinical 3T T2W MRI cohort:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Two-Stage Physics + Multi-Head 3D U-Net + Tip-Aware Relational
  Graph Architecture (\texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026}):} We combine a
  zero-parameter Stage 0 Rust/C++ 3D multi-radii circular dark-core
  symmetry filter (\(S_0\)) with a 5-head anisotropic 3D residual U-Net
  (\texttt{NeedleUNet3D}) enhanced by an Implant-Region Gating Module
  (IRGM), a 4-layer Triplet Message-Passing Neural Network
  (\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN}) with synchronous Hungarian cost-matrix
  tracking, and a global \texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer} with
  3rd-order polynomial regularization (\texttt{Poly3}) and physics-based
  synthetic bundle pre-training (\textbf{SynthBrachy3T}).
\item
  \textbf{Rigorous Head-to-Head 5-Fold Cross-Validation Against Modern
  Baselines:} To eliminate historical evaluation discrepancies across
  cohorts and matching rules, we re-implement and evaluate three
  representative external paradigms on identical 5-fold patient-level
  splits of all 54 patients (1,063 catheters) and on the \textbf{Severe
  Catheter-Adhesion Subgroup} (23 patients, 233 catheters approaching
  within \(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\)): (i) \texttt{PMB\ 2019} Isotropic
  \(1\text{ mm}^3\) 3D U-Net + DBSCAN/Skeleton (Zaffino et al. 2019),
  (ii) Native-Resolution 3D ResUNet + \texttt{clDice} + Morphological
  Skeletonization (Isensee et al. 2021; Shit et al. 2021), and (iii)
  \texttt{Xiao\ 2026} IRGM + Direction-Constrained 3D Hough +
  Synchronous Inward Cost-Matrix Tracking + \texttt{Poly3} (Xiao, Liu,
  et al. 2026), alongside an oracle ground-truth mask evaluation.
\item
  \textbf{Human Inter-Observer Equivalence, Few-Shot Sim-to-Real
  Efficiency, and Zero-Needle Specificity:} Across 40 dual-contoured
  clinical scenes (481 paired trajectories digitized independently by
  expert teams \texttt{CY} and \texttt{LB}/\texttt{AMe}), we demonstrate
  that \texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026} achieves lower shaft discrepancy
  (\(0.55\text{ mm}\) mean, \(0.45\text{ mm}\) median) than human
  inter-observer variability (\(0.71\text{ mm}\) mean,
  \(0.51\text{ mm}\) median). Furthermore, we evaluate clinical
  annotation budgets of \(k \in \{0, 1, 5, 10, 20, 43\}\) training
  patients per fold---showing that \textbf{SynthBrachy3T} pre-training
  unlocks \textbf{75.1\% zero-shot F1 (\(k=0\))} and \textbf{82.0\%
  one-shot F1 (\(k=1\) single training patient)}, surpassing full
  43-patient \texttt{PMB\ 2019} (\texttt{81.3\%}) with one clinical
  scan---and benchmark zero-needle out-of-distribution specificity on 48
  public 3T pelvic T2W MRI volumes (\texttt{MSD\ Task05\_Prostate}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Materials and Methods}\label{materials-and-methods}

\subsection{Clinical Patient Cohorts and Public Specificity
Benchmark}\label{clinical-patient-cohorts-and-public-specificity-benchmark}

\input{tables/tab1_cohorts.tex}

Table \ref{tbl:cohorts} summarizes the clinical and public imaging
cohorts evaluated in this study. Our primary clinical cohort comprises
54 intraoperative 3T T2-weighted turbo spin-echo (T2W TSE) MRI datasets
acquired during MR-guided interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy in the
AMIGO suite at Brigham and Women's Hospital (Harvard Medical School,
Boston, MA, USA) under Institutional Review Board approval (Pernelle et
al. 2013; Mastmeyer et al. 2017; Zaffino et al. 2019). This 54-patient
cohort represents the complete consolidated superset of the 15-patient
MICCAI 2013 dataset (Pernelle et al. 2013) and the 41-patient PMB 2019
cohort (Zaffino et al. 2019), expanded with 13 additional clinical
implants. Across all 54 patients, expert medical physicists digitized
\textbf{1,063 reference 3D trajectories} (\texttt{6\ to\ 34} per
patient, mean \(19.7 \pm 6.4\)), comprising \textbf{759 full-depth
active interstitial catheters} (tracked axial span \(\ge 30\text{ mm}\))
and \textbf{304 short or partial-FOV reference trajectories and vaginal
obturator tracks} (\(< 30\text{ mm}\)). In-plane pixel spacing ranges
from \(0.27 \times 0.27\text{ mm}^2\) to
\(0.75 \times 0.75\text{ mm}^2\) with axial slice thickness
\(\Delta z \in [1.6, 3.0]\text{ mm}\) (anisotropy ratio \(4:1\) to
\(9:1\)).

Following Xiao \emph{et al.} (2026), we partition the 54 clinical cases
into a \textbf{Whole Cohort} (54 patients, 1,063 catheters) and a
\textbf{Severe Catheter-Adhesion Subgroup} comprising the \textbf{23/54
patients (42.6\%)} containing \textbf{233 total catheters (194 long
shafts)} where at least two ground-truth catheter centerlines approach
within a Euclidean distance \(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\) across \(\ge 3\)
consecutive axial slices. In addition, \textbf{40 clinical scenes} were
independently contoured by two expert observer groups (\texttt{CY}: 505
active MRML trajectories; \texttt{LB}/\texttt{AMe}: 513 trajectories),
yielding 481 one-to-one Hungarian matched human trajectory pairs within
\(3.5\text{ mm}\) to establish the empirical human inter-observer floor.
Finally, to evaluate out-of-distribution false-positive specificity on
complex pelvic anatomy containing zero catheters, we evaluate all 48
clinical 3T axial T2W TSE pelvic MRI volumes from the public
\textbf{Medical Segmentation Decathlon (MSD) \texttt{Task05\_Prostate}}
benchmark (\(0.60\text{--}0.75\text{ mm}\) in-plane,
\(3.0\text{--}4.0\text{ mm}\) slice thickness).

\subsection{Stage 0: Analytical 3D Multi-Radii Circular Dark-Core
Symmetry
Prior}\label{stage-0-analytical-3d-multi-radii-circular-dark-core-symmetry-prior}

A fundamental physical property distinguishing a polymeric brachytherapy
catheter from a dark tissue boundary or vessel wall on axial T2W MRI is
\textbf{radial contrast symmetry}: whereas a fascial plane or muscle
border exhibits a one-sided intensity step edge, an axial catheter
cross-section forms a localized dark core surrounded across all
\(360^\circ\) of azimuth by brighter tissue. In Stage 0, we compute a
zero-parameter analytical 3D circular dark-core symmetry prior
\(S_0(x, y, z)\) implemented in multi-threaded native Rust/C++
(\texttt{rayon} + \texttt{PyO3}).

For each voxel \((x, y, z)\) on axial slice \(z\) with in-plane spacing
\((s_x, s_y)\), we sample \(M = 8\) azimuthal directions
\(\theta_m = m\pi/4\) (\(m \in \{0, \dots, 7\}\)) at multiple physical
radii \(r \in \mathcal{R} = \{1.0, 1.5, 2.2, 3.0\}\text{ mm}\). Let
\(I_0 = I(x, y, z)\) denote the central intensity and
\(I_{m,r} = I(x + r\cos\theta_m / s_x, y + r\sin\theta_m / s_y, z)\)
denote the bilinearly interpolated ring intensity. To enforce strict
azimuthal completeness while suppressing one-sided edges, we pair
opposite directions \((m, m+4)\) across 4 diameters and define the
multi-radii dark-core symmetry response:
\[S_{\text{2D}}(x,y,z) = \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \left[ \min_{d \in \{0,1,2,3\}} \max\!\left(0,\, \min(I_{d,r}, I_{d+4,r}) - I_0\right) \cdot \exp\!\left(-\frac{\sigma_{\theta,r}}{2\mu_{\theta,r} + \epsilon}\right) \right]\]
where \(\mu_{\theta,r}\) and \(\sigma_{\theta,r}\) are the mean and
standard deviation of \(\{I_{m,r} - I_0\}_{m=0}^7\). Taking the minimum
across opposite diameters guarantees that \(S_{\text{2D}} = 0\) along
planar tissue interfaces where one side of the ring remains dark. We
then aggregate \(S_{\text{2D}}\) across adjacent slices
\(\{z-1, z, z+1\}\) within a \(35^\circ\) physical deflection cone to
obtain the 3D tubular symmetry prior \(S_0(x,y,z) \in [0, 1]\),
executing in \(60\text{ ms}\) per volume on an 8-core CPU.

\subsection{Stage 1: Multi-Head Anisotropic 3D ResUNet with
Implant-Region Gating
(IRGM)}\label{stage-1-multi-head-anisotropic-3d-resunet-with-implant-region-gating-irgm}

Standard isotropic resampling (\(1 \times 1 \times 1\text{ mm}^3\))
blurs \(0.27\text{ mm}\) in-plane catheter voids by a factor of
\(3.7\times\), destroying sub-millimeter shaft boundaries. Conversely,
standard \(3 \times 3 \times 3\) convolutions on native anisotropic
grids (\(0.27 \times 0.27 \times 2.5\text{ mm}^3\)) mix features across
10x larger physical distances along \(z\) than in \((x,y)\). As
illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:arch}(a), Stage 1 (\texttt{NeedleUNet3D})
operates on the native high-resolution grid using a 2-channel input
\([I_{\text{norm}}(x,y,z),\, S_0(x,y,z)]\) and employs
\textbf{anisotropic \(1 \times 3 \times 3\) in-plane residual blocks} at
full resolution before transitioning to 3D \(3 \times 3 \times 3\)
residual blocks with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel attention at
deeper scales (\texttt{32\ -\textgreater{}\ 64\ -\textgreater{}\ 128}
channels).

To prevent false-positive activations in peripheral pelvic musculature
and bowel loops outside the interstitial implant envelope, we integrate
a physics-conditioned \textbf{Implant-Region Gating Module (IRGM)}
inspired by Xiao \emph{et al.} (2026). Whereas Xiao \emph{et al.} train
an auxiliary binary region branch from scratch, we condition our IRGM
directly on the analytical Stage 0 dark-core hull
\(\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(S_0 > \eta)\) alongside multi-scale decoder
features \(F\):
\[g = \sigma\!\left(W_g * [F \,;\, S_0]\right), \qquad \tilde{F} = F \odot (1 + g) \cdot \mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(S_0 > \eta)\]
where \(\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}\) is a smooth 3D Gaussian spatial envelope
(\(\sigma_{xy} = 3.5,\, \sigma_z = 0.7\) voxels) around the implant
core. From the gated full-resolution feature map \(\tilde{F}\),
\texttt{NeedleUNet3D} predicts \textbf{five complementary output heads}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tubular Mask Probability (\(P_{\text{mask}} \in [0,1]\)):}
  Supervised by a weighted binary cross-entropy (BCE) + soft Dice loss
  (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{mask}}\)) on a \(1.5\text{ mm}\) radius cylinder
  around each GT trajectory.
\item
  \textbf{Sub-Voxel 2D Gaussian Centerline Peak
  (\(P_{\text{peak}} \in [0,1]\)):} Supervised by a focal MSE loss
  (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{peak}}\)) against a sharp 2D Gaussian heatmap
  (\(\sigma = 0.85\text{ mm}\)) centered at the exact sub-pixel slice
  intersection \((x_c(z), y_c(z))\) of each catheter, enabling natural
  separation of touching shafts whose binary masks merge.
\item
  \textbf{3D Directional Tangent Vector Field
  (\(V_x = \Delta x / \Delta z,\, V_y = \Delta y / \Delta z\)):}
  Supervised by a smooth \(L_1\) loss (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{vec}}\))
  within foreground tubular masks, providing local 3D trajectory slope
  estimates at every voxel.
\item
  \textbf{Dedicated Vaginal Obturator Channel
  (\(P_{\text{obt}} \in [0,1]\)):} Explicitly segments the large central
  vaginal obturator / tandem assembly (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{obt}}\)) to
  prevent its central lumen or outer perimeter from generating spurious
  interstitial needle tracks.
\end{enumerate}

As a controlled Stage 1 architectural ablation, we also evaluate
\texttt{AxialTransUNet3D}, which augments the 3D ResUNet bottleneck with
a 2-layer Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) Transformer across the axial
depth dimension \(Z\).

\subsection{Stage 2: Synchronous Triplet MPNN, Bundle Graph Transformer,
and Polynomial
Regularization}\label{stage-2-synchronous-triplet-mpnn-bundle-graph-transformer-and-polynomial-regularization}

To link 2D slice detections into smooth, unfragmented 3D catheter
trajectories while resolving catheter adhesion and rejecting anatomical
false positives, Stage 2 operates on two hierarchical geometric graphs
(Figure \ref{fig:arch}(b)):

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Stage 2A: 3D Slice Graph and Triplet
Message-Passing Neural Network
(\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN})}{Stage 2A: 3D Slice Graph and Triplet Message-Passing Neural Network (NeedleSliceMPNN)}}\label{stage-2a-3d-slice-graph-and-triplet-message-passing-neural-network-needleslicempnn}

On each axial slice \(z\), local maxima of \(P_{\text{peak}}\) and Stage
0 symmetry peaks form candidate 2D nodes \(v_i^z \in \mathcal{V}\)
characterized by a 16-dimensional feature vector
\(\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{16}\) encoding normalized 3D coordinates,
\(P_{\text{mask}}\), \(P_{\text{peak}}\), \(S_0\), local \(3 \times 3\)
intensity statistics, predicted slopes \((V_x, V_y)\), and obturator
proximity \(P_{\text{obt}}\). Directed inter-slice edges
\(e_{ij} = (v_i^z, v_j^{z+\Delta k})\) connect nodes across step sizes
\(\Delta k \in \{1, 2, 3\}\) within a physical deflection cone
(\(\|\Delta\mathbf{xy}\| \le 5.5\text{ mm}\)), with 12-dimensional edge
features \(\mathbf{e}_{ij} \in \mathbb{R}^{12}\) capturing
\(\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z\), chord slope, and alignment with the
Stage 1 tangent field \((V_x, V_y)\).

Because standard pairwise GNNs cannot penalize second-order trajectory
kinks (bending energy \(\kappa_{ijk}\)), we design a \textbf{4-layer
Triplet Message-Passing Neural Network (\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN}, hidden
dimension 64)} that computes collinearity-aware messages across
consecutive edge triplets \((e_{ij}, e_{jk})\) spanning
\((v_i^{z-1} \to v_j^z \to v_k^{z+1})\):
\[\mathbf{m}_{ijk}^{(l)} = \phi_{\text{tri}}^{(l)}\!\left(\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)},\, \mathbf{h}_j^{(l)},\, \mathbf{h}_k^{(l)},\, \mathbf{e}_{ij}^{(l)},\, \mathbf{e}_{jk}^{(l)},\, \cos\theta_{ijk},\, \|\mathbf{s}_{ij} - \mathbf{s}_{jk}\|_2\right)\]
where \(\cos\theta_{ijk}\) is the 3D cosine collinearity between
incoming chord \(e_{ij}\) and outgoing chord \(e_{jk}\), and
\(\|\mathbf{s}_{ij} - \mathbf{s}_{jk}\|_2\) is the slope discrepancy.
Node embeddings predict refined sub-pixel offsets
\((\delta x_i, \delta y_i)\) and node/edge probabilities
\((p_i^{\text{node}}, p_{ij}^{\text{edge}})\).

To resolve \textbf{catheter adhesion} where two shafts approach within
\(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\) or cross on shared slices, we replace greedy
single-track linking with \textbf{synchronous Hungarian cost-matrix
tracking} across slices (Xiao, Liu, et al. 2026). At each slice
transition \(z \to z+1\), active tracks \(\mathcal{T}_z\) and candidate
slice detections \(\mathcal{D}_{z+1}\) are matched simultaneously by
solving the linear assignment problem
\(\min_{\Pi} \sum_{a,b} C_{a,b} \Pi_{a,b}\) with composite cost:
\[C_{a,b} = w_{\text{dist}} \|\hat{\mathbf{p}}_a(z+1) - \mathbf{p}_b\|_2 + w_{\text{dir}}(1 - \cos\theta_{a,b}) - w_{\text{mpnn}} \log p_{a,b}^{\text{edge}}\]
where \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}_a(z+1)\) is the linear-extrapolated position of
track \(a\) using an intensity-weighted 6-slice local PCA direction
vector.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Stage 2B: Global Bundle Graph Transformer
(\texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer}), Physics-Based Synthetic
Pre-Training (\texttt{SynthBrachy3T}), and Polynomial Regularization
(\texttt{Poly3})}{Stage 2B: Global Bundle Graph Transformer (TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer), Physics-Based Synthetic Pre-Training (SynthBrachy3T), and Polynomial Regularization (Poly3)}}\label{stage-2b-global-bundle-graph-transformer-tipawarebundlegraphtransformer-physics-based-synthetic-pre-training-synthbrachy3t-and-polynomial-regularization-poly3}

While Stage 1 and Stage 2A achieve \(>95\%\) recall, candidate pools
inevitably contain 10--25 short false-positive tracks per patient along
pelvic blood vessels or obturator boundaries. Crucially, real
interstitial catheters are inserted through a common perineal template
or vaginal corridor and therefore share \textbf{global bundle-level
geometric coherence}: their insertion angles are quasi-parallel to the
central obturator axis, their lateral spacing respects template channel
geometry (\(\ge 2.0\text{ mm}\)), and their distal tips form a coherent
3D surface covering the target volume.

We elevate each candidate 3D trajectory \(T_m\)
(\(m \in \{1, \dots, N_c\}\)) to an enriched \textbf{track token}
\(\mathbf{u}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{31}\) encoding its tracked length
\(L_m\), slice count, linear/polynomial fit RMSE, mean/max \(S_0\),
\(P_{\text{mask}}\), \(P_{\text{peak}}\), \(P_{\text{obt}}\), MPNN
confidence, multi-model consensus agreement, and local cranial/caudal
tip boundary contrast gradients, together with pairwise inter-track
spatial relationship features \(\mathbf{r}_{mn} \in \mathbb{R}^6\)
(minimum 3D shaft clearance, mean XY separation, angular divergence
\(\arccos(\mathbf{d}_m \cdot \mathbf{d}_n)\), \(z\)-overlap ratio, and
duplicate-overlap indicator). The
\texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer} applies \textbf{2 layers of
Edge-Biased Graph Attention (GAT)} over spatial neighbor tracks followed
by \textbf{2 layers of global Set Transformer self-attention} across the
entire implant bundle to predict calibrated per-track probabilities
\(p_m \in [0, 1]\) alongside cranial and caudal tip boundary
trim/extension offsets
\((\Delta z_m^{\text{cran}}, \Delta z_m^{\text{caud}})\). Tracks
satisfying \(p_m \ge \tau\) (pre-specified default \(\tau = 50\%\)) are
retained, refined at their distal endpoints using the predicted tip
boundary offsets and local axial contrast drop-off, and smoothed via
\textbf{3rd-order polynomial trajectory regularization
(\texttt{Poly3})}, fitting \(x(z) = \sum_{d=0}^3 \alpha_d z^d\) and
\(y(z) = \sum_{d=0}^3 \beta_d z^d\) to suppress high-frequency voxel
staircasing across thick MRI slices.

Finally, to overcome clinical annotation scarcity and maximize
Sim-to-Real generalization across diverse template geometries and severe
shaft adhesions, we introduce \textbf{SynthBrachy3T}, a physics-based 3D
synthetic brachytherapy bundle and MRI simulator. \texttt{SynthBrachy3T}
generates 1,500 realistic 3D interstitial catheter bundles (12--32
catheters per volume, central vaginal obturator channel, cubic
polynomial shaft deflections, and explicit \(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\)
adhering/crossing shaft pairs) together with simulated 3T T2W MRI
susceptibility voids, partial-volume slice averaging, Rician noise, and
false-positive pelvic vascular/fascial tubular distractors. Pre-training
the Stage 2 relational graph and tip-refinement heads on
\texttt{SynthBrachy3T} before fine-tuning on the \(k\) available
clinical training patients per fold provides rich supervision over rare
adhesion topologies and distal tip terminations without requiring any
external patient data.

\section{Experimental Setup and Evaluation
Protocol}\label{experimental-setup-and-evaluation-protocol}

\subsection{Unified Apples-to-Apples 5-Fold Cross-Validation
Protocol}\label{unified-apples-to-apples-5-fold-cross-validation-protocol}

All deep learning models (\texttt{NeedleUNet3D},
\texttt{AxialTransUNet3D}, \texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN},
\texttt{BundleGraphTransformer}, and all external baselines) are trained
and evaluated using \textbf{strict 5-fold patient-level out-of-fold
(OOF) cross-validation} across the 54 clinical BWH patients
(\texttt{1,063} total catheters; \texttt{759} long shafts). No slices,
patches, or trajectories from any test patient ever appear in training
or hyperparameter selection for that fold.

To ensure fair comparison with the 2019--2026 literature, we implement
and train three representative external baselines on the exact same
5-fold splits:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{\texttt{Baseline\ 1:\ PMB\ 2019\ 3D\ U-Net\ +\ DBSCAN/Skeleton}}
  (Zaffino et al. 2019): Volumes are resampled to
  \(1.0 \times 1.0 \times 1.0\text{ mm}^3\) isotropic resolution,
  segmented via a 3D U-Net trained on \(64^3\) patches, and
  post-processed via 2D Hough principal-axis estimation, DBSCAN
  clustering, and B-spline fitting.
\item
  \textbf{\texttt{Baseline\ 2:\ Native\ 3D\ ResUNet\ +\ clDice\ +\ Skeleton}}
  (Isensee et al. 2021; Shit et al. 2021): A native-resolution 3D
  residual U-Net trained with combined BCE + soft-Dice + soft-skeleton
  centerline-Dice (\texttt{clDice}, \(\alpha = 0.4\), 5-iteration
  differentiable morphological soft-skeletonization), followed by 3D
  connected-component extraction and polynomial centerline fitting.
\item
  \textbf{\texttt{Baseline\ 3:\ Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track}} (Xiao, Liu, et
  al. 2026): Re-implements the July 2026 structure-aware catheter
  digitization framework of Xiao \emph{et al.}
  (\texttt{arXiv:2607.11290}), combining Implant-Region Gating (IRGM),
  direction-constrained 3D Hough transform + intensity-weighted PCA
  direction initialization, synchronous inward Hungarian cost-matrix
  tracking, and 3rd-order polynomial regularization (\texttt{Poly3}).
  Following Xiao \emph{et al.} (2026) Table 2, we evaluate this tracker
  both \textbf{end-to-end on 5-fold OOF segmentation masks} (Part A) and
  \textbf{on oracle ground-truth binary masks} (Part B).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quantitative Metrics and Statistical
Testing}\label{quantitative-metrics-and-statistical-testing}

For each patient volume, predicted 3D trajectories
\(\{T_m\}_{m=1}^{N_p}\) and ground-truth trajectories
\(\{G_n\}_{n=1}^{N_g}\) are matched via \textbf{strict one-to-one
Hungarian bipartite assignment}
(\texttt{scipy.optimize.linear\_sum\_assignment}) with a maximum
in-plane shaft distance cutoff of \(3.5\text{ mm}\) across overlapping
axial slices. We report:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Detection Accuracy (\%):} Strict one-to-one Hungarian Recall
  (\(\text{TP} / N_g\)), Precision (\(\text{TP} / N_p\)), and
  \(\text{F1} = 2\text{TP}/(N_g + N_p)\).
\item
  \textbf{In-Plane Shaft Localization Error (\(E_{\text{shaft}}\), mm):}
  Mean, standard deviation (SD), median, interquartile range (IQR), and
  95th percentile (P95) of the 2D Euclidean distance
  \(\|\hat{\mathbf{p}}(z) - \mathbf{g}(z)\|_{xy}\) across all shared
  axial slices \(z\).
\item
  \textbf{Symmetric 3D Curve Distance (\(D_{\text{avg}}\), mm) and
  95th-Percentile Hausdorff Distance (\(\text{HD}_{95}\), mm):}
  Bidirectional 3D point-to-curve distance densely resampled at 50
  points along each trajectory.
\item
  \textbf{3D Distal-Tip Error (\(E_{\text{tip}}\), mm):} 3D Euclidean
  distance
  \(\|\hat{\mathbf{p}}(z_{\text{tip}}) - \mathbf{g}(z_{\text{tip}})\|_2\)
  at the cranial/distal catheter tip.
\end{enumerate}

Statistical significance between methods is assessed across all
\(N = 54\) patients using \textbf{paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank
tests} alongside 1,000-iteration patient-level cluster bootstrap 95\%
confidence intervals.

Several key findings emerge from Table \ref{tbl:main} and Figure
\ref{fig:raincloud}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Resolution of the Mask-Only Precision Bottleneck:}
  \texttt{Baseline\ 2}
  (\texttt{Native\ 3D\ ResUNet\ +\ clDice\ +\ Skeleton}) achieves high
  one-to-one recall (\(90.4\%\) whole cohort; \(94.9\%\) adhesion
  subgroup) and sub-millimeter shaft accuracy on matched fragments
  (\(0.43\text{ mm}\) median), but suffers from catastrophic
  \textbf{false-positive over-fragmentation (\(23.9\%\) precision,
  \(37.9\%\) F1, generating 4,015 disconnected segments across 54
  volumes)}. Because spin-echo T2W MRI exhibits localized signal
  dropouts along plastic shafts and tubular susceptibility voids along
  pelvic veins, morphological skeletonization breaks single catheters
  into multiple short tracks while hallucinating false tracks in
  vascular plexuses.
\item
  \textbf{Comparison with Structure-Aware Inward Tracking
  (\texttt{Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track}):} When evaluated on \textbf{oracle
  ground-truth binary masks} (Table \ref{tbl:main} Part B bottom row,
  matching the experimental check of Xiao \emph{et al.} (2026) Table 2
  Part B), \texttt{Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track} achieves \textbf{\(97.4\%\)

  direction-constrained 3D Hough initialization and synchronous
  Hungarian cost-matrix tracking accurately separate touching shafts
  when the underlying mask is noise-free. When applied
  \textbf{end-to-end on 5-fold OOF clinical 3T MRI masks} (Table
  \ref{tbl:main} Part A), \texttt{Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track} boosts F1 by
  \(+15.8\text{ pp}\) over morphological skeletonization
  (\(37.9\% \to 53.7\%\) whole cohort; \(47.8\% \to 63.1\%\) adhesion
  subgroup) by bridging single-slice mask gaps and suppressing
  out-of-hull noise via IRGM. Nevertheless, without Stage 0 circular
  dark-core symmetry verification and Stage 2B global bundle-level graph
  pruning, unidirectional inward tracking still spawns false-positive
  trajectories from intra-implant vascular and muscular structures,
  limiting precision to \(38.9\%\) (\(53.7\%\) F1).
\item
  \textbf{Superiority of
  \texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026\ +\ SynthBrachy3T\ +\ Tip-Aware}:} At the

  1,063 catheters (and \textbf{\(88.4\%\) F1} at \(\tau = 55\%\),
  \textbf{\(94.7\%\) one-to-one recall} at \(\tau = 25\%\)),
  statistically significantly outperforming our non-synthetic
  \texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026\ +\ IRGM\ +\ Poly3} baseline by
  \textbf{\(+2.2\text{ pp}\) F1 (\(p = 6.42 \times 10^{-5}\))},
  \texttt{PMB\ 2019\ 3D\ U-Net} by \textbf{\(+6.9\text{ pp}\) F1
  (\(p = 1.15 \times 10^{-5}\))}, \texttt{Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track} by
  \textbf{\(+34.5\text{ pp}\) F1 (\(p = 2.28 \times 10^{-10}\))}, and
  \texttt{ResUNet\ +\ clDice} by \textbf{\(+50.3\text{ pp}\) F1
  (\(p = 2.85 \times 10^{-10}\))}. Furthermore,
  \texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026\ +\ SynthBrachy3T\ +\ Tip-Aware} reduces
  mean 3D distal-tip error across all 1,063 catheters from
  \(10.72\text{ mm}\) (\texttt{PMB\ 2019}, median \(3.35\text{ mm}\)),

\subsection{Positioning Within the 2013--2026 Brachytherapy Digitization
Literature}\label{positioning-within-the-20132026-brachytherapy-digitization-literature}

Table \ref{tbl:literature} contextualizes \texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026}
within the broader landscape of automated brachytherapy catheter
reconstruction across CT and MRI (2013--2026). A critical distinction
highlighted by Table \ref{tbl:literature} is the physical
signal-to-noise gap between planning CT (grey-shaded rows) and spin-echo
pelvic T2W MRI (unshaded rows). In CT-guided brachytherapy (Dai et al.
2020; Weishaupt et al. 2022; Aleong et al. 2024; Quetin et al. 2025;
Xiao, Zhou, et al. 2026; Xiao, Liu, et al. 2026), metallic stylets or
air-filled catheter lumens exhibit extreme Hounsfield contrast
(\(\pm 500\text{ to }+3000\text{ HU}\)) at thin slice spacing
(\(0.6\text{--}1.25\text{ mm}\)), enabling 3D U-Nets to achieve voxel
Dice scores \(>0.85\) and shaft errors of
\(0.22\text{--}0.67\text{ mm}\). By contrast, on clinical 3T T2W MRI
without exogenous contrast markers (Pernelle et al. 2013; Zaffino et al.
2019; Shaaer et al. 2022; Xie et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024; Kostoulas
et al. 2025), polymeric catheters appear only as \(2.0\text{ mm}\)
signal voids indistinguishable in raw intensity from blood vessels,
tendons, and signal dropouts across \(1.6\text{--}3.0\text{ mm}\) thick
slices.

Our controlled head-to-head evaluation on the 54-patient
(1,063-catheter) BWH cohort (Table \ref{tbl:main}) demonstrates why
neither pure 3D CNN segmentation (Zaffino et al. 2019; Isensee et al.
2021; Shit et al. 2021) nor purely mask-driven inward tracking (Xiao,
Liu, et al. 2026) suffices on clinical 3T T2W MRI:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Why Voxel \texttt{clDice} + Skeletonization Fails on T2W MRI
  (\texttt{37.9\%} F1):} While soft-skeleton \texttt{clDice} (Shit et
  al. 2021) preserves centerline connectivity on continuous vascular
  trees, brachytherapy catheters on T2W MRI frequently exhibit 1--2
  slice signal dropouts where surrounding edematous tumor tissue is
  similarly hypointense. Morphological skeletonization fragments each
  interrupted shaft into multiple pieces and converts every
  false-positive vessel blob into a candidate track (\(23.9\%\)
  precision).
\item
  \textbf{Why Hybrid Physics + Multi-Head Centerline Regression +
  \texttt{SynthBrachy3T} Tip-Aware Graph Attention Succeeds
  (\texttt{88.2\%}--\texttt{88.4\%} F1):} First, our analytical Stage 0
  3D multi-radii circular dark-core symmetry prior (\(S_0\)) explicitly
  tests for \(360^\circ\) azimuthal contrast completeness, rejecting
  planar tissue interfaces before neural inference begins. Second,
  conditioning the Stage 1 Implant-Region Gating Module (IRGM) on
  \(S_0\) and regressing sharp 2D Gaussian centerline peaks
  (\(P_{\text{peak}}\)) alongside directional slopes \((V_x, V_y)\)
  preserves distinct sub-pixel centers even when adjacent catheter masks
  merge. Third, combining Stage 2A collinearity-aware triplet message
  passing (\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN}) and synchronous Hungarian
  cost-matrix tracking with Stage 2B global
  \texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer} attention pre-trained on 1,500
  physics-based synthetic brachytherapy bundles (\texttt{SynthBrachy3T})
  allows each candidate track and its distal tip boundary to be judged
  in the context of the entire 15--30 catheter implant bundle, boosting
  whole-cohort F1 to \texttt{88.2\%} (\texttt{89.7\%} on severe
  adhesion) and 1-shot Sim-to-Real F1 to \texttt{82.0\%}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Clinical Workflow Impact and
Limitations}\label{clinical-workflow-impact-and-limitations}

Implemented as a one-click module inside \textbf{3D Slicer} (Pernelle et
al. 2013; Zaffino et al. 2019) with zero GPU dependency required for
sub-second inference (\(0.61\text{ s}\) on CPU),
\texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026} transforms intraoperative MR-only catheter
digitization from a 15--40 minute manual contouring task into a
\textbf{1--2 minute verify-and-approve workflow}: the physicist clicks
\emph{Detect Needles}, inspects the 3D bundle and axial intersections,
and adjusts any residual distal tip or partial-FOV track. Because the
in-plane shaft accuracy (\(0.45\text{ mm}\) median on all 1,063
catheters and on the 759 long shafts) lies well below both the
\(1.0\text{ mm}\) AAPM TG-317 tolerance (Beaulieu et al. 2025) and the
\(0.51\text{ mm}\) human inter-observer median floor (Table
\ref{tbl:interobserver}), automated shaft trajectories require virtually
no manual in-plane editing.

Two limitations merit discussion and motivate future work. First, while
\texttt{NeedleFinder\ 2026\ +\ SynthBrachy3T\ +\ Tip-Aware} reduces mean
3D distal-tip error by 49.5\% compared to
\texttt{Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track} (\(15.64 \to 7.90\text{ mm}\)) and
achieves a median 3D tip error of \textbf{\(2.61\text{ mm}\)
(\(2.98\text{ mm}\) on the severe adhesion subgroup)}---within 1 slice
thickness (\(\Delta z = 2.5\text{ mm}\)) and below the
\(3.19\text{ mm}\) human inter-observer tip median---outliers still
occur when a catheter tip terminates inside a dark signal-void pocket at
the cranial uterine fundus or when short partial-FOV reference tracks
exit the slab boundary. Incorporating thin-slice 3D isotropic T2W
SPACE/VISTA acquisitions (\(\Delta z \le 1.0\text{ mm}\)) or
multi-contrast T1W/T2W fusion is expected to further tighten cranial tip
localization toward \(<1.5\text{ mm}\). Second, although our 54-patient
BWH cohort (1,063 catheters) is the largest clinically annotated 3T MRI
interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy cohort reported to date and is
complemented by 48 external 3T pelvic MRI scans from
\texttt{MSD\ Task05\_Prostate}, prospective multi-institutional
validation across 1.5T and 3T scanners from multiple vendors will
further establish cross-site generalization.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

We have presented \textbf{NeedleFinder 2026}, a unified two-stage
physics-informed deep learning, synthetic data pre-training
(\textbf{SynthBrachy3T}), and relational 3D graph architecture for
automated catheter reconstruction in MR-guided interstitial gynecologic
brachytherapy. By uniting an analytical 3D circular dark-core symmetry
prior (\(S_0\)), a 5-head anisotropic 3D residual U-Net with
Implant-Region Gating (IRGM), a synchronous adhesion-aware Triplet
Message-Passing Neural Network (\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN}), and a global
\texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer} pre-trained on 1,500 synthetic
3T bundles with 3rd-order polynomial regularization, our framework
achieves \textbf{91.3\% one-to-one recall, 85.3\% precision, and 88.2\%
F1} at \(\tau=50\%\) (\textbf{88.4\% F1} at \(\tau=55\%\),
\textbf{94.7\% recall} at \(\tau=25\%\), and \textbf{89.7\% F1} on
severe adhesion cases) across 1,063 clinical catheters (with
\textbf{96.4\% recall, 88.3\% obturator-adjusted F1, 0.45 mm median
shaft error, and 2.61 mm median tip error} on full-depth interstitial
shafts) in \textbf{\(0.61\text{ s}\) on CPU (\(0.35\text{ s}\) on GPU)},
outperforming state-of-the-art voxel segmentation and synchronous
tracking baselines while matching human expert agreement within the
inter-observer floor.
