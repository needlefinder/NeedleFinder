\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Objective.} In MRI-guided interstitial gynecologic
brachytherapy, a clinical team inserts 15 to 30 thin plastic catheters
into a pelvic tumor and acquires a 3T T2-weighted MRI scan while the
patient is under anesthesia. Before radiation can be planned, a
physicist must trace the 3D path of every catheter on the scan. Doing
this by hand takes 30 to 45 minutes. Existing automatic methods based on
voxel segmentation struggle on T2-weighted MRI because plastic catheters
appear only as tiny dark dots that look much like blood vessels, and
brief signal dropouts break single catheters into disconnected pieces.
Here we present NeedleFinder 2026, an algorithm that reconstructs the
entire catheter bundle automatically, to sub-millimeter shaft accuracy,
in a few seconds.

\textbf{Approach.} NeedleFinder 2026 combines imaging physics with three
stages of machine learning. First, an analytical 3D filter (Stage 0)
locates the entry plane of the perineal template automatically and
measures circular dark-core symmetry on each slice---highlighting round
catheter cross-sections while ignoring one-sided tissue borders. Second,
a native-resolution 3D residual U-Net (Stage 1) predicts the sub-pixel
center and local 3D tilt of each catheter on every slice. To prevent
this network from mistaking pelvic veins for catheters, we pre-train it
on 51 external needle-free pelvic MRI scans injected with synthetic
catheter voids and fine-tune it on consensus traces from two expert
teams. Third, a graph-based tracking pipeline (Stage 2) links detections
across slices, stitches fragments broken by signal dropouts, evaluates
the entire 15-to-30-catheter bundle together using a graph transformer,
and pinpoints each catheter's deepest tip using a 1D axial neural
network equipped with calibrated uncertainty intervals. We evaluated the
pipeline across 54 clinical 3T MRI scans (1,063 catheters) using
strictly nested \(5 \times 5\) patient-level cross-validation.

\textbf{Main results.} NeedleFinder 2026 detected 91.4\% of all 1,063
catheters with 86.9\% precision and an F1 score of 89.1\% (and
90.0\%--90.5\% F1 on the 759 full-depth treatment shafts), outperforming
three published segmentation and tracking methods by 6.0 to 50.4 F1
points (\(p \le 1.1 \times 10^{-5}\)). Along the catheter shaft, the
median in-plane error was 0.47 mm (mean 0.59 mm). At the deepest tip,
the median 3D error was 2.27 mm (mean 6.23 mm), with 98.8\% of the
residual tip variance lying longitudinally along the catheter track.
Across 40 scans traced independently by two human expert teams,
three-cornered-hat analysis and one-sided non-inferiority tests showed
that the algorithm is statistically non-inferior to human inter-observer
agreement on both shafts (\(p = 1.3 \times 10^{-8}\)) and tips
(\(p = 2.6 \times 10^{-7}\)). Post-implant CT scans from 38 paired
patients confirmed that 52.0\% of the algorithm's apparent ``false
alarms'' on MRI were real physical catheters missed by the primary human
team on MRI. In high-dose-rate \(^{192}\text{Ir}\) radiation treatment
simulations, reviewing just the 2 to 5 most uncertain tips flagged by
the algorithm's conformal intervals took under a minute of simulated
review and kept the median tumor dose error (\(|\Delta D_{90}|\)) to
2.8\% (mean 5.9\%). Without retraining, the gynecologic pipeline
generalized directly to 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI (96.3\%--100.0\%
F1), balanced steady-state MRI (92.9\% F1), live 4D needle-insertion
scans (82.8\% F1), and post-implant CT. End-to-end reconstruction takes
4.15 s per patient on a workstation CPU (1.26 s on standard-resolution
scans) and 2.67 s on a GPU.

\textbf{Significance.} Combining a simple physical symmetry prior with
multi-stage graph learning turns a 30-to-45-minute manual tracing
bottleneck into a 4-second automatic reconstruction followed by a brief,
uncertainty-guided check in 3D Slicer.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Plain-Language Summary}\label{plain-language-summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Plain-Language Summary}

Advanced cancers of the cervix, uterus, and vagina are often treated
with \emph{interstitial brachytherapy}. While the patient is asleep
under anesthesia, doctors guide 15 to 30 thin plastic tubes (catheters)
into the tumor. A tiny radioactive source will later travel through
these tubes for a few minutes to destroy the cancer from the inside
while sparing nearby healthy organs like the bladder and bowel.

Before any radiation can be delivered, the medical team takes an MRI
scan to see where the tubes landed inside the tumor. Because radiation
strength drops off very rapidly with distance, being off by even a
couple of millimeters can under-treat the tumor or over-irradiate a
healthy organ. Currently, a physicist must sit at a computer while the
patient remains under anesthesia and trace every single tube by hand
across dozens of MRI slices---a painstaking job that takes 30 to 45
minutes.

Automating this step on MRI has been difficult for a simple physical
reason: plastic tubes contain no mobile water, so they do not glow on an
MRI scan. Instead, each tube appears only as a tiny dark dot, two
millimeters wide, on each image slice. The human pelvis is full of other
dark structures---blood vessels, air pockets, and tissue borders---so
standard artificial intelligence programs easily confuse blood vessels
with tubes, or break a single tube into pieces when the dark dot fades
on one slice.

We built \textbf{NeedleFinder 2026} to solve this problem in the way a
careful human physicist does---by combining local image appearance with
the overall geometry of the implant:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Look for dark circles, not dark edges (Stage 0).} A simple
  mathematical filter checks whether each dark spot is surrounded by
  brighter tissue in \emph{all} directions (like a tube cross-section)
  rather than dark on one side only (like a muscle border). It also
  automatically finds where the needle-guiding template sits at the base
  of the scan.
\item
  \textbf{Pinpoint tube centers and directions in 3D (Stage 1).} A 3D
  neural network---first pre-trained on 51 external pelvic MRI scans to
  tell artificial tubes apart from real blood vessels, and then trained
  on traces from two independent teams of human experts---marks the
  exact center and tilt of each tube on every slice, even when two tubes
  touch.
\item
  \textbf{Connect the dots into a coherent bundle and find each tip
  (Stage 2).} Two graph networks connect the marks from slice to slice,
  bridge gaps where a tube briefly fades, and judge whether all 15 to 30
  curves make geometric sense together as a single implant. Finally, a
  specialized 1D network looks along the end of each tube to pinpoint
  its deepest tip and provides an uncertainty bar telling the physicist
  which tips are clear and which ones deserve a quick second look.
\end{enumerate}

Tested across 54 patients (1,063 tubes), NeedleFinder 2026 found 91.4\%
of all tubes automatically. Along the length of each tube, its path
agreed with human experts to within half a millimeter (0.47 mm
median)---closer than two teams of human experts agreed with each other
(0.49 mm median). At the deepest tip, its typical error was 2.27 mm,
compared with 3.24 mm between the two human teams. Even more strikingly,
when we checked post-implant CT scans (where tubes are easy to see),
more than half (52\%) of the ``extra'' tubes found by our software on
MRI turned out to be real tubes that the primary human team had missed
on MRI.

In practice, NeedleFinder 2026 runs inside the free \textbf{3D Slicer}
medical software in \textbf{4 seconds} on a standard workstation
computer (1.3 seconds on standard-resolution scans). Instead of tracing
25 tubes from scratch, the physicist clicks one button, checks the 2 to
5 tube tips that the software flags as uncertain in under a minute of
simulated review, and proceeds to radiation planning with tumor dose
accuracy within 2.8\% (median) of a fully manual plan.

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

\subsection{The Clinical Bottleneck in MRI-Guided
Brachytherapy}\label{the-clinical-bottleneck-in-mri-guided-brachytherapy}

For locally advanced cervical and vaginal cancer, high-dose-rate (HDR)
brachytherapy---delivering radiation directly inside the tumor---is
essential for cure {[}1,2{]}. The international EMBRACE-I study
demonstrated 92\% five-year local tumor control by delivering a median
dose of 90 Gy (EQD2) to the high-risk tumor volume {[}2{]}, and the
EMBRACE-II protocol prescribes 90 to 95 Gy to the target while strictly
limiting dose to the adjacent bladder, rectum, and sigmoid colon
{[}1{]}. When a tumor is large, asymmetric, or extends into surrounding
tissues, a central uterine tandem alone cannot cover it safely.
Clinicians therefore perform \emph{interstitial} brachytherapy:
alongside the central applicator, they insert 10 to 30 thin plastic
catheters through a perineal template or freehand directly into the
tumor {[}3--6{]}.

T2-weighted (T2W) MRI is the gold standard for visualizing gynecologic
tumors and surrounding soft-tissue organs, and international guidelines
(GEC-ESTRO and AAPM Task Group 303) recommend MRI-based treatment
planning {[}7--10{]}. Ideally, the entire workflow---imaging, tumor
contouring, catheter reconstruction, and radiation dose
optimization---takes place on the T2W planning MRI alone {[}11--13{]}.
In practice, however, many clinics still acquire an additional CT scan
and fuse it to the MRI simply because plastic catheters are so difficult
to see on MRI {[}14,15{]}, or rely on specialized positive-contrast
lumen markers {[}11,16,17{]} or zero-echo-time sequences {[}13,18{]}
that require extra scan time and image registration.

Why is reconstructing catheters on a standard T2W MRI so challenging,
and why does its accuracy matter so much?

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Radiation dose changes steeply over millimeters.} The
  \(^{192}\text{Ir}\) source steps through each catheter in 2.5 to 5.0
  mm increments, and its dose falls off roughly with the inverse square
  of distance. A 1 mm shift along the catheter path or a 1.5 mm shift
  toward an organ at risk can alter bladder or rectal dose metrics
  (\(D_{2\text{cc}}\)) by 5\% to 10\% {[}19--21{]}, and swapping or
  mis-tracing catheters can shift target and urethral doses by 5\% to
  20\% {[}22,23{]}. Even experienced clinicians make errors when
  digitizing catheters under time pressure {[}24,25{]}.
\item
  \textbf{Catheters look like tiny dark voids surrounded by dark
  anatomy.} A 2 mm (6F) plastic catheter contains no mobile water
  protons. On an axial T2W MRI slice, its plastic wall and lumen produce
  a small proton-density signal void, 2 to 5 pixels across, accompanied
  by a mild magnetic susceptibility (\(\Delta\chi\)) transition at the
  catheter--tissue interface {[}26{]}. Unfortunately, the female pelvis
  is full of other dark structures: flow-void blood vessels, air pockets
  in the rectum or vagina, calcifications, and the dark
  connective-tissue planes (fascia) between muscles.
\item
  \textbf{Manual tracing takes tens of minutes while the patient is
  anesthetized.} Because each scan contains 28 to 160 axial slices (1.2
  to 6.0 mm thick) and 15 to 30 catheters that bend, cross, or touch one
  another, tracing every catheter slice by slice takes an average of 46
  minutes in reported MRI-only gynecologic workflows {[}27{]}---nearly
  half of the 100-minute operating-room procedure {[}28{]}.
\end{enumerate}

Our goal is to replace this manual bottleneck with an automatic
reconstruction algorithm that operates directly on standard clinical 3T
T2W MRI, matches human expert precision along both the shaft and the
tip, flags its own uncertain tips for rapid human verification, and
finishes in a few seconds.

\subsection{A Quick Guide for Readers Outside Medical Physics or Machine
Learning}\label{a-quick-guide-for-readers-outside-medical-physics-or-machine-learning}

Because this paper bridges clinical radiation oncology, MRI physics, and
graph deep learning, we briefly define the core terms used throughout
the text:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Catheter (or needle), shaft, and tip.} We use \emph{catheter}
  and \emph{needle} interchangeably for the hollow 2 mm plastic tubes
  inserted into the tumor. The \textbf{shaft} is the main body of the
  tube passing through the tissue; the \textbf{tip} (or
  \emph{distal/cranial end}) is the deepest point the tube reaches
  inside the body, where the radioactive source stops first.
\item
  \textbf{Obturator and tandem.} Larger central plastic guides placed in
  the vagina (\emph{obturator}) and uterus (\emph{tandem}) around which
  the thinner interstitial catheters are arranged.
\item
  \textbf{Voxel, in-plane resolution, and slice thickness.} A 3D MRI
  scan is a stack of 2D horizontal cross-sections (\emph{axial slices}).
  Within each 2D slice (\emph{in-plane}), the image is sharp (pixel size
  0.31 to 0.88 mm). Between slices (along the vertical body axis,
  \(z\)), the spacing is much coarser (slice thickness 1.2 to 6.0 mm).
  This anisotropy explains why finding the sideways (\emph{in-plane})
  center of a catheter is easier than judging the exact height (\(z\))
  where its tip ends between two slices.
\item
  \textbf{Recall, precision, and F1 score.} \emph{Recall} is the
  percentage of real catheters the algorithm successfully finds.
  \emph{Precision} is the percentage of algorithm-reported tracks that
  correspond to a real catheter (rather than a false alarm on a blood
  vessel). The \emph{F1 score} is the harmonic mean of recall and
  precision; it is high only when the algorithm finds almost all real
  catheters while inventing very few false ones.
\item
  \textbf{Shaft error (\(E_{\text{shaft}}\)) and tip error
  (\(E_{\text{tip}}\)).} For every detected catheter that matches an
  expert trace (within 3.5 mm), \emph{shaft error} measures the sideways
  distance (in mm) between the automatic curve and the expert curve
  along their shared length. \emph{Tip error} measures the 3D distance
  (in mm) between the predicted deepest tip and the expert's deepest
  tip.
\item
  \textbf{Strictly nested cross-validation (out-of-fold evaluation).} To
  test our models on all 54 patients without ever testing on data used
  for training or tuning, we split the 54 patients into 5 outer groups
  (folds). In each round, 43 or 44 patients are used for training and
  tuning (via an internal 80/20 split or 5-fold inner cross-fitting),
  and the remaining 10 or 11 patients are held in a locked vault and
  evaluated \emph{once}. Every patient is thus evaluated strictly
  ``out-of-fold'' (as an unseen patient).
\item
  \textbf{Dosimetric metrics (\(D_{90}\), \(D_{2\text{cc}}\), and 3D
  \(\gamma\)).} In brachytherapy planning, \(D_{90}\) is the minimum
  radiation dose covering 90\% of the high-risk tumor volume (which
  clinicians want to keep high), while \(D_{2\text{cc}}\) is the dose to
  the most-irradiated \(2\text{ cm}^3\) of a nearby healthy organ like
  the bladder or rectum (which clinicians want to keep low). The 3D
  \emph{gamma (\(\gamma\)) pass rate} (\(2\%/2\text{ mm}\)) measures the
  percentage of points in the 3D volume where the dose planned on
  automatic catheters agrees with the reference plan within 2\% dose or
  2 mm distance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why Existing Approaches Fall Short on T2-Weighted
MRI}\label{why-existing-approaches-fall-short-on-t2-weighted-mri}

Over the past decade, automatic catheter reconstruction has evolved
through three generations (reviewed in {[}29,30{]}):

\textbf{Generation 1: Geometric and mechanical tracking from manual
clicks (2012--2019).} Early work in our Brigham and Women's Hospital
(BWH) program introduced image-guided catheter tracking in the Advanced
Multimodality Image Guided Operating (AMIGO) suite {[}3,31{]}. Starting
from a manual click at each catheter tip, cone-search trackers {[}32{]}
and mechanical rod-and-spring splines {[}4,33{]} achieved
\textasciitilde1.4--1.5 mm accuracy on T2W MRI. Similar geometry-guided
or template-registered methods work well on CT, ultrasound, or rigid
applicators {[}34--39{]}. However, they still require a human to click
every catheter tip first, and they often lose track when catheters bend
sharply or cross dark vessels.

\textbf{Generation 2: 3D voxel segmentation with clustering
(2019--2026).} To eliminate manual clicks, Zaffino \emph{et al.} {[}5{]}
trained a 3D U-Net on 50 BWH gynecologic MRI scans to classify every
voxel as ``catheter'' or ``background,'' then grouped the segmented
voxels into tubes using density clustering (DBSCAN). Subsequent studies
applied 3D U-Nets and nnU-Net {[}40{]} across CT, ultrasound, and
prostate or marker-assisted MRI {[}27,41--62{]}. Yet even when trained
with topology-preserving losses such as centerline-Dice (clDice)
{[}63--65{]}, pure voxel segmentation suffers from two fundamental
failure modes on T2W MRI: 1. \emph{Over-fragmentation:} If a catheter's
dark void fades on even a single slice due to partial-volume averaging,
the segmented mask breaks into 2 to 4 separate pieces. 2. \emph{Touching
catheters and vascular false alarms:} When two catheters touch
(\emph{catheter adhesion}), their voxel masks merge into a single blob;
meanwhile, dark pelvic veins and tissue edges are segmented as dozens of
short false catheters.

\textbf{Generation 3: Structure-aware tracking and graph networks
(2022--2026).} Recently, Xiao \emph{et al.} {[}66,67{]} addressed
catheter adhesion on pelvic CT by combining an implant-region gating
module with 3D Hough initialization and synchronous slice-to-slice
tracking. In computer vision, message-passing graph networks
{[}68--70{]}, keypoint regression {[}71{]}, and synthetic-to-real
transfer {[}72{]} have similarly improved curvilinear tracking. However,
when a mask-driven tracker is applied to T2W MRI, it still trusts the
underlying segmentation mask: any blood vessel inside the implant region
is tracked as a catheter, and the tracker has no mechanism to ask
whether the resulting collection of 25 trajectories makes sense
\emph{together} as a physical brachytherapy implant, nor to resolve
sub-slice tip terminations on thick MRI slices. (Hardware-based
electromagnetic or active MR tracking avoids image ambiguity
{[}28,73--79{]}, but requires specialized catheters and operating-room
hardware.)

\subsection{How NeedleFinder 2026 Solves These
Problems}\label{how-needlefinder-2026-solves-these-problems}

NeedleFinder 2026 bridges all three generations on the complete
54-patient, 1,063-catheter BWH gynecologic MRI cohort---the largest
annotated clinical MRI dataset for interstitial brachytherapy to date
(Figure \ref{fig:overview}). Each stage of our architecture is designed
to solve a specific failure mode of prior work:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Physics before learning (Stage 0):} Before any neural network
  runs, an analytical 3D circular dark-core symmetry filter separates
  round dark cross-sections (catheters and vessels) from one-sided dark
  boundaries (fascial planes and organ walls), while an automatic
  template-plane locator finds the base of the implant without needing
  manual bounding boxes.
\item
  \textbf{Centerline peaks and self-supervised pelvic pre-training
  (Stage 1):} Instead of relying on binary masks alone, our
  native-resolution 3D U-Net regresses sharp sub-pixel \emph{centerline
  peaks} and \emph{3D tangent directions}---so two touching catheters
  still produce two distinct peaks. To prevent the network from
  confusing pelvic veins with catheters, we pre-train it on 51 external
  pelvic MRI volumes (from 50 subjects) by injecting realistic synthetic
  catheter voids while explicitly penalizing real pelvic vessels and
  fascia as hard negatives, and then fine-tune it using consensus traces
  from two independent expert teams.
\item
  \textbf{Graph linking, fragment stitching, bundle-level reasoning, and
  1D tip localization (Stage 2):} A triplet message-passing graph
  network links slice detections while penalizing sharp kinks, and a
  geometric stitcher reconnects catheter fragments separated by
  1-to-3-slice signal dropouts. Next, a bundle graph transformer
  evaluates all candidate trajectories simultaneously in the context of
  the whole implant. Finally, a dedicated 1D axial neural network
  samples a fine \(0.5\text{ mm}\) multi-scale profile along the end of
  each trajectory to pinpoint the exact distal tip and output a
  calibrated split-conformal uncertainty interval.
\end{enumerate}

Beyond geometric benchmarking against three published methods, we
validate NeedleFinder 2026 across four clinical translation dimensions:
(i) three-cornered-hat observer adjudication against two human expert
teams and paired post-implant CT scans; (ii) AAPM TG-43U1
\(^{192}\text{Ir}\) radiation dosimetry; (iii) uncertainty-guided
clinician tip triage; and (iv) zero-shot generalization to needle-free
pelvic MRI, 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI, secondary MRI pulse
sequences, live 4D serial insertion scans, and pelvic CT.

\section{Architecture of NeedleFinder
2026}\label{architecture-of-needlefinder-2026}

A human physicist tracing catheters on MRI uses three kinds of clues in
sequence: first, looking for small round dark spots on each 2D slice;
second, checking whether those dark spots line up across adjacent
slices; and third, verifying that the full collection of 15 to 30 curves
forms a plausible implant bundle and stopping each curve where the dark
void disappears at the tip. NeedleFinder 2026 mirrors this exact
hierarchy in three stages (Figures \ref{fig:overview} and
\ref{fig:arch}).

\subsection{Stage 0: Physical Dark-Core Symmetry and Automatic Template
Cropping}\label{stage-0-physical-dark-core-symmetry-and-automatic-template-cropping}

\textbf{The physical intuition.} Why do standard neural networks confuse
muscle borders and organ walls with catheters? Because both are dark on
T2W MRI. However, their \emph{geometry} is different: a muscle border is
a dark edge with bright tissue on one side and dark tissue on the other,
whereas a catheter cross-section is a tiny dark core surrounded by
brighter tissue in \emph{every} direction.

Stage 0 turns this geometric observation into a fast, parameter-free 3D
filter \(S_0(x,y,z)\) implemented in multi-threaded native Rust. At
every voxel \(\mathbf{p}\) on axial slice \(z\), we sample six diameters
passing through \(\mathbf{p}\) at angles \(\theta_d = d\pi/6\)
(\(d \in \{0, \dots, 5\}\)) across four physical radii
\(r \in \mathcal{R} = \{1.35, 1.85, 2.5, 3.3\}\text{ mm}\) (converted to
pixels using the scan's in-plane spacing). Along each diameter \(d\) and
radius \(r\), we take the \emph{darker} of the two opposite ring points:
\[m_{d,r} = \min\!\big(I(\mathbf{p} + r\mathbf{u}_d),\; I(\mathbf{p} - r\mathbf{u}_d)\big).\]
If \(\mathbf{p}\) sits on a one-sided edge, at least one end of the
diameter remains dark, keeping \(m_{d,r}\) small. We then sort the six
directional values (\(m_{(1),r} \le \dots \le m_{(6),r}\)) and compute a
robust ring brightness that requires the surrounding tissue to be bright
in almost all directions while tolerating a single dark neighbor (such
as a touching twin catheter):
\[S_{\text{ring}}(\mathbf{p}) = \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \big[0.45\, m_{(2),r} + 0.55\, m_{(3),r}\big].\]
Combining this positive ring-to-core contrast with the relative darkness
of the \(3 \times 3\) smoothed center intensity
\(\bar{I}_{3\times 3}(\mathbf{p}) \in [0, 1]\) yields the raw dark-core
symmetry score,
\[S_0(\mathbf{p}) \propto \max\!\big(0,\; S_{\text{ring}}(\mathbf{p}) - \bar{I}_{3\times 3}(\mathbf{p})\big)\cdot\big[1 - \bar{I}_{3\times 3}(\mathbf{p})\big],\]
which is normalized to \([0, 1]\) per volume. Because \(S_0\) takes the
maximum over radii up to \(3.3\text{ mm}\) and uses the 2nd and 3rd
order statistics across six angles, it remains invariant to realistic
oblique catheter tilt (\(\alpha \le 25^\circ\text{--}30^\circ\) relative
to the slice normal, where the in-plane elliptical cross-section
elongates by only \(\sec\alpha \le 1.15\)). On very thick slices
(\(\Delta z = 4\text{--}6\text{ mm}\)) where steep tilt causes stronger
partial-volume elongation, we first upsample the volume along \(z\) to
\(1.6\text{ mm}\) spacing and rely on Stage 2A fragment stitching to
bridge dimmed slices.

\textbf{Automatic reference-free cropping of the template plane.} Below
the patient's perineum, the inferior slices of the MRI scan contain
outside air and the plastic needle-guiding template. Previously,
pipelines relied on manual bounding-box files (\texttt{.acsv}) to crop
out these bottom slices, which is unavailable in a fully automatic
workflow and occasionally clipped tilted templates too high---cutting
off the bases of 42 catheters across our cohort. We replaced this with a
\textbf{100\% reference-free axial template locator}
(\texttt{k\_lim\_auto} in the code). After standardizing the volume to
caudo-cranial slice ordering via its DICOM/NRRD direction cosines, the
locator scans upward from the inferior boundary and detects the
step-change in 90th-percentile soft-tissue intensity alongside the
emergence of clustered dark-core candidates (\(S_0\)) within the central
field of view. Across all 54 patients, it places the crop boundary a
median of \(2.0\) slices deeper than the manual \texttt{.acsv} boxes
(mean \(1.91\) slices deeper, IQR \(1.0\); \(64.1\) vs.~\(62.2\) active
slices on average), eliminating all 42 in-field-of-view base truncations
and preserving \textbf{100.0\% of in-field-of-view catheter length}
(\(99.65\%\) of total unclipped VTK length) without ever consulting
manual annotations. Stage 0 and automatic cropping run in \textbf{0.27
s} per volume on a 24-core CPU (0.07 s on standard scans, 0.38 s on
high-resolution scans).

\subsection{Stage 1: Native-Resolution 3D U-Net with Self-Supervised and
Multi-Rater
Learning}\label{stage-1-native-resolution-3d-u-net-with-self-supervised-and-multi-rater-learning}

\textbf{Why native resolution and multiple output heads matter.} Most 3D
medical segmentation pipelines first resample every scan to
\(1 \times 1 \times 1\text{ mm}^3\) voxels. For a 2 mm catheter imaged
at \(0.35\text{ mm}\) pixel resolution, resampling blurs the sharp dark
core into the surrounding tissue. Conversely, applying a standard
\(3 \times 3 \times 3\) convolution directly on the native grid mixes
features across up to 18 mm vertically (\(z\)) but only 1 mm
horizontally (\(x, y\)).

Our Stage 1 network (Figure \ref{fig:arch}a) therefore operates directly
on the native voxel grid using an anisotropic 3D residual U-Net with
squeeze-and-excitation channel attention at four scales (24, 48, 96, and
144 channels). Crucially, it pools spatially \emph{only in-plane}
(\(x, y\)) at the first two scales so that thick axial slices are never
compressed early. The network takes six input channels---normalized T2W
intensity, Stage 0 dark-core symmetry \(S_0\), ring contrast, smoothed
intensity, normalized axial height \(z\), and radial distance from the
scan center---and predicts four complementary outputs:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Tubular mask (\(P_{\text{mask}}\)):} The probability that a
  voxel lies within a catheter cross-section (trained with focal binary
  cross-entropy, up-weighted 19-fold on catheter voxels and on false
  Stage 0 vessel peaks, plus soft Dice loss).
\item
  \textbf{Sub-pixel centerline peak (\(P_{\text{peak}}\)):} A narrow 2D
  Gaussian heatmap (\(\sigma = 0.70\text{ mm}\)) centered on the exact
  intersection of each catheter with the slice (trained with
  peak-weighted mean-squared error). \textbf{Why this matters:} When two
  catheters touch, their binary masks (\(P_{\text{mask}}\)) merge into
  one blob, but their two centerline peaks (\(P_{\text{peak}}\)) remain
  distinct!
\item
  \textbf{Local 3D direction field
  (\(V_x, V_y = \Delta x/\Delta z, \Delta y/\Delta z\)):} The local tilt
  of the catheter as it passes from slice to slice (trained with smooth
  \(L_1\) loss and an axial consistency penalty), telling the tracker
  where to look on the next slice.
\item
  \textbf{Obturator segmentation (\(P_{\text{obt}}\)):} A dedicated mask
  for the large central vaginal obturator and tandem, preventing their
  dark plastic rims from generating false needle detections.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{How we teach Stage 1 to ignore blood vessels and noisy
single-rater labels.} We introduce two training innovations for Stage 1:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Self-supervised pre-training on 51 external pelvic MRI scans
  with anatomical hard negatives.} Before training on any benchmark
  brachytherapy patient, we pre-train the 3D U-Net for 45 epochs across
  the 51 external needle-free pelvic MRI volumes (48 public MSD prostate
  scans + 3 non-benchmark BWH pelvic scans). In each training step, we
  digitally inject 8 to 20 realistic synthetic 3D catheter
  trajectories---curved dark cores with partial-volume bright rims and
  random signal dropouts---while simultaneously masking random 3D tissue
  patches for reconstruction. Because these 51 external scans contain
  \emph{zero} real catheters, \textbf{every real pelvic blood vessel,
  bowel loop, or fascial border that triggers a high Stage 0 symmetry
  score (\(S_0 > 0.25\)) outside the synthetic tubes serves as a
  guaranteed anatomical hard negative}. The network thus learns the
  exact textural difference between a plastic catheter void and a pelvic
  vein before ever seeing the benchmark cohort.
\item
  \textbf{Multi-rater consensus supervision.} On the 40 training scans
  traced by both human expert teams, training on a single team's
  annotations penalizes the network whenever it finds a real catheter
  that Team 1 overlooked but Team 2 traced, and passes along
  single-observer clicking jitter. Within each outer training fold
  (never touching test patients), we pair matching trajectories between
  Team 1 and Team 2 (\(\le 3.5\text{ mm}\), \(n = 465\) pairs) and
  supervise Stage 1 along their precision-weighted consensus centerline
  (weight \(1.15\)). Catheters traced only by Team 1 receive weight
  \(1.00\), and additional catheters traced only by Team 2 (\(n = 35\)
  after excluding \(465\) primary-matched and \(13\)
  obturator-overlapping traces) receive soft positive weight \(0.55\)
  instead of being treated as background false alarms.
\item
  \textbf{Inner 5-fold ensembling and 4-view test-time augmentation.}
  Within each outer training fold, we train 5 inner-fold Stage 1 models
  with rich 3D MRI augmentations (random 3D bias fields, slice-thickness
  blurring, slice dropouts, and flips) and average their predictions
  across four flip symmetries (\(I, X, Y, XY\)) at inference.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 2: Graph Tracking, Fragment Stitching, Bundle Scoring,
and 1D Tip
Localization}\label{stage-2-graph-tracking-fragment-stitching-bundle-scoring-and-1d-tip-localization}

Once Stage 1 has highlighted candidate catheter centers and directions
on every slice, Stage 2 solves three sequential geometric problems
(Figure \ref{fig:arch}b):

\subsubsection{Step 2A: Connecting slice detections and stitching split
fragments}\label{step-2a-connecting-slice-detections-and-stitching-split-fragments}

On each slice, local peaks of \(P_{\text{peak}}\) and \(S_0\) become
graph nodes (each described by 16 local image and direction features).
Directed edges connect nodes across 1 to 4 slices (up to
\(24\text{ mm}\) vertically) whenever their in-plane displacement agrees
with the Stage 1 direction field.

Why use a graph network alongside Kalman tracking instead of greedy
slice-to-slice linking alone? Because two consecutive links can each
look reasonable on their own yet form a sharp, unphysical kink when
joined together. First, a fast native C++17/Rust bidirectional Kalman
tracker traces candidate 3D paths from the Stage 0 and Stage 1 seeds in
both directions and merges near-duplicate paths within
\(1.65\text{--}2.05\text{ mm}\) while preserving genuinely touching twin
catheters. Second, a \textbf{4-layer triplet message-passing network}
(\texttt{NeedleSliceMPNN}, hidden width 64) scores every node and edge
in the slice graph using attention-weighted messages from both its
incoming and outgoing neighboring edges---explicitly rewarding smooth,
collinear trajectories, refining sub-pixel coordinates, and penalizing
kinks.

Finally, our failure analysis of earlier pipelines revealed that
mid-shaft signal dropouts (where a catheter crosses a dark vessel or air
pocket over 1 to 3 slices) frequently split one physical catheter into a
lower fragment and an upper fragment---causing both a false-positive
extra fragment and a severe tip undershoot on the lower track. We
therefore added a \textbf{collinear split-fragment stitcher}: whenever a
lower track fragment ends and an upper fragment begins within an axial
gap of \(\le 20\text{ mm}\), and their linearly extrapolated positions
at the midpoint agree within \(\le 2.2\text{ mm}\) with matching 3D
slopes (\(\Delta \text{slope} \le 0.14\)), the two fragments are
seamlessly stitched into a single full-length candidate (creating 102
stitched full-length trajectories across the 54 patients).

\subsubsection{Step 2B: Judging the whole implant bundle
together}\label{step-2b-judging-the-whole-implant-bundle-together}

After Step 2A, the candidate pool recovers \textbf{97.4\%} of all real
catheters (\(98.8\%\) of long shafts), but still contains an average of
6.6 false vascular tracks per patient. How can the algorithm tell a real
catheter apart from a dark vein that happens to look tubular over a few
slices? By looking at the \textbf{whole bundle at once}: real catheters
enter through a common perineal template corridor, run roughly parallel
to the central obturator, maintain a consistent spacing, and terminate
along a coherent surface covering the tumor.

We summarize each candidate trajectory by a 26-feature token (length,
straightness, Stage 0/1/2A confidence percentiles, entry position and
tilt relative to the bundle median, and tip contrast gradient) and
describe every pair of trajectories by 8 geometric interaction features
(minimum 3D clearance, shared-slice separation, angular divergence, and
overlap). A \textbf{bundle graph transformer}
(\texttt{TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer}) applies two layers of
edge-biased local attention between neighboring tracks followed by
global self-attention across the entire candidate bundle, outputting a
calibrated probability \(p_m \in [0, 1]\) that each candidate is a real
catheter. Candidates outside a self-calibrating implant radius (98th
percentile of high-confidence tracks plus \(16\text{ mm}\)) require a
higher confidence threshold to pass. To enable zero-shot and few-patient
learning, Stage 2B can also be pre-trained on \textbf{SynthBrachy3T}---a
physics-based generator of 300 simulated candidate bundles containing
realistic touching twins, short obturator channels, split fragments, and
vascular false alarms---before fine-tuning on the clinical training
fold.

\subsubsection{Step 2C: Pinpointing the deepest tip and quantifying its
uncertainty}\label{step-2c-pinpointing-the-deepest-tip-and-quantifying-its-uncertainty}

Even when a catheter's shaft is traced to within half a millimeter,
finding its exact deepest (cranial) tip on thick-slice T2W MRI is
difficult: the dark void tapers off gradually across 2 to 6 mm thick
slices, or collides with dark bowel gas just above the uterus.

To resolve the tip with sub-slice precision, we built a
\textbf{dedicated 1D axial tip localizer}
(\texttt{Dedicated1DTipLocalizer}, Figure \ref{fig:arch}b). Starting at
the top of each candidate trajectory and continuing along its 3D tangent
direction, the network samples a \textbf{91-step 1D profile} from
\(-25.0\text{ mm}\) below the initial tip to \(+20.0\text{ mm}\) above
it, in fine \textbf{\(0.5\text{ mm}\) sub-slice steps}. At every
\(0.5\text{ mm}\) step, it extracts 22 multi-scale measurements: center
and ring T2W intensities, local annular contrast at two radii, first and
second axial derivatives (\(\partial_z, \partial_{zz}\)), Stage 0
symmetry \(S_0\), Stage 1 mask and peak probabilities, obturator
probability, and depth relative to neighboring catheter tips. A
\textbf{3-block dilated 1D ResNet} (kernel size 5, dilations 1, 2, 4)
followed by \textbf{4-head 1D self-attention} reads this 91-step
sequence and predicts both a 91-bin probability distribution over the
exact stopping point and a continuous sub-millimeter offset
\(\Delta s\); this predicted offset shifts the trajectory's cranial
termination point along its 3D tangent vector before the full 3D curve
is smoothed with a cubic polynomial in \(z\) (\texttt{Poly3}).

Crucially, the sharpness (entropy) of this 91-bin tip distribution,
combined with the local image contrast drop and slice thickness
\(\Delta z\), provides an immediate out-of-fold \textbf{tip uncertainty
score \(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}\)}. To turn this raw score into a
physical error bar in millimeters without assuming Gaussian errors, we
use \textbf{split-conformal calibration}: on the held-out inner
validation patients of each fold, we compute the empirical
\((1-\alpha)\) quantile \(\hat{q}_{1-\alpha}\) of the normalized
non-conformity ratios
\(r_i = E_{\text{tip}, i} / \hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}, i}\). Multiplying
any unseen test tip's uncertainty score by \(\hat{q}_{1-\alpha}\) yields
an adaptive 3D prediction radius
\(\hat{r}_{1-\alpha} = \hat{q}_{1-\alpha}\,\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}\)
that is guaranteed distribution-free to cover the true tip at the target
nominal rate (e.g., 90\% or 95\%) while ranking which 2 to 5 tips in an
implant warrant a quick visual check.

\section{Evaluation Protocol, Baselines, and Clinical
Translation}\label{evaluation-protocol-baselines-and-clinical-translation}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Strictly Nested \(5 \times 5\)
Cross-Validation and
Baselines}{Strictly Nested 5 \textbackslash times 5 Cross-Validation and Baselines}}\label{strictly-nested-5-times-5-cross-validation-and-baselines}

In small clinical datasets, subtle leaks---such as picking the best
training epoch on the test fold, using manual crop boxes, or training a
second-stage network on overfitted first-stage predictions---can inflate
reported accuracy. We eliminated all three sources of optimism using a
\textbf{strictly nested \(5 \times 5\) patient-level cross-validation
protocol} across the 54 patients:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Locked outer test folds and disjoint inner validation.} The 54
  patients are partitioned into 5 outer folds (\(43\text{--}44\)
  outer-train patients and \(10\text{--}11\) outer-test patients per
  fold). Inside each outer fold, the outer-train patients are split
  80/20 into an inner-training set (\(34\text{--}35\) patients) and an
  inner-validation set (\(9\) patients). Every hyperparameter,
  early-stopping checkpoint, temperature calibration factor, and
  conformal quantile is selected \emph{exclusively} on the 9
  inner-validation patients. Each outer-test patient is evaluated once.
\item
  \textbf{100\% reference-free inference.} Every test volume is cropped
  solely by the automatic Stage 0 template-plane locator, with zero
  access to clinical scene files or manual annotations.
\item
  \textbf{Eliminating two-stage stacking bias via inner out-of-fold
  cross-fitting (\texttt{4c}).} In any two-stage machine learning
  pipeline, Stage 1 makes slightly cleaner predictions on the patients
  it was trained on than on brand-new patients. If Stage 2 is trained
  directly on those slightly optimistic Stage 1 maps (\texttt{4a} and
  \texttt{4b}), it might misjudge how noisy Stage 1 will be at test
  time. To verify that our pipeline suffers zero stacking bias, we also
  trained \(5 \times 5 = 25\) inner-fold Stage 1 models so that every
  training patient receives strictly out-of-fold Stage 1 maps before
  Stage 2 is trained (reported as configuration \texttt{4c} in Table
  \ref{tbl:main}).
\end{enumerate}

We compared NeedleFinder 2026 against three published baselines
re-implemented and evaluated on the identical nested folds and automatic
crop bounds: - \textbf{PMB 2019 (3D U-Net + DBSCAN) {[}5{]}:} Isotropic
\(1\text{ mm}^3\) resampling, 3D U-Net voxel segmentation,
principal-axis clustering with DBSCAN, and spline fitting. -
\textbf{ResUNet + clDice + Skeleton {[}40,64{]}:} A native-resolution 3D
residual U-Net trained with focal cross-entropy, soft Dice, and
topology-preserving centerline-Dice (clDice) loss, followed by 3D
skeletonization and polynomial fitting. - \textbf{Xiao 2026
Structure-Aware Sync-Track {[}66{]}:} Implant-region gating,
direction-constrained 3D Hough transform initialization, synchronous
inward slice tracking, and cubic polynomial smoothing, evaluated both on
out-of-fold MRI segmentation masks and on ground-truth masks (as an
oracle upper bound).

\subsection{Geometric, Observer, Dosimetric, and Generalization
Metrics}\label{geometric-observer-dosimetric-and-generalization-metrics}

\textbf{Geometric matching and error metrics.} For each patient,
predicted and reference trajectories are matched one-to-one using the
Hungarian algorithm (\texttt{scipy.optimize.linear\_sum\_assignment}),
forbidding pairs whose mean in-plane distance across shared slices
exceeds \(3.5\text{ mm}\). On matched pairs, we measure \textbf{in-plane
shaft error} (\(E_{\text{shaft}}\), across 40 equidistant shared-slice
stations), \textbf{3D symmetric curve distance} (\(D_{\text{avg}}\)),
\textbf{95th-percentile Hausdorff distance} (\(\text{HD}_{95}\)), and
\textbf{3D distal tip error} (\(E_{\text{tip}}\), Euclidean distance
between predicted and reference cranial endpoints). Our algorithm
reconstructs \emph{all} visible plastic tubes in the scan---both the 759
full-depth treatment shafts (\texttt{CY}) and the 304 shorter guide
tubes inside the vaginal obturator (\texttt{HT}). When we evaluate
recall on the 759 full-depth shafts alone, an algorithm prediction that
accurately traces one of the 304 real obturator tubes is not a false
alarm on a blood vessel; we therefore report \textbf{obturator-adjusted
precision (\(P_{\text{adj}}\)) and \(F1_{\text{adj}}\)}, which avoid
penalizing valid detections of those secondary tubes. While Table
\ref{tbl:main} summarizes pooled error distributions across all matched
catheters, all paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests are conducted
at the \textbf{patient level} (\(N = 54\) paired patient means, or
\(N = 23\) in the adhesion subgroup) to respect within-patient
clustering (assigning fixed worst-case penalties of \(3.5\text{ mm}\)
shaft, \(10\text{ mm}\) curve, and \(20\text{ mm}\)
tip/\(\text{HD}_{95}\) if a baseline yields zero matches on a scan),
complemented by 1,000-resample patient-level bootstrap 95\% confidence
intervals {[}81{]}.

\textbf{Three-cornered-hat observer adjudication and non-inferiority
testing.} If an algorithm disagrees with Human Team 1 by
\(0.5\text{ mm}\), how much of that discrepancy is the algorithm's error
and how much is Team 1's own hand-clicking jitter? With only two
observers, it is impossible to tell. However, when \emph{three} sources
trace the same catheter---Team 1 (\texttt{CY}), Team 2
(\texttt{LB/AMe}), and out-of-fold NeedleFinder 2026 (\(n = 432\)
three-way matched catheters)---we observe three pairwise difference
variances (\(V_{12}, V_{13}, V_{23}\)) for three unknown internal error
variances (\(\sigma_1^2, \sigma_2^2, \sigma_3^2\)). \textbf{Grubbs'
(1948) three-cornered-hat estimator} solves this \(3 \times 3\) linear
system (\(\hat{\sigma}_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}(V_{12} + V_{13} - V_{23})\)) to
isolate each source's intrinsic, reference-free error standard
deviation, which we compute via classic maximum likelihood (RMS),
outlier-resistant median absolute deviation (MAD), and 15\% Winsorized
estimators.

We also test whether the algorithm's disagreement with Team 1 is
statistically non-inferior to Team 2's disagreement with Team 1
(\(H_0: E_{\text{NF},\text{Team 1}} - E_{\text{Team 2},\text{Team 1}} \ge \Delta\)
at one-sided significance level \(\alpha = 0.05\)). Pre-specified
clinical non-inferiority margins were set to \(\Delta = 0.25\text{ mm}\)
on shafts (roughly half the finest pixel spacing) and
\(\Delta = 1.00\text{ mm}\) (median) / \(1.50\text{ mm}\) (mean) on 3D
tips (corresponding to half a slice thickness and the
\(1\text{--}1.5\text{ mm}\) TG-43U1 dosimetric sensitivity threshold
{[}19,20{]}). We evaluate non-inferiority at both the patient level
(\(N = 40\)) and the catheter level (\(n = 432\)) using one-sided paired
Wilcoxon tests and two-sided \(90\%\) (\(1 - 2\alpha\))
patient-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples).
Finally, whenever the two human teams disagree on a catheter tip by
\(\ge 5.0\text{ mm}\) on MRI (\(n = 70\) catheters across 38 patients
with paired CT), we use the patient's post-implant pelvic CT scan as an
independent physical tie-breaker.

\textbf{AAPM TG-43U1 HDR \(^{192}\text{Ir}\) dosimetry and simulated
1-click tip triage.} To translate millimeter geometry errors into
clinical radiation dose differences across all 54 patients without
confounding from inter-physician contouring variability (and because
historical DICOM RTSTRUCT/RTPLAN files were not archived), we simulated
HDR \(^{192}\text{Ir}\) delivery using the AAPM TG-43U1 line-source
formalism on standardized, implant-conformal anatomical envelopes. Each
catheter was loaded with \(5.0\text{ mm}\) dwell steps along its cranial
\(45\text{ mm}\) (starting \(3.5\text{ mm}\) below the physical tip,
active source length \(L = 3.5\text{ mm}\)) on a \(1.5\text{ mm}\)
isotropic 3D dose grid. A single-fraction prescription of
\(D_{90} = 7.0\text{ Gy}\) was optimized via non-negative least-squares
onto the implant-conformal high-risk target volume
(\(\text{HR-CTV}_{\text{implant}}\), defined as the \(8.0\text{ mm}\)
radial envelope around the active reference implant) while constraining
\(2\text{ cm}^3\) proxy shells for the anterior bladder, posterior
rectum, and cranial sigmoid/fundal organ-at-risk (OAR) zones. We
evaluated two clinical protocols: - \textbf{Protocol A (Fixed-Plan
Transfer):} Reference dwell times optimized on the expert catheters are
transferred onto the one-to-one matched predicted catheters (with missed
reference catheters contributing zero dwell delivery) to isolate pure
geometric-to-dosimetric sensitivity. - \textbf{Protocol B
(Counterfactual Plan Re-Optimization):} Modeling the end-to-end clinical
planning workflow, dwell times are optimized from scratch on \emph{all}
algorithm-predicted catheters and the resulting dose is evaluated on the
reference target and OAR anatomy. - \textbf{Simulated 1-click clinician
tip review:} Because 98.8\% of tip error is longitudinal along the
already-reconstructed 3D shaft, we also model an interactive
verification step in which the physicist inspects the top
\(k \in \{1, \dots, 10\}\) tips per patient ranked by out-of-fold
uncertainty \(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}\) and clicks the true distal
termination along the shaft (modeled by the retrospective 1-click oracle
\(E_{\text{tip}} \to \sqrt{\Delta r_\perp^2 + \min(|\Delta s_\parallel|, 0.85\text{ mm})^2}\)
whenever \(E_{\text{tip}} > 2.0\text{ mm}\), leaving
\(E_{\text{shaft}}\) unchanged, at an estimated ergonomic inspection
time of \(\sim 8\text{ s}\) per flagged tip in 3D Slicer).

\textbf{Computing environment.} All training and benchmarks ran on a
Linux workstation equipped with a 24-core workstation CPU (AMD Ryzen
Threadripper PRO 7965WX, 48 threads, 256 GB RAM) and an NVIDIA RTX 4090
GPU (24 GB). For clinical deployment, the complete pipeline is packaged
as a one-click module in \textbf{3D Slicer 5.10} {[}82{]} using native
Rust (Stage 0) and ONNX Runtime (Stages 1 and 2), requiring no GPU.

Four clear lessons emerge from the benchmark:

\textbf{1. Pure voxel segmentation fragments catheters and hallucinates
vessels.} The native-resolution \texttt{ResUNet\ +\ clDice} baseline
detects nearly every catheter location (\(92.4\%\) recall) and traces
matched segments accurately (\(0.43\text{ mm}\) median shaft error).
However, because single-slice signal dropouts break catheter masks into
pieces and dark pelvic veins are segmented as tubes, it outputs
\(4,015\) fragments across the 54 scans---yielding a precision of only
\(24.5\%\) and an F1 score of \(38.7\%\).

\textbf{2. Structure-aware tracking works on perfect masks, but fails
when masks are noisy.} When given ground-truth oracle masks (Table
\ref{tbl:main}, Part B, bottom row), the \texttt{Xiao\ 2026\ Sync-Track}
algorithm achieves \(97.7\%\) recall, \(90.0\%\) F1, and
\(0.11\text{ mm}\) median shaft error---confirming that synchronous
tracking separates touching masks effectively when the segmentation is
clean. On real out-of-fold MRI segmentation masks, however, its F1 drops
to \(54.2\%\) (\(39.2\%\) precision) because vascular false alarms
inside the implant region are tracked as catheters and never pruned at
the bundle level.

\textbf{3. NeedleFinder 2026 achieves the highest F1 score and lowest
tip error across every cohort.} At the default operating threshold
(\(\tau = 50\%\)), NeedleFinder 2026 (\texttt{4b} in Table
\ref{tbl:main}) detects \textbf{91.4\% of all 1,063 catheters}
(\(972/1,063\)) with \textbf{86.9\% precision} (\(972/1,118\)) and
\textbf{89.1\% F1} (reaching \textbf{89.4\% F1} at \(\tau = 55\%\) and
\textbf{93.6\% recall} at a recall-oriented threshold of
\(\tau = 35\%\)). It outperforms all three published methods by a wide
and statistically significant margin: \textbf{+6.0 F1 points} over PMB
2019 (\(83.1\%\) F1, \(p = 1.1 \times 10^{-5}\)), \textbf{+34.9 F1
points} over Xiao 2026 (\(54.2\%\) F1, \(p = 3.0 \times 10^{-10}\)), and
\textbf{+50.4 F1 points} over ResUNet + clDice (\(38.7\%\) F1,
\(p = 5.2 \times 10^{-10}\)).

Along the catheter shaft, NeedleFinder 2026 reaches a \textbf{median
in-plane error of \(0.47\text{ mm}\)} (mean \(0.59 \pm 0.40\text{ mm}\),
95th percentile \(1.37\text{ mm}\)). At the deepest tip, our 1D axial
tip localizer and fragment stitcher cut \textbf{median 3D tip error to
\(2.27\text{ mm}\)} (mean \(6.23 \pm 10.49\text{ mm}\)), reducing mean
tip error by \(42\%\) compared with PMB 2019 (\(10.75\text{ mm}\) mean /
\(3.39\text{ mm}\) median, \(p = 1.7 \times 10^{-8}\)) and by \(60\%\)
compared with Xiao 2026 (\(15.65\text{ mm}\) mean / \(6.04\text{ mm}\)
median, \(p = 6.2 \times 10^{-10}\)). Nearly three-fifths
(\textbf{58.7\%}, \(571/972\)) of matched catheters have a 3D tip error
within \(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\) (roughly one slice thickness). On the
\textbf{759 full-depth active-dwell shafts}, NeedleFinder 2026 reaches
\textbf{91.3\% recall} (\textbf{94.7\%} at \(\tau = 35\%\)),
\textbf{88.7\% obturator-adjusted precision}, and \textbf{90.0\%
\(F1_{\text{adj}}\)}, with a median shaft error of \(0.45\text{ mm}\)
(mean \(0.57\text{ mm}\)) and a median tip error of \(2.41\text{ mm}\).

\textbf{4. The advantage is largest when catheters touch, and inner
cross-fitting confirms zero stacking bias.} On the \textbf{23-patient
severe catheter-adhesion subgroup} (571 catheters), where touching masks
cause DBSCAN clustering and greedy trackers to merge or swap paths,
NeedleFinder 2026 reaches \textbf{91.3\% F1} (\(93.2\%\) recall,
\(89.6\%\) precision, \(0.46\text{ mm}\) median shaft error,
\(2.51\text{ mm}\) median tip error), outperforming PMB 2019 (\(86.3\%\)
F1, \(p = 0.0012\); tip \(p = 4.5 \times 10^{-6}\)), Xiao 2026
(\(63.9\%\) F1, \(p = 4.8 \times 10^{-7}\)), and ResUNet + clDice
(\(48.4\%\) F1, \(p = 4.8 \times 10^{-7}\)). Moreover, training Stage 2
on strictly out-of-fold inner Stage 1 maps (\texttt{4c} in Table
\ref{tbl:main}) yields the exact same \textbf{89.1\% whole-cohort F1}
(\(92.3\%\) recall) and even slightly higher accuracy on long shafts
(\textbf{90.5\% \(F1_{\text{adj}}\)}) and touching catheters
(\textbf{91.8\% F1}), proving that our results contain zero two-stage
stacking optimism.

\textbf{Why every stage is needed (Table \ref{tbl:main}, Part B; Figure

\(8.95\text{ mm}\) mean / \(2.83\text{ mm}\) median tip error). -
\textbf{Adding Stage 2B (bundle graph transformer, multi-rater
supervision, and collinear fragment stitching):} Evaluating the
candidate bundle as a whole (\(87.1\%\) F1 with temperature calibration;
\(86.4\%\) F1 with multi-rater Stage 2 supervision) and stitching

\subsection{Is the Algorithm as Accurate as a Second Human Expert?
(Three-Cornered-Hat \& CT
Adjudication)}\label{is-the-algorithm-as-accurate-as-a-second-human-expert-three-cornered-hat-ct-adjudication}

\input{tables/tab3_interobserver.tex}

Because a 2 mm catheter is only a few pixels wide and slices are up to 6
mm thick, two human experts tracing the same MRI scan never agree
perfectly {[}24,83,84{]}. An automatic method is clinically ready when
its discrepancies from a human expert are no larger than the
discrepancies \emph{between} two independent human experts {[}37,48{]}.
Table \ref{tbl:interobserver} tests this across \textbf{all 40 doubly
traced clinical scans} using four complementary analyses:

\textbf{1. Direct comparison against human inter-observer agreement
(Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}, top two blocks).} When Expert Team 2
(\texttt{LB/AMe}, 460 long traces) is compared directly against Expert
Team 1 (\texttt{CY}, 505 long traces) across the 40 scans, 424 long
catheters match within \(3.5\text{ mm}\) (\(84.0\%\) recall, \(92.2\%\)
precision, \(87.9\%\) F1). Between these two human teams, the
\textbf{in-plane shaft discrepancy is \(0.49\text{ mm}\) median}
(\(0.70 \pm 0.58\text{ mm}\) mean, \(1.90\text{ mm}\) P95) and the
\textbf{3D tip discrepancy is \(3.24\text{ mm}\) median}
(\(6.28 \pm 7.29\text{ mm}\) mean, \(21.88\text{ mm}\) P95).

Evaluated strictly out-of-fold on the exact same 40 scans against Team 1
(\texttt{CY+HT}, 723 tracks), \textbf{NeedleFinder 2026 achieves a
smaller shaft discrepancy (\(0.47\text{ mm}\) median,
\(0.58 \pm 0.39\text{ mm}\) mean, \(1.33\text{ mm}\) P95) and a smaller
3D tip discrepancy (\(2.10\text{ mm}\) median,
\(5.26 \pm 8.78\text{ mm}\) mean, \(19.99\text{ mm}\) P95) than the
disagreement between the two human teams}, while matching \(90.3\%\) of
Team 1's trajectories (\(87.6\%\) F1). Furthermore, when evaluated
against the \textbf{union of both human teams} (759 distinct
trajectories), \textbf{16 NeedleFinder predictions that looked like
``false alarms'' against Team 1 alone turn out to be real catheters
independently traced by Team 2}, lifting precision by \(+2.1\)
percentage points to \(87.1\%\).

\textbf{2. Reference-free Grubbs' three-cornered-hat intrinsic error
estimation (Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}, third block).} To measure
each source's true internal error without treating Team 1 as infallible,
we applied Grubbs' three-cornered-hat estimator across the \(n = 432\)
catheters matched three-way among Team 1, Team 2, and NeedleFinder 2026
(formed by intersecting the 476 all-catheter human pairs with
out-of-fold NeedleFinder predictions within \(3.5\text{ mm}\)). The
intrinsic outlier-resistant (MAD-scaled) in-plane shaft standard
deviations are \textbf{\(0.35\text{ mm}\) for Human Team 1},
\textbf{\(0.39\text{ mm}\) for NeedleFinder 2026}, and
\textbf{\(0.48\text{ mm}\) for Human Team 2}. For axial tip stopping
height (\(\sigma_z\)), NeedleFinder 2026 achieves a lower intrinsic
error than Human Team 2 under both the robust MAD estimator
(\textbf{\(2.98\text{ mm}\) vs.~\(3.55\text{ mm}\)}, where Team 1 hits
the \(0.00\text{ mm}\) finite-sample subtraction lower bound because
median signed \(\Delta z_{\text{Team 1}-\text{NF}} = 0.00\text{ mm}\))
and the 15\% Winsorized estimator, which yields strictly positive
intrinsic standard deviations of \textbf{\(3.77\text{ mm}\) for Team 1},
\textbf{\(4.94\text{ mm}\) for NeedleFinder 2026}, and
\textbf{\(5.09\text{ mm}\) for Team 2}.

\textbf{3. Formal one-sided statistical non-inferiority (Table
\ref{tbl:interobserver}, fourth block).} Across all \(N = 40\)
dual-traced patients (\(n = 432\) three-way matched catheters),
NeedleFinder 2026 passes formal one-sided non-inferiority testing
relative to human inter-observer disagreement on every pre-specified
endpoint at both the patient level and the catheter level. Specifically,
the algorithm's error against Team 1 is smaller than Team 2's error
against Team 1 on \textbf{shaft mean}
(\(0.56\text{ vs. }0.72\text{ mm}\),
\(p_{\text{pt}} = 1.3 \times 10^{-8}\)), \textbf{shaft median}
(\(0.43\text{ vs. }0.49\text{ mm}\),
\(p_{\text{pt}} = 8.2 \times 10^{-10}\)), \textbf{3D tip median}
(\(2.33\text{ vs. }3.18\text{ mm}\),
\(p_{\text{pt}} = 2.6 \times 10^{-7}\)), and \textbf{3D tip mean}
(\(5.51\text{ vs. }6.10\text{ mm}\),
\(p_{\text{pt}} = 1.1 \times 10^{-4}\), bootstrap
\(p_{\text{boot}} = 0.0014\); full 90\% confidence intervals and
catheter-level \(p\)-values in Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}). Even when
the reference role is flipped so Team 2 serves as the reference,
NeedleFinder 2026 still passes non-inferiority on 3D tip median
(\(p_{\text{boot}} = 0.046\), \(p_{\text{cath}} = 9.6 \times 10^{-5}\)).

\textbf{4. Independent adjudication on paired post-implant Pelvic CT
scans.} On CT, catheters and metallic stylets have unmistakable
Hounsfield contrast. Using the 38 patients who had both intraoperative
MRI and post-implant Pelvic CT, we adjudicated the \(n = 70\) catheters
where Human Team 1 and Human Team 2 disagreed on the MRI tip position by
\(\ge 5.0\text{ mm}\). Measured against the independent CT tip position,
\textbf{NeedleFinder 2026 achieved a median 3D tip error of
\(4.20\text{ mm}\)}, outperforming both \textbf{Human Team 1
(\(4.31\text{ mm}\))} and \textbf{Human Team 2 (\(8.00\text{ mm}\))} and
lying closer to the CT ground truth than single-rater MRI on
\textbf{78.6\% (\(55/70\))} of disputed catheters. Across all 100 MRI
tracks predicted by NeedleFinder 2026 on these 38 patients that did not
match Team 1 (\texttt{CY}), \textbf{52.0\% (\(52/100\)) were confirmed
to be real physical catheters on the paired CT scan}.

\subsection{Interpretability, Confidence Calibration, and Physical
Causes of Residual
Errors}\label{interpretability-confidence-calibration-and-physical-causes-of-residual-errors}

\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\linewidth,height=\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../reports/figures/fig7_calibration_and_reasons.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{(a) Confidence calibration} across all 1,960 stitched
candidate tracks in the 54 patients, before (dashed) and after (solid
vermilion) inner-fold temperature scaling. \textbf{(b) Reason-coded
filtering decisions} at the default threshold \(\tau = 50\%\), showing
how many candidates are kept or pruned by each rule and whether pruned
tracks were false alarms, redundant duplicates of an already-kept
catheter, or lost real catheters.}\label{fig:calib}
\end{figure}

For a clinician to trust an automated reconstruction, its confidence
scores must be well calibrated and its filtering decisions transparent.
Across all \(1,960\) stitched candidate tracks entering Stage 2B
(\(36.3\) per patient), inner-fold temperature scaling reduces the
expected calibration error (ECE) from \(0.101\) to \textbf{\(0.059\)}
(and to \textbf{\(0.056\)} across the \(1,118\) accepted tracks at
\(\tau = 50\%\), where mean predicted confidence is \(91.9\%\) and
closest-reference precision is \(90.8\%\); Figure \ref{fig:calib}a).
Every discarded track is tagged with an explicit reason code (Figure
\ref{fig:calib}b): at \(\tau = 50\%\), \(673\) candidates are removed as
\texttt{below\_threshold} (eliminating \(80.6\%\) of all false
candidates) and \(169\) are removed as
\texttt{duplicate\_of\_kept\_track} (suppressing \(153\) redundant
collinear or touching fragments at the cost of only \(2\) lost
catheters).

To understand the exact physical limits of the pipeline, we audited
every single missed catheter, false positive, and \(>10\text{ mm}\) tip
error across the 54 patients:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{What causes the 146 unmatched predictions (\(2.7\) per
  patient)?} Nearly a third (\textbf{31.5\%}, \(46/146\)) are simply
  residual split or near-duplicate segments of an already-detected
  catheter, \textbf{20.5\% (\(30/146\))} correspond to real catheters
  supported by Expert Team 2 (\texttt{LB/AMe}) within \(5.0\text{ mm}\)
  (and \(52.0\%\) are confirmed as real catheters on paired CT), and
  \(2.7\%\) (\(4/146\)) lie within \(3.5\text{--}6.0\text{ mm}\) of an
  unmatched reference track. Only \textbf{45.2\% (\(66/146\), or
  \(1.22\) per patient)} are genuine background false alarms
  (\(>6.0\text{ mm}\) from any reference catheter), occurring along dark
  pelvic vessels or fascial intersections.
\item
  \textbf{What causes the 91 missed catheters (\(1.7\) per patient)?}
  Before Stage 2B thresholding, our \(1,960\)-track candidate pool
  actually contains \textbf{97.4\% (\(1,035/1,063\))} of all reference
  catheters within \(3.5\text{ mm}\) (and \textbf{98.8\%
  {[}\(750/759\){]}} of the long active-dwell shafts). Thus, fewer than
  a third of missed catheters (\textbf{30.8\%, \(28/91\)}) are lost
  upstream due to severe multi-slice signal dropouts. The remaining
  \textbf{69.2\% (\(63/91\))} are successfully traced by Stages 1 and 2A
  but filtered out in Stage 2B---most often because their bundle
  confidence falls just below the \(\tau = 50\%\) threshold (\(46\)
  catheters) or because they lose one-to-one matching inside a tight
  cluster of touching needles (\(15\) catheters). Lowering the threshold
  to \(\tau = 35\%\) recovers more than a quarter of these misses,
  raising whole-cohort recall to \textbf{93.6\%} and long-shaft recall
  to \textbf{94.7\%}.
\item
  \textbf{Why do some tips have errors \(> 10\text{ mm}\)?} By
  decomposing every 3D tip error vector into its component \emph{along}
  the catheter shaft (\(\Delta s_\parallel\)) and its component
  \emph{sideways} perpendicular to the shaft (\(\Delta r_\perp\)), we
  found a striking physical result: \textbf{98.8\% of total squared 3D
  tip error across all 972 matched catheters (and 99.3\% in the
  \(>10\text{ mm}\) tail) is purely longitudinal along the catheter
  track}, whereas sideways error at the tip is tiny (\(0.72\text{ mm}\)
  median). In other words, the algorithm almost never loses the
  catheter's sideways path; when a tip error exceeds \(10\text{ mm}\)
  (\(162\) catheters, \(16.7\%\)), it is almost always an axial stopping
  ambiguity caused by mid-shaft signal dropout (\(41.4\%\)), dark
  bowel/air pockets sitting directly atop the catheter tip (\(25.3\%\)),
  intrinsic human inter-rater tip ambiguity \(>6\text{ mm}\)
  (\(16.7\%\)), crowded tip arrays (\(12.3\%\)), or top-of-scan
  truncation (\(4.3\%\)).
\end{itemize}

Finally, we asked four practical questions that determine whether an
algorithm is genuinely useful in clinical radiation oncology (Table
\ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization} and Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}):

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Does geometric accuracy translate into
accurate radiation dose delivery? (Table
\ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part A; Figure
\ref{fig:clinical_gen}a)}{Does geometric accuracy translate into accurate radiation dose delivery? (Table , Part A; Figure a)}}\label{does-geometric-accuracy-translate-into-accurate-radiation-dose-delivery-table-part-a-figure-a}

Not all catheter errors matter equally to the radiation dose: a tip
error on a peripheral catheter carrying almost zero dwell time has
little effect, whereas a tip error on a heavily weighted catheter inside
the tumor shifts the target dose substantially. Indeed, across our 54
patients, \textbf{dwell-time-weighted 3D tip error}
(\(E_{\text{tip}}^{\text{dwell-w}}\)---the average 3D tip error across a
patient's catheters weighted by each catheter's share of total radiation
dwell time) correlates much more strongly with target dose error
\(|\Delta D_{90}|\) than unweighted tip error
(\(\rho = 0.863, p = 4.9 \times 10^{-17}\) vs.~\(\rho = 0.786\)).

NeedleFinder 2026 cuts dwell-weighted tip error nearly in half compared
with PMB 2019 (\(5.72\text{ mm}\) vs.~\(10.51\text{ mm}\) mean;
\(4.66\text{ mm}\) vs.~\(8.36\text{ mm}\) median), and beats Human Team
2 on the 40 dual-traced scans (\(5.18\text{ mm}\) vs.~\(6.27\text{ mm}\)
mean). Consequently: - Under \textbf{Protocol A (transferring a fixed
dwell plan)}, NeedleFinder 2026 cuts mean target dose error
\(|\Delta D_{90}|\) from \(27.6\%\) (PMB 2019) to \textbf{15.2\%}
(median \textbf{11.5\%}, \(p = 4.9 \times 10^{-6}\)) and achieves a
\textbf{76.6\% global 3D \(\gamma(2\%/2\text{ mm})\) pass rate}
(\textbf{84.3\%} at \(\gamma(3\%/3\text{ mm})\))---significantly
outperforming Human Team 2 on the 40 dual-traced scans (\(13.5\%\)
vs.~\(21.6\%\) \(|\Delta D_{90}|\), \(p = 0.028\); \(79.0\%\)
vs.~\(68.1\%\) \(\gamma_{2\%/2\text{mm}}\), \(p = 0.011\)). - Under
\textbf{Protocol B (clinical workflow: re-optimizing dwell weights on
the predicted catheters)}, dwell optimization compensates for minor
longitudinal shifts, cutting unreviewed target \(|\Delta D_{90}|\) to
\textbf{8.2\% mean / 4.2\% median} (vs.~\(37.1\% / 10.2\%\) for PMB 2019
and \(20.9\% / 4.0\%\) for Human Team 2). When the physicist reviews
just the \textbf{top 2 uncertainty-flagged tips per patient}, target
\(|\Delta D_{90}|\) drops to \textbf{5.9\% mean / 2.8\% median}
(\(78.2\%\) \(\gamma_{2\%/2\text{mm}}\) on the 40 dual-traced scans).

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Can calibrated uncertainty intervals
guide a 1-minute clinician tip check? (Table
\ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Parts B1--B2; Figure
\ref{fig:clinical_gen}b)}{Can calibrated uncertainty intervals guide a 1-minute clinician tip check? (Table , Parts B1--B2; Figure b)}}\label{can-calibrated-uncertainty-intervals-guide-a-1-minute-clinician-tip-check-table-parts-b1b2-figure-b}

the endpoint along the track (a single click taking \textasciitilde8
seconds). Our out-of-fold tip uncertainty score
\(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}\) identifies \(>10\text{ mm}\) tip outliers
with an \textbf{AUROC of 0.805}, and our nested \textbf{split-conformal
3D prediction intervals} achieve exact empirical coverage across all 54
unseen test patients despite within-patient clustering (\textbf{79.3\%}
at 80\% nominal, \textbf{84.4\%} at 85\%, \textbf{90.2\% coverage at the
90\% nominal level}, and \textbf{95.4\% at the 95\% level}, with
\(31.5\%\text{--}35.1\%\) tighter median error bars than unscaled
intervals; Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part B2).

In our simulated clinician-in-the-loop workflow where a physicist
inspects the top \(k\) highest-uncertainty tips per patient and clicks
the true distal termination (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization},
Part B1; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}b): - Reviewing \textbf{\(k = 2\)
tips per patient} (\textasciitilde16 s simulated) lowers mean tip error
from \(6.23\text{ mm}\) to \textbf{\(5.07\text{ mm}\)}
(\(1.95\text{ mm}\) median). - Reviewing \textbf{\(k = 5\) tips per
patient} (\textasciitilde40 s simulated) cuts \(>10\text{ mm}\) tip
outliers in half (\(16.7\% \to 8.0\%\)) and lowers mean tip error to
\textbf{\(3.72\text{ mm}\)} (\(1.67\text{ mm}\) median). - Reviewing
\textbf{\(k = 8\) tips per patient} (\textasciitilde64 s simulated)
brings the \textbf{95th-percentile tip error across the entire
972-catheter cohort below \(10\text{ mm}\) (\(9.90\text{ mm}\)
vs.~\(27.27\text{ mm}\) unreviewed)} and reduces mean tip error to
\textbf{\(2.77\text{ mm}\)} (\(1.51\text{ mm}\) median).

template-plane cropping (\texttt{ACSV=47}), while
\texttt{Stage\ 1\ Full\ Nested} reached \textbf{100.0\% F1 (\(14/14\),
\(0.30\text{ mm}\) shaft, \(1.08\text{ mm}\) tip)} directly under
\texttt{auto=40}. \textbf{Why does it transfer so cleanly across
organs?} When we zeroed out the Stage 0 physical symmetry channels
(\(X_{1:3}=0\)), prostate detection collapsed to \textbf{0\% (0/14)},
whereas the 1-channel \texttt{ResUNet\ +\ clDice} baseline hallucinated
11 false vascular tracks (\(56.0\%\) precision, \(71.8\%\) F1). Thus,
the Stage 0 physical symmetry representation acts as a domain-invariant
bridge between gynecologic and prostate anatomy. - \textbf{Secondary MRI
pulse sequences (243 scans):} On 3D balanced steady-state free
precession (\texttt{3D\ wbSSFP\ /\ True-FISP}), where tissue contrast
differs markedly from TSE T2W, zero-shot NeedleFinder 2026 achieved
\textbf{97.9\% recall (\(46/47\)), 88.5\% precision, and 92.9\% F1}
(\(0.72\text{ mm}\) median shaft error, \(3.40\text{ mm}\) median tip
error vs.~\(10.62\text{ mm}\) for PMB 2019). On 49 thick-slice 2D axial
T2W scans (\(\Delta z = 2.4\text{--}6.0\text{ mm}\)), Z-upsampling to
\(1.6\text{ mm}\) recovered \textbf{88.4\% (\(780/882\)) of physically
visible catheters} (\(76.9\%\) F1 vs.~\(57.6\%\) for clDice). -
\textbf{Zero-shot tracking on post-implant Pelvic CT (40 CT volumes, 38
paired patients):} Extending Stage 0 to detect both bright metallic
stylets and dark air lumens in Hounsfield units enabled zero-shot CT
reconstruction across 801 MRI-paired reference catheters, matching
\textbf{68.9\% at \(\le 3.5\text{ mm}\) and 79.2\% at
\(\le 5.0\text{ mm}\)} across non-rigid MRI-to-CT anatomical deformation

insertion and measure tissue biomechanics in 4D? (Table

In 4 patients (\texttt{Cases\ 68–71}), 23 sequential T2W scans were
acquired \emph{during} the implant procedure as needles were
progressively inserted (5 to 7 timepoints per patient, \(1.31\text{ s}\)
mean monitoring runtime per scan). Evaluating each patient's 4D series
strictly with the outer-fold model where that patient was held out in
the locked test vault (\texttt{Case\ 068} in Fold 4, \texttt{Case\ 069}
in Fold 0, \texttt{Case\ 070} in Fold 1, \texttt{Case\ 071} in Fold 2),
coupling NeedleFinder 2026 with a \textbf{causal 4D Kalman filter} that
propagates catheter states forward in time boosted \textbf{pooled
precision across the 108 catheters by +12.4 percentage points
(\(76.0\% \to 88.4\%\))}, reduced mean 3D tip error from
\(11.23\text{ to }9.75\text{ mm}\), and improved pooled F1 from
\textbf{81.6\% to 82.8\%} (reaching \textbf{93.3\% F1} on
\texttt{Case\ 070}, up from \(85.7\%\) with independent 3D tracking,
while trading single-frame recall on newly inserted final-frame needles
in \texttt{Case\ 071} for \(100.0\%\) precision).

Tracking \(212\) persisted catheter transitions across these sequential
insertions also revealed two quantitative biomechanical phenomena in
vivo (Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}d): - \textbf{Inter-catheter ``tissue
push'':} Inserting a new needle pushes surrounding soft tissue aside.
Already-implanted catheters located within \(\le 10\text{ mm}\) of a
newly inserted needle shifted sideways by a \textbf{median of
\(1.44\text{ mm}\)} (mean \(1.61\text{ mm}\), 90th percentile
\(2.81\text{ mm}\)), compared with \textbf{\(1.10\text{ mm}\)} at
\(10\text{--}20\text{ mm}\) distance and \textbf{\(1.00\text{ mm}\)}
beyond \(20\text{ mm}\) (\(p = 9.97 \times 10^{-4}\)). -
\textbf{Depth-dependent needle bending:} Deviation of a catheter from
its initial entry angle grows non-linearly with insertion depth, rising
from \(0.003\text{ mm}\) median at \(0\text{--}30\text{ mm}\) depth and
\(0.15\text{ mm}\) at \(30\text{--}60\text{ mm}\) to
\textbf{\(1.22\text{ mm}\) at \(60\text{--}90\text{ mm}\)} and
\textbf{\(3.29\text{ mm}\) median (\(4.56\text{ mm}\) mean,
\(10.49\text{ mm}\) P90) beyond \(90\text{ mm}\) depth}
(\(\rho = +0.555, p = 5.6 \times 10^{-9}\))---explaining why linear
extrapolation fails on deep pelvic implants.

\subsection{Why Combining Physics, Peak Regression, and Graph Reasoning
Works}\label{why-combining-physics-peak-regression-and-graph-reasoning-works}

Table \ref{tbl:literature} places NeedleFinder 2026 in the context of
published catheter digitization methods across CT, ultrasound, and MRI.
On CT and ultrasound, metallic stylets, air lumens, or specular
reflections create strong contrast against soft tissue, allowing voxel
segmentation networks to reach \(0.1\text{ to }0.7\text{ mm}\) shaft
errors {[}46,48,52,61,66{]}. On clinical T2W MRI without exogenous
contrast markers, a plastic catheter is a 2 mm dark proton-density and
susceptibility void imaged across 1.2 to 6.0 mm slices. Prior MRI
studies reported \(0.74\text{ to }0.97\text{ mm}\) shaft errors on
gynecologic implants using positive-contrast markers or CT-assisted
ground truth {[}27,62{]} and \(0.75\text{ to }0.93\text{ mm}\) on
prostate implants {[}43,59{]}. NeedleFinder 2026 achieves a
\textbf{median shaft error of \(0.47\text{ mm}\)} (\(0.59\text{ mm}\)
mean; \(0.45\text{ mm}\) median on active-dwell shafts) and a
\textbf{median 3D tip error of \(2.27\text{ mm}\)} directly on standard
T2W MRI across 1,063 catheters.

Our controlled ablations explain why neither voxel segmentation
{[}5,40,64{]} nor mask-based tracking {[}66{]} suffices on T2W MRI, and
why each part of our three-stage design is needed: 1. \textbf{Local
physical symmetry before deep learning:} Stage 0 eliminates one-sided
anatomical borders analytically and provides a domain-invariant
representation that allows a gynecologic network to transfer zero-shot
to prostate MRI and needle-free scans without hallucinating blood
vessels. 2. \textbf{Centerline peaks and hard-negative pre-training
instead of binary masks:} By pre-training Stage 1 on 51 external pelvic
MRI scans where real blood vessels and fascia are penalized as hard
negatives alongside synthetic catheter voids, and by predicting sharp
sub-pixel Gaussian peaks (\(P_{\text{peak}}\)) supervised by two expert
teams, touching catheters stay separate instead of merging into a single
blob. 3. \textbf{Graph message passing, fragment stitching, and
bundle-level context:} Rather than trusting local voxel connectivity,
Stage 2A enforces smooth triplet collinearity and stitches fragments
across 1-to-3-slice signal dropouts, while the Stage 2B graph
transformer judges every candidate trajectory against the geometry of
the entire 15-to-30-catheter bundle. 4. \textbf{Dedicated 1D axial tip
profiling:} Because 98.8\% of tip error is longitudinal along the shaft,
sampling a fine \(0.5\text{ mm}\) 22-channel profile along the cranial
trajectory tangent allows a 1D ResNet + self-attention localizer to
pinpoint where the catheter void terminates and to quantify its own
uncertainty.

\subsection{How NeedleFinder 2026 Changes the Clinical Workflow in 3D
Slicer}\label{how-needlefinder-2026-changes-the-clinical-workflow-in-3d-slicer}

Deployed as a one-click module in \textbf{3D Slicer} {[}82{]},
NeedleFinder 2026 runs in \textbf{4.15 s} on a workstation CPU
(\(1.26\text{ s}\) on standard-resolution scans) and \textbf{2.67 s} on
a GPU. Instead of spending 30 to 45 minutes manually clicking 15 to 30
catheters slice by slice while the patient is under anesthesia, the
medical physicist follows a structured, \textbf{under-one-minute
simulated verification workflow}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Click \emph{Detect Needles} (4 seconds):} The software crops
  the template plane automatically and reconstructs the 3D catheter
  bundle. Because our three-cornered-hat and non-inferiority analyses
  prove that the reconstructed shafts (\(0.39\text{ mm}\) intrinsic MAD
  SD vs.~\(0.35\text{ mm}\) and \(0.48\text{ mm}\) for the two human
  expert teams) are statistically non-inferior to human inter-observer
  agreement, the shafts themselves rarely need manual editing.
\item
  \textbf{Check the 2 to 8 tips with the widest conformal uncertainty
  bars (16 to 64 seconds simulated):} The interface highlights the few
  catheter tips with large split-conformal uncertainty intervals
  (\(\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}\)). Sliding the top 5 to 8 flagged
  endpoints along their already-traced shafts takes under a minute in
  simulation, drives 95th-percentile tip error below \(10\text{ mm}\)
  (\(9.90\text{ mm}\), mean \(2.77\text{ mm}\)), and keeps
  counterfactual TG-43U1 target dose error \(|\Delta D_{90}|\) to
  \textbf{2.8\% median} (\(5.9\%\) mean).
\item
  \textbf{Verify total catheter count (5 seconds simulated):} Since the
  clinician knows the exact number of needles inserted through the
  template (\(N_{\text{implant}}\)), if
  \(N_{\text{pred}} < N_{\text{implant}}\), clicking the missed catheter
  (\(0.65\) clicks per patient on average) raises 1-to-1 recall to
  \textbf{93.0\%} (\(94.2\%\) closest-match recall). Moreover, during
  progressive needle insertion in intraoperative MRI suites, the
  \(1.31\text{ s}\) causal 4D Kalman mode can alert the implanting
  physician in real time if a newly inserted needle pushes a neighboring
  catheter out of position or deflects toward an organ at risk.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and Future
Directions}\label{limitations-and-future-directions}

Four limitations of the present study point directly to future work: 1.
\textbf{Longitudinal tip ambiguity on thick slices.} While our 1D axial
tip localizer cuts mean tip error to \(6.23\text{ mm}\) and median tip
error to \(2.27\text{ mm}\) (\(58.7\%\) within \(\le 3.0\text{ mm}\)),
\(16.7\%\) of unreviewed catheters still have a longitudinal tip error
exceeding \(10\text{ mm}\) when a catheter passes through a multi-slice
signal dropout or terminates directly beneath dark bowel gas on 3 to 6
mm thick slices. Although split-conformal uncertainty triage
(\(k = 5\text{--}8\) tips reviewed) effectively contains this tail
clinically, combining T2W planning scans with thin-slice isotropic 3D
sequences (\(\le 1\text{ mm}\)) or zero-echo-time sequences {[}13,18{]}
is the natural path to eliminating unreviewed tip outliers altogether.
2. \textbf{Single-institution clinical implant cohort.} While we
demonstrated zero-shot transfer across 48 external public prostate
scans, 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI, multiple MRI pulse sequences, 4D
insertion series, and paired CT scans, all implanted brachytherapy
patients were treated at Brigham and Women's Hospital. Prospective
multi-center validation across 1.5T, 3T, and emerging low-field MRI
systems {[}85{]} from multiple scanner vendors is the essential next
step. 3. \textbf{Standardized TG-43U1 anatomical envelopes and simulated
review timings.} Because clinical DICOM RTSTRUCT and RTPLAN files were
not archived across the historical 54-patient repository, our TG-43U1
dosimetric study used standardized target and organ-at-risk envelopes
derived from the active implant geometry, and our clinician tip-review
curves used a quantitative 1-click oracle rather than prospective
stopwatch trials in the operating room. 4. \textbf{Role of synthetic
bundle pre-training (\texttt{SynthBrachy3T}).} Once Stage 1 is
pre-trained on 51 external pelvic MRI scans and paired with collinear
fragment stitching and the 1D tip localizer, \texttt{SynthBrachy3T}
provides its largest detection boost when clinical bundle labels are
scarce (\(k = 0\text{ to }5\) patients, lifting zero-shot long-shaft
\(F1_{\text{adj}}\) by \(+6.3\) points to \(83.4\%\)), while offering a
smaller refinement at the full 43-patient training budget (\(+0.4\) F1
points, while reducing dwell-weighted tip error from
\(6.65\text{ to }5.72\text{ mm}\) and counterfactual \(|\Delta D_{90}|\)
from \(12.8\%\text{ to }8.2\%\)).

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

NeedleFinder 2026 reconstructs interstitial brachytherapy catheters
directly on clinical 3T T2-weighted MRI by combining an analytical 3D
dark-core symmetry prior (Stage 0), a self-supervised and multi-rater
trained 3D residual U-Net (Stage 1), and a multi-stage graph and 1D
axial tip localization pipeline (Stage 2). Evaluated under strictly
nested \(5 \times 5\) cross-validation across 54 gynecologic
brachytherapy patients (1,063 catheters), the method achieves
\textbf{89.1\% F1} (\textbf{90.0\%--90.5\% \(F1_{\text{adj}}\)} on the
759 full-depth active-dwell shafts and \textbf{91.3\%--91.8\% F1} on the
23 patients with severe catheter adhesion), \textbf{\(0.47\text{ mm}\)
median (\(0.59\text{ mm}\) mean) in-plane shaft error}, and
\textbf{\(2.27\text{ mm}\) median (\(6.23\text{ mm}\) mean) 3D distal
tip error} in \textbf{4.15 s} on a workstation CPU (\(1.26\text{ s}\) on
standard-resolution scans) and \textbf{2.67 s} on a GPU. Across 40
doubly annotated clinical scans and 38 paired post-implant CT patients,
three-cornered-hat analysis, one-sided non-inferiority tests, and CT
adjudication demonstrate that NeedleFinder 2026 is statistically
non-inferior to human expert agreement, while its split-conformal tip
uncertainty intervals enable an under-one-minute simulated verification
workflow in 3D Slicer that preserves AAPM TG-43U1 target dosimetry
(\(2.8\%\) median \(|\Delta D_{90}|\)) and generalizes zero-shot to
prostate MRI, secondary MRI sequences, 4D intra-procedural insertion
monitoring, and pelvic CT.
