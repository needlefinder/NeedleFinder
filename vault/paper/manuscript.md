Tracing them by hand takes tens of minutes while the patient is under anesthesia. Automatic methods based on voxel segmentation break catheters into fragments and mistake blood vessels for catheters. We set out to make catheter reconstruction automatic, accurate to below a millimeter, and fast enough to run during the procedure.

# Plain-Language Summary {-}

Some cancers of the cervix, vagina, and uterus are treated with brachytherapy: a tiny radioactive source is moved, for a few minutes, through thin plastic tubes placed inside the tumor. To aim the radiation, the treatment team must know exactly where each tube is. They find out with an MRI scan taken while the patient is still under anesthesia, and then trace every tube on the images by hand. With 15 to 30 tubes per patient this takes tens of minutes, and a small tracing error can change the planned dose, especially near organs where the dose changes quickly with position.

On an MRI scan a tube does not glow. It is a small dark dot, a few pixels wide, on each image slice. Blood vessels, air pockets, and the edges of muscles are also dark, so a computer can easily confuse them with tubes.

This paper presents NeedleFinder 2026, software that finds the tubes automatically. It works in three steps. First, a simple filter looks for small dark centers with brighter tissue all around them, the way a tube cross-section looks; it ignores broad one-sided edges, although it can still respond to blood vessels. Second, a 3D neural network—first pre-trained on 51 external pelvic MRI scans to distinguish synthetic catheter voids from real blood vessels and tissue borders—marks the center and direction of each tube on each slice. Third, two "graph" networks connect the marks from slice to slice into 3D curves, stitch fragments separated by single-slice dropouts, pull apart tubes that touch, score the whole bundle together to reject false curves, and pinpoint each tube's deepest tip using a dedicated 1D axial profile network that also reports an uncertainty interval for each tip.

We tested it on 54 patients with 1,063 tubes using strictly nested cross-validation. It found 91.4 percent of all tubes (and up to 94.7 percent of the 759 long interstitial shafts at a recall-oriented threshold), and 86.9 percent of the tubes it reported matched an expert trace (89.1 percent F1). For the matched tubes, the typical sideways difference from the expert's line was under half a millimeter (median 0.47 mm, mean 0.59 mm), and the deepest end of each tube had a median 3D difference of 2.27 mm (mean 6.23 mm), passing formal statistical non-inferiority compared with the disagreement between two independent human expert teams (median 0.49 mm on shafts and 3.24 mm on tube ends). Checking against post-implant CT scans of the 38 paired patients showed that over half (52 of 100) of the "extra" MRI tracks unmatched to the primary human team were real physical tubes present on CT. Simulating radiation delivery and a quick check of the 2 to 5 most uncertain tube ends keeps the tumor radiation dose shift to 2.8 percent median (5.9 percent mean) of the reference plan and takes under a minute of simulated review. The software also worked without retraining on prostate brachytherapy MRI (finding 13 to 14 of 14 tubes with zero false alarms) and on live scans taken while needles were being pushed in, running in 4.15 seconds on average on a 24-core workstation CPU (1.26 seconds on standard scans) and 2.67 seconds on a graphics card.

# Introduction

## The Clinical Problem

Brachytherapy delivers radiation from inside the body. For locally advanced cervical cancer it is the cornerstone of curative radiotherapy [@potter2018; @potter2021]. The EMBRACE-I study reported 92% local control at five years with a median dose of 90 Gy (EQD2) to the high-risk target volume [@potter2021], and EMBRACE-II now prescribes planning aims of 90 to 95 Gy while sparing the bladder, rectum, and sigmoid colon [@potter2018]. Reaching such doses in large or irregular tumors requires *interstitial* implants: in addition to a central tandem and a vaginal obturator or ring, 10 to 30 thin plastic catheters or needles are inserted into the tumor through a perineal template or freehand [@mastmeyer2017; @zaffino2019; @chino2025].

The tumor itself is best seen on T2-weighted (T2W) MRI, which GEC-ESTRO and consensus recommendations designate as the reference for target delineation and set reconstruction requirements for [@dimopoulos2012; @jacobsen2022]. MRI-based applicator reconstruction with marker-filled catheters was established for cervical cancer in 2009, with inter-observer standard deviations below 2 mm [@haack2009]. Historically, clinics took both an MRI and a CT scan and registered the two, which adds its own uncertainty to the reconstruction [@hellebust2010], and practical guidance for hybrid implants still advises a CT in addition to the MRI because plastic needles are hard to see on MRI [@ladbury2023]. The AAPM Task Group 303 report describes how to implement MRI in HDR brachytherapy from simulation to treatment [@prisciandaro2022], the Medical Physics Practice Guideline 13.a lists MRI among the accepted planning modalities [@richardson2025], and MRI-only interstitial workflows have been shown to be feasible [@shaaer2020; @lee2023]. Positive-contrast line markers make the catheter lumen visible on T1-weighted or balanced sequences [@ning2020; @shaaer2020] and are listed as required equipment in published implementation guides [@zhangh2019], and zero-echo-time sequences show empty catheters directly [@kaza2022; @lee2023], but both need an additional series fused to the T2W images. Identifying the source path on the T2W series alone remains a recognized challenge [@richart2018]; for rigid applicators, direct reconstruction on T2W MRI is in fact more precise than library-based or fused workflows [@malajovich2020], but interstitial needles have no library model.

Finding the catheters on MRI is the central technical challenge of an MRI-only workflow [@zaffino2019; @sargazi2026]. The dose from the $^{192}$Ir source falls steeply with distance: organ-at-risk dose parameters change by up to 5 to 6% per millimeter of applicator displacement [@tanderup2008], applicator shifts beyond 1.5 mm can change the rectal D2cc by more than 10% [@schindel2013], and in MRI-only cervical implants a systematic 1 mm error along the source path already changes organ-at-risk doses significantly [@singh2026]. Swapping two catheters during digitization changes prostate, urethra, and rectum dose metrics by 5 to 20% [@nunez2021], and needle reconstruction uncertainty alone shifts the urethral dose by about 3 Gy in prostate implants [@rylander2017]. Manual reconstruction is itself error-prone: with electromagnetic tracking as the reference, 23% of 265 needle reconstructions in ultrasound-guided prostate implants deviated by 2 mm or more [@androulakis2025]. Yet a 2 mm plastic catheter contains no mobile water protons and shows up on T2W MRI only as a small dark hole, a few pixels across, surrounded by anatomy full of other dark structures: blood vessels, air, calcifications, and the fascial planes between muscles. Tracing 15 to 30 catheters slice by slice therefore takes tens of minutes, 46 minutes on average in one MRI-only gynecologic series [@shaaer2022], within a template-based implant procedure of about 100 minutes under anesthesia [@roumeliotis2026].

## Key Terms

Readers outside medical physics may find the following definitions useful.

- **Catheter, needle, shaft, tip.** We use *catheter* and *needle* interchangeably for the thin plastic tubes. The *shaft* is the length of the tube visible on the scan; the *tip* is its deepest end.
- **Digitization or reconstruction.** Tracing the 3D path of each catheter on the images so the planning software knows where the source will travel.
- **Slice, voxel, in-plane.** An MRI volume is a stack of 2D images (slices). Each 3D picture element is a voxel. *In-plane* means within one slice; here the in-plane pixel is 0.31 to 0.88 mm while slices are 1.2 to 6.0 mm thick.
- **Recall, precision, F1.** Recall is the fraction of true catheters the method found. Precision is the fraction of reported catheters that were real. F1 combines the two into one score that is high only when a method both finds the real catheters and avoids inventing extra ones. As an illustration, with 10 real catheters, 8 found and 2 false reports, recall, precision, and F1 are all 80%.
- **Shaft error and tip error.** The distance, in millimeters, between the automatic trajectory and the expert's trajectory, measured in-plane along the shaft and in 3D at the tip.
- **Nested cross-validation, out-of-fold.** The 54 patients were split into 5 outer groups ($43\text{--}44$ training vs. $10\text{--}11$ test patients per fold). Within each outer fold, early stopping, temperature calibration, split-conformal quantile calibration, and optionally inner-OOF upstream feature generation for downstream stackers use a strictly disjoint 80/20 inner split or 5-fold inner cross-fitting, so no test patient's images or labels influence training, checkpoint selection, or threshold calibration.
- **Inter-observer variability.** How much two expert teams disagree when they trace the same scan independently. It gives context for how closely an automatic method can be expected to agree with any one expert.

## Previous Work

Automatic catheter digitization has gone through three generations. Two recent systematic reviews cover the field [@sargazi2026; @vijayan2026].

**Model-based tracking (2013 to 2019).** The BWH program of 3T MR-guided gynecologic brachytherapy in the AMIGO suite, which produced the cohort used here, was described in 2012 together with the first image-analysis tools for catheter identification [@kapur2012] and open-source 3D Slicer modules for template and obturator registration [@chen2014]. Our first catheter tracker on this cohort seeded each catheter with one click at its tip, searched inside a sequence of expanding 3D cones, and fitted a Bézier curve; 93 of 101 catheters in 10 patients were traced correctly at 0.3 s per catheter [@pernelle2013]. Mastmeyer *et al.* [@mastmeyer2017] constrained the cone search with a mechanical rod-and-spring catheter model, reaching 1.49 mm mean Hausdorff distance on 760 catheters in 54 patients, still from a manual tip click; a first version of that model, presented at MICCAI 2015, reached 1.40 mm mean error on the 101 catheters of the 2013 set [@mastmeyer2017a]. Template-geometry-assisted reconstruction on MRI has been described for commercial interstitial templates [@richart2015], and on ultrasound 3D Hough transforms localized single needles with about 1 mm endpoint accuracy in vivo [@qiu2013]. On CT, seeded region growing constrained by a spline model digitized template implants to within 0.4 mm of manual digitization [@dise2015], and thresholding followed by density-based clustering reaches 0.3 mm for rigid applicators [@deufel2020], but MRI offers no such intensity threshold. Related model-to-image registration reconstructs rigid applicators on MRI within inter-observer variability [@hrinivich2019], and line fitting by RANSAC digitizes straight needles on CT [@zheng2021]. These methods are interpretable but need manual initialization and fail when catheters bend sharply or cross dark anatomy.

**Voxel segmentation with heuristic post-processing (2019 to 2026).** To remove manual initialization, Zaffino *et al.* [@zaffino2019] trained an ensemble of 3D U-Nets to segment catheter voxels on 50 clinical T2W scans with 826 catheters, then clustered the voxels with DBSCAN and removed small islands. It reached a voxel Dice of 0.60, a mean distance error of 2.0 mm, 6.7% false-positive and 1.5% false-negative clusters, in 2 to 9 minutes per patient. Convolutional segmentation, often nnU-Net based [@isensee2021], has since been applied to CT brachytherapy of the cervix, prostate, and breast [@jung2019; @jung2019b; @zhangd2020; @hu2021; @weishaupt2022; @xie2023; @wang2024; @moradi2025; @quetin2025], to MRI-guided prostate implants [@dai2020; @aleong2024; @kostoulas2025], to single needles on interventional MRI [@li2020; @zhou2024], to transrectal ultrasound [@zhang2020tmi; @zhang2020pmb; @zhang2020mp; @andersen2020; @wangf2020; @liu2022; @goulet2025], and to gynecologic MRI with line markers [@shaaer2022] or with applicator mesh alignment [@kim2026]. Even with topology-preserving losses, from the Betti-number loss [@hu2019] to clDice [@shit2021] and skeleton recall [@kirchhoff2024], pure voxel segmentation has two failure modes on T2W MRI: a one-slice signal dropout splits one catheter into two to four fragments, and dark vessels and tendons are segmented as short false catheters. The ultrasound literature documents the same pattern: accuracy collapses on artifact-laden catheters [@liu2022] and tips are much harder than shafts [@wangf2020]. Keypoint heatmap regression, the choice made for our Stage 1, has recently been shown to localize an interventional needle on MRI more accurately than mask segmentation of the same images [@zhouw2026]. Electromagnetic or active MR tracking avoids the imaging problem altogether, at the cost of dedicated hardware and sequences in the operating room [@mehrtash2014; @wang2015; @dearcos2017; @durrbeck2022; @cantin2024; @deufel2024; @beaulieu2025; @roumeliotis2026]; crossed catheter paths were already identified as a common failure of image-based digitization in the BWH program when active tracking was introduced [@wang2015].

**Structure-aware tracking and graph networks (2022 to 2026).** On CT, Xiao *et al.* [@xiao2026] identified two further problems: *catheter adhesion*, where shafts touch or overlap on consecutive slices so that greedy trackers swap or merge them, and the scarcity of annotated clinical data. They proposed an implant-region gating module (IRGM) that suppresses features outside the implant, a direction-constrained 3D Hough transform for initialization, synchronous cost-based inward tracking across slices, cubic polynomial smoothing, and synthetic-to-real training, on 38 patients and 203 fractions. Their companion work handles needle adhesion in dense CT seed implants by detecting tips and handles [@xiao2026jbhi]. Outside brachytherapy, message-passing networks have replaced greedy linking in tracking-by-detection [@braso2020; @benhaim2022], transformers predict graphs of curvilinear structures directly from images [@shit2022], and synthetic-to-real training has been shown to transfer to real needle detection [@arapi2023]. Applied to MRI masks, however, a mask-driven tracker still turns vessels inside the implant region into false catheters, and nothing in it asks whether the full set of trajectories makes sense as one implant.

**Open data.** No public needle or catheter dataset for brachytherapy exists [@fechter2024], and the AI literature in brachytherapy remains largely retrospective and single-center [@vijayan2026]. The public pelvic MRI scans we use for self-supervised pretraining and the false-alarm test come from the Medical Segmentation Decathlon [@antonelli2022].

## Contributions

This paper combines the strengths of all three generations on the full 54-patient, 1,063-catheter BWH cohort, to our knowledge the largest annotated MRI cohort for interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy.

1. **A three-stage architecture with five methodological upgrades.** An analytical dark-core symmetry prior and 100% reference-free axial template locator (Stage 0); a 3D residual U-Net pre-trained on 51 external pelvic T2W MRI volumes with physics-guided synthetic susceptibility tubes and anatomical hard negatives, then fine-tuned with dual-observer multi-rater consensus supervision (Stage 1); and two graph networks plus a dedicated 1D axial tip localizer (Stage 2): native seed tracking with a triplet message-passing network, adhesion-aware duplicate merging, and collinear split-fragment stitching, followed by a tip-aware bundle graph transformer and a 91-step dilated 1D ResNet + self-attention axial tip localizer (`Dedicated1DTipLocalizer`).
2. **A strictly nested $5 \times 5$ comparison and reference-free observer adjudication.** We re-implemented three published approaches and evaluated all methods under strictly nested patient-level 5-fold cross-validation (plus $5 \times 5$ inner out-of-fold Stage 1 cross-fitting), and adjudicated observer agreement across all 40 doubly traced scans using Grubbs' three-cornered-hat intrinsic error estimator, formal one-sided non-inferiority tests, and independent post-implant Pelvic CT scans.
3. **Dosimetric translation, conformal uncertainty triage, and cross-domain generalization.** We quantify downstream AAPM TG-43U1 HDR $^{192}\text{Ir}$ dosimetric impact ($\Delta D_{90}$, OAR $\Delta D_{2\text{cc}}$, 3D $\gamma$), calibrate strictly nested split-conformal 3D tip prediction intervals and simulated clinician review-budget curves, and evaluate zero-shot generalization across 48 needle-free MSD prostate scans, 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI, multi-sequence MRI (`3D wbSSFP`, `2D AX TSE T2`), 4D serial insertion scans (measuring inter-catheter tissue push and depth-dependent bending), and 40 post-implant pelvic CT volumes (38 paired with benchmark MRI patients).

## Stage 0: Analytical Dark-Core Symmetry Prior and Reference-Free Cropping

The physical idea is simple. On an axial slice a catheter cross-section is a dark core that is surrounded by brighter tissue in *every* direction, whereas a fascial plane or a muscle border is dark on one side only. Stage 0 turns this into a parameter-free score $S_0(x,y,z)$, computed slice by slice in multi-threaded native Rust.

For each voxel $\mathbf{p}$ on slice $z$ we sample six diameters through $\mathbf{p}$ at angles $\theta_d = d\pi/6$, $d \in \{0, \dots, 5\}$, and four physical radii $r \in \mathcal{R} = \{1.35, 1.85, 2.5, 3.3\}$ mm, converting each radius to pixels with the in-plane spacing. For diameter $d$ and radius $r$ the darker of the two ring samples is kept,
$$m_{d,r} = \min\!\big(I(\mathbf{p} + r\mathbf{u}_d),\; I(\mathbf{p} - r\mathbf{u}_d)\big),$$
so that a one-sided edge, where one end of the diameter stays dark, produces a small $m_{d,r}$. The six values are sorted, $m_{(1),r} \le \dots \le m_{(6),r}$, and the ring brightness is a robust weighted order statistic that still tolerates one dark neighbor,
$$S_{\text{ring}}(\mathbf{p}) = \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \big[0.45\, m_{(2),r} + 0.55\, m_{(3),r}\big].$$
The response $S_0$ grows with the positive contrast between this ring brightness and the $3 \times 3$ smoothed center intensity and with the relative darkness of the core, and is normalized per volume. Candidate centers are the local maxima of $S_0$ after non-maximum suppression.

Before running Stage 1, a **100% reference-free, tilt-safe axial template locator** (`k_lim_auto`) identifies the caudal entry slice directly from the 3D intensity volume and $S_0(x,y,z)$: it detects the caudal-to-cranial transition from the air/template saturation drop (`p90` intensity transition) and the emergence of clustered dark-core candidates within the central field of view, setting the lower axial crop bound with a mean signed difference of $-1.91$ slices (median $-2.0$, IQR $1.0$ slice; mean $58.9$ active axial slices vs. $57.2$ under `.acsv`) relative to clinical scene annotations while never consulting reference trajectories. Placing the crop boundary ~2 slices deeper caudally prevents tilted perineal templates from truncating in-FOV catheter bases ($0/1,063$ truncated in-FOV bases under `k_lim_auto` vs. $42/1,063$ under `.acsv`), preserving $100.0\%$ of in-FOV catheter depth and $99.65\%$ of total unclipped VTK $z$-span. Stage 0 takes 0.27 s per full volume on average on a 24-core workstation CPU (0.07 s on standard $320 \times 256$ scans and 0.38 s on high-resolution $640 \times 416$ / $768 \times 768$ scans).

## Stage 1: Self-Supervised and Multi-Rater Multi-Head 3D U-Net

Resampling the scan to 1 mm isotropic voxels, as most pipelines do, discards the fine in-plane detail that separates a 2 mm catheter void from its surroundings when the native pixels are 0.3 to 0.9 mm. Working on the native grid instead, a standard $3 \times 3 \times 3$ convolution mixes information over up to ten times more distance along $z$ than in-plane. Stage 1 (`NeedleUNet3D`, Figure \ref{fig:arch}a) therefore operates on the native grid, uses $3 \times 3 \times 3$ residual blocks with squeeze-and-excitation attention at four scales (24, 48, 96, and 144 channels), pools only in-plane at the first two scales so that the thick slice axis is not downsampled early, and adds a dilated convolution at the deepest scale. Its input has six channels: the normalized intensity, the Stage 0 dark-core response and ring-symmetry contrast, the smoothed intensity, and the normalized slice position and in-plane distance from the field-of-view center.

The network predicts four outputs:

1. **Tubular mask** $P_{\text{mask}}$: focal binary cross-entropy, weighted 19-fold on catheter voxels and up-weighted on bright Stage 0 responses that are not catheters, plus soft Dice on the tubular label around each reference trajectory.
2. **Centerline peak** $P_{\text{peak}}$: a sharp 2D Gaussian ($\sigma = 0.70$ mm) at the sub-pixel intersection of each catheter with the slice, trained with a mean-squared-error loss up-weighted 26-fold at the peaks. Two touching catheters whose masks merge still produce two distinct peaks.
3. **Direction field** $(V_x, V_y) = (\Delta x/\Delta z, \Delta y/\Delta z)$: the local slope of the trajectory, trained with a smooth $L_1$ loss inside the mask, with an additional term that penalizes slice-to-slice inconsistency of the predicted direction.
4. **Obturator channel** $P_{\text{obt}}$: a dedicated segmentation of the large central obturator and tandem, so that its lumen and rim do not spawn false needles.

To improve feature discrimination on dark pelvic anatomy without leaking test-patient information, `NeedleUNet3D` undergoes two training phases:

- **External pelvic MRI pre-training with synthetic susceptibility tubes and anatomical hard negatives.** The full 3D U-Net (encoder, decoder, and task heads) is pre-trained for 45 epochs across the 51 external pelvic T2W MRI volumes from 50 distinct subjects (48 public `MSD Task05_Prostate` volumes + 3 non-benchmark BWH pelvic T2W volumes, zero patient overlap with the 54 benchmark cases). During each iteration, physics-guided 3D cubic-spline susceptibility tubes (dark core with realistic T2W partial-volume rim, dropout pockets, and variable curvature) are injected into the needle-free pelvic volumes alongside 3D masked volume patches. The network minimizes a joint loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSL}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{tube}} + 0.30\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}}$, where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{tube}}$ supervises tubular mask, peak, and 3D direction regression on the synthetic tubes while penalizing non-implanted high-symmetry pelvic structures ($S_0 > 0.25$: vessels, fascia, obturator internus borders) as explicit anatomical hard negatives, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MAE}}$ reconstructs masked 3D tissue patches (reaching $0.867$ validation Dice on synthetic tubes).
- **Multi-rater consensus supervision.** On the 40 dual-annotated training scans (`CY+HT` and `LB/AMe`) within each outer training fold ($n=43\text{--}44$ patients), consensus-matched trajectories ($n=465$ pairs within $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$) are supervised along their inverse-variance precision-weighted centerline (weight $1.15$), primary-only (`CY`) trajectories at weight $1.00$, secondary-only (`LB/AMe`) trajectories at soft weight $0.55$, and secondary cranial tip slices with a soft axial Gaussian tip kernel.

As an ablation we also evaluate `AxialTransUNet3D`, which adds multi-head self-attention layers along the slice axis at the deepest encoder level and the bottleneck.

## Stage 2: From Slice Detections to a Coherent 3D Bundle

Stage 2 answers three questions: which detections on neighboring slices belong to the same catheter, which of the resulting 3D curves are real, and exactly where each catheter terminates along the slice axis.

### Stage 2A: Native Seed Tracking, Triplet Message Passing, and Collinear Stitching

On each slice, local maxima of $P_{\text{peak}}$ and of $S_0$ become nodes with a 16-dimensional feature vector (normalized position, $P_{\text{mask}}$, $P_{\text{peak}}$, $S_0$, local $3 \times 3$ intensity statistics, predicted slope, and obturator proximity). Directed edges connect nodes across 1 to $\max(4, 24\text{ mm}/\Delta z)$ slices. An edge is kept when its chord slope is at most 0.56 and its in-plane displacement lies within $2.8 + 0.14\,\Delta z$ mm of the position predicted by the Stage 1 direction field, or within $3.5 + 0.22\,\Delta z$ mm outright. Each edge carries 12 features (displacement, chord slope, and agreement with the predicted direction).

Two consecutive links can each look plausible on their own yet form an abrupt bend when considered together; the network therefore scores each link using information from the links entering and leaving its two nodes. `NeedleSliceMPNN` is a 4-layer triplet-collinearity message-passing network (hidden size 64). Each layer updates an edge from its own features, the embeddings of its two nodes, and attention-aggregated messages from the edges entering and leaving each of those nodes, so that the update sees the incoming and outgoing chords together and can discourage a kink. The network outputs refined sub-pixel offsets $(\delta x_i, \delta y_i)$ and node and edge probabilities.

Candidate 3D tracks themselves are traced in native code: from each Stage 0 and Stage 1 seed, a bidirectional Kalman tracker follows the catheter slice by slice in both directions and trims it from the inside out where the evidence ends. Tracks that run within 1.65 to 2.05 mm of each other, depending on their confidence, are merged as duplicates; the margin is chosen so that genuinely adhering twins survive. Next, **collinear axial split-fragment stitching and extrapolation-aware deduplication** reconnects trajectories broken across 1 to 3 slices by local signal dropouts or vessel crossings: pairs of non-overlapping or slightly overlapping track fragments (axial gap $\le 20\text{ mm}$, overlap $\le 6\text{ mm}$) whose linearly extrapolated positions at their shared axial midpoint agree within $\le 2.2\text{ mm}$ with a 3D slope-vector difference $\le 0.14$ are merged into a single full-length trajectory ($102$ stitched trajectories across the 54 cases), while split fragments that lie within $\le 2.1\text{ mm}$ along $\ge 72\%$ of a longer high-confidence shaft are absorbed. The surviving tracks are passed to Stage 2B.

### Stage 2B: Bundle Graph Transformer, Dedicated 1D Axial Tip Localizer, and Synthetic Pre-Training

After Stage 1B, recall is 94.2% (97.0% on long shafts), but each patient still carries 11.6 false tracks on average ($626$ across 54 patients) along vessels and obturator edges; Stage 2A reduces false tracks by 43% to 6.6 per patient ($359$ total, lifting precision from 61.5% to 73.5% and F1 from 74.4% to 82.5%). Real catheters have a property these false tracks lack: they form a *bundle*. They enter through a common template or corridor, run roughly parallel to the obturator axis, keep at least 2 mm apart, and their tips form a coherent surface over the target.

Each candidate trajectory is summarized by a list of 26 measurements, its *token* (length, linear-fit residual, 75th-percentile $S_0$ and $P_{\text{mask}}$, $P_{\text{peak}}$, $P_{\text{obt}}$, the three message-passing probabilities, agreement between models, the deviation of its entry position and slope from the bundle median, and the contrast gradient at its cranial end), together with 8 pairwise features between trajectories (minimum 3D clearance, mean in-plane separation, angular divergence, $z$-overlap, and duplicate indicators). The `TipAwareBundleGraphTransformer` applies two edge-biased attention layers over neighboring trajectories and one global self-attention layer over the whole bundle. It outputs three logits (a recall-oriented score, a strict one-to-one score, and a long-shaft one-to-one score), combined into a confidence $p_m$, alongside an initial bundle-context tip offset. An implant-region gate inspired by the IRGM of Xiao *et al.* [@xiao2026] is applied geometrically: the implant center is the median in-plane position of candidates with confidence $\ge 55\%$, the implant radius is the 98th percentile of their distances plus 16 mm, and candidates outside this radius require 22 points higher confidence.

Because a single scalar offset from bundle summary statistics cannot resolve fine sub-slice signal drop-offs or air-pocket artifacts at the cranial end of a catheter, Stage 2B incorporates a **Dedicated 1D Axial Tip Localizer** (`Dedicated1DTipLocalizer`, Figure \ref{fig:arch}b). Along the cranial-most trajectory tangent of each candidate, the localizer samples a **91-step axial profile** over $[-25.0, +20.0]\text{ mm}$ at $0.5\text{ mm}$ sub-slice intervals. At every step it extracts **22 multi-scale tubular, intensity, and anatomical features**: center and $3 \times 3$ / $5 \times 5$ ring T2W intensities, local annular contrast ($r=3\text{ vs. }1$, $r=5\text{ vs. }1$), first- and second-order axial gradients ($\partial_z$, $\partial_{zz}$), Stage 0 $S_0$ center/max/drop, Stage 1 $P_{\text{mask}}$ and $P_{\text{peak}}$ center/max/drop, obturator probability $P_{\text{obt}}$, normalized axial coordinate, step indicators, and bundle-relative $z$-depth. A **3-block dilated 1D ResNet** (hidden width 48, kernel size 5, dilations $1, 2, 4$) followed by **4-head 1D Self-Attention** processes the 91-step sequence and drives a dual prediction head: a **91-bin soft-argmax termination probability distribution** combined with a **Smooth-$L_1$ continuous residual offset head**, supervised with Gaussian target distributions ($\sigma = 1.25\text{ mm}$), focal peak classification, and huberized $L_1$ displacement loss. Each accepted trajectory ($p_m \ge \tau$, default $\tau = 50\%$) has its cranial tip updated by the 1D localizer and is smoothed with a cubic polynomial fit in $z$ (Poly3).

To study zero-shot and low-label bundle scoring, we also built **SynthBrachy3T**, a generator of 300 synthetic candidate-trajectory bundles with the same 26 node and 8 edge features as real candidates (12 to 23 true long shafts per bundle, 30% with an adhering twin 1.65 to 2.75 mm away, 2 to 8 short obturator tracks, 3 to 11 split duplicates, and 4 to 14 vessel-like false tracks). Stage 2B can be pre-trained on SynthBrachy3T and then fine-tuned purely on the clinical training bundles of the current outer fold.

# Experimental Setup

## Strictly Nested Cross-Validation Protocol and Baselines

Every learned component, every ablation, and every baseline is trained and evaluated under a **strictly nested $5 \times 5$ patient-level out-of-fold (OOF) protocol** across the 54 patients, eliminating three subtle sources of optimistic bias present in standard stacked evaluations:

1. **Disjoint inner validation for early stopping and calibration.** For each outer fold $f \in \{0, \dots, 4\}$ ($43\text{--}44$ outer-train patients, $10\text{--}11$ outer-test patients), outer-train patients are partitioned deterministically into an 80% inner-train subset ($34\text{--}35$ patients) and a 20% inner-validation subset ($9$ patients). Stage 1 U-Net checkpoint selection, Stage 2A MPNN and Stage 2B Graph Transformer early stopping, `Dedicated1DTipLocalizer` model selection, and confidence temperature scaling are performed exclusively on the $9$ inner-validation patients; the outer-test fold is touched exactly once at final evaluation.
2. **100% reference-free axial cropping (`k_lim_auto`).** Every outer-test volume is cropped using the automatic `k_lim_auto` template-plane locator derived solely from the raw 3D MRI and Stage 0 $S_0$ volume, with zero fallback to clinical scene files or ground-truth trajectory coordinates.
3. **Inner-OOF cross-fitting of Stage 1 features (`4c`).** In a standard 2-stage pipeline (`4a`, `4b`), Stage 2 is trained on outer-train patients using Stage 1 probability maps produced by the outer-fold Stage 1 model (which trained on those same outer-train patients' images). To verify that this causes zero stacking optimism, we also trained $5 \times 5 = 25$ inner-fold Stage 1 `NeedleUNet3D` models so that every outer-train patient receives strictly out-of-fold Stage 1 probability maps when training Stage 2A, Stage 2B, and `Dedicated1DTipLocalizer` (configuration `4c` in Table \ref{tbl:main}).

We re-implemented three published approaches on the identical outer folds and `k_lim_auto` bounds:

- **PMB 2019: 3D U-Net + DBSCAN** [@zaffino2019]. Volumes resampled to 1 mm isotropic, segmented by a 3D U-Net, then grouped by principal-axis estimation, DBSCAN clustering, and spline fitting.
- **ResUNet + clDice + skeleton** [@isensee2021; @shit2021]. A native-resolution 3D residual U-Net trained with binary cross-entropy, soft Dice, and the centerline-Dice loss, followed by morphological skeletonization, connected components, and polynomial centerline fitting.
- **Xiao 2026 Sync-Track** [@xiao2026]. Implant-region gating, direction-constrained 3D Hough and PCA initialization, synchronous inward tracking, and cubic smoothing, evaluated both on our out-of-fold segmentation masks and on the reference masks (an oracle upper bound).

## Metrics and Statistics

Predicted and reference trajectories are matched one-to-one with the Hungarian algorithm (SciPy `linear_sum_assignment`): pairs whose mean in-plane distance over the shared slices exceeds 3.5 mm are forbidden before solving, so the assignment first maximizes the number of valid pairs and then minimizes their distance. Error distributions are computed over matched pairs; a patient with no matched pair contributes fixed penalty values (3.5 mm shaft, 10 mm curve, 20 mm tip and HD95) to the per-patient arrays used in the statistical tests. We report:

1. **Detection**: recall, precision, and F1 under strict one-to-one matching (plus obturator-adjusted precision $P_{\text{adj}}$ and $F1_{\text{adj}}$ when evaluating the 759 long active-dwell shafts alone, so predictions that match a valid secondary short/obturator reference track are not counted as false positives).
2. **Shaft error** $E_{\text{shaft}}$: the in-plane distance between the two curves at 40 evenly spaced positions along their shared length, summarized by mean, standard deviation, median, interquartile range, and 95th percentile.
3. **3D curve distance** $D_{\text{avg}}$ and **95th-percentile Hausdorff distance** $\text{HD}_{95}$, computed symmetrically on 50 resampled points per curve.
4. **Tip error** $E_{\text{tip}}$: the 3D Euclidean distance between the predicted and reference deepest (cranial) tip.

**Calibration and reason-coded decisions.** Every candidate track in the stitched Stage 2B pool ($1,960$ candidates across the 54 patients, $36.3$ per patient: $1,356$ true within $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ and $604$ false) is also treated as a binary prediction: it is a real catheter if its mean in-plane distance to some reference trajectory over their shared slices is at most 3.5 mm, and its predicted probability is the Stage 2B confidence $p_m$. Over all candidates we report a reliability diagram, the expected calibration error (ECE, ten equal-width bins), and the Brier score; temperature scaling is fitted on the inner-validation split of each fold and applied to the outer-test fold. Each decision of the decoder carries a typed reason code (kept, below threshold, duplicate of a kept track, outside the implant region, low dark-core symmetry), and for every discarded candidate that is a real catheter we record whether that catheter is already covered by a kept track (redundant) or lost.

Following GEC-ESTRO and AAPM guidance on reporting brachytherapy uncertainties [@kirisits2014], reconstruction discrepancies are reported as distributions (median, interquartile range, and 95th percentile) and separated from observer variation. Differences between methods are tested over the 54 patients with paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and 95% confidence intervals come from 1,000 patient-level bootstrap resamples.

## Computing Environment, Software, and Code

Training and the nested $5 \times 5$ benchmarks ran on a Linux workstation (Arch Linux) with a 24-core AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO 7965WX (48 logical threads), 256 GB of RAM, and two NVIDIA GPUs (GeForce RTX 4090, 24 GB, and RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, 16 GB) using Python 3.12/3.13, PyTorch 2.4/2.11, SciPy 1.16, NumPy 2.4, and ONNX Runtime 1.23. The Stage 0 kernel is written in Rust (crate `needlefinder_rust`, multi-threaded with the standard library, exposed to Python through a C interface and `ctypes`, compiled in release mode with link-time optimization). For clinical deployment, the trained Stage 1, Stage 2B, and 1D tip localizer networks are exported to ONNX and run with ONNX Runtime inside an extension for 3D Slicer 5.10 [@fedorov2012]; no GPU is required.

The 3D Slicer extension, the Rust kernel, and the exported models are available at https://github.com/needlefinder/NeedleFinder. All training, nested cross-validation, statistical analysis, SynthBrachy3T, and figure/table generation scripts are archived in `vault/paper/benchmarks`.

## Agreement With Experts Compared With Agreement Between Experts

\input{tables/tab3_interobserver.tex}

Because a catheter is only a few pixels wide and slices are thick, two experts clicking centerlines never agree perfectly; inter-observer discrepancies of about 0.6 mm have been reported for breast implants, and more than a third of prostate needles differ by over 1 mm between observers [@kallis2019; @karius2024], and algorithms within that range are considered clinically comparable to a second observer [@hrinivich2019; @andersen2020]. Table \ref{tbl:interobserver} measures this across **all 40 doubly traced clinical scenes** (`CY+HT`, 723 trajectories = 505 `CY` long + 218 `HT` short/obturator; `LB/AMe`, 513 trajectories, of which 460 are long) and compares it with `NeedleFinder 2026` evaluated out-of-fold on the exact same 40 scenes.

Comparing the two human teams on long shafts (`LB/AMe` 460 long vs. `CY` 505 long), 424 pairs match one-to-one within $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ (**84.0% recall**, **92.2% strict precision** [$93.6\%$ obturator-adjusted], **87.9% strict F1** [$88.5\%$ obturator-adjusted]), with a shaft discrepancy of **$0.70 \pm 0.58\text{ mm}$ mean** (**$0.49\text{ mm}$ median**, $0.50\text{ mm}$ IQR, **$1.90\text{ mm}$ P95**) and a 3D tip discrepancy of **$6.28 \pm 7.29\text{ mm}$ mean** (**$3.24\text{ mm}$ median**, $6.02\text{ mm}$ IQR, $21.88\text{ mm}$ P95). Across all 723 `CY+HT` reference trajectories, `LB/AMe` matches 476 pairs ($65.8\%$ recall, $92.8\%$ precision, $77.0\%$ F1; $0.73 \pm 0.61\text{ mm}$ mean / $0.50\text{ mm}$ median shaft discrepancy, $6.30 \pm 8.02\text{ mm}$ mean / $3.21\text{ mm}$ median tip discrepancy).

Evaluated out-of-fold across all 40 scenes, `NeedleFinder 2026` ($\tau = 50\%$, 768 predicted trajectories) matches **653 of 723 `CY+HT` trajectories** (**90.3% recall**, **85.0% precision**, **87.6% F1**; rising to **92.5% recall** at $\tau = 35\%$) with a mean shaft error of **$0.58 \pm 0.39\text{ mm}$** (**$0.47\text{ mm}$ median**, $0.35\text{ mm}$ IQR, **$1.33\text{ mm}$ P95**) and a 3D tip error of **$5.26 \pm 8.78\text{ mm}$ mean** (**$2.10\text{ mm}$ median**, $4.11\text{ mm}$ IQR, $19.99\text{ mm}$ P95)—within the inter-observer disagreement between the two human teams in mean, median, and 95th-percentile error for both shafts and tips. Even more revealingly, when evaluated against the **de-duplicated union of both expert teams** (`CY+HT` $\cup$ `LB/AMe`, $759$ union trajectories), `NeedleFinder 2026` matches **669 trajectories** (**88.1% recall**, **87.1% precision**, **87.6% F1**, $0.59\text{ mm}$ mean / $0.47\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $5.44\text{ mm}$ mean / $2.17\text{ mm}$ median tip error): **16 trajectories that appeared to be false positives against `CY+HT` alone are in fact genuine catheters independently traced by `LB/AMe`**, raising precision by $+2.1\text{ pp}$. Against `LB/AMe` alone (513 traces), `NeedleFinder 2026` matches 465 trajectories (**90.6% recall**, $0.77\text{ mm}$ mean / $0.51\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $3.97\text{ mm}$ median tip error; unadjusted precision is $60.5\%$ solely because `LB/AMe` omitted most of the 218 `HT` short obturator tracks that `NF 2026` detects).

To separate each source's intrinsic error from the choice of reference team, the third block of Table \ref{tbl:interobserver} applies **Grubbs' (1948) three-cornered-hat estimator** across the $n = 432$ catheters matched three-way among `CY`, `LB/AMe`, and out-of-fold `NeedleFinder 2026`. The intrinsic robust (MAD-scaled) in-plane shaft standard deviations are **$0.35\text{ mm}$ for Rater 1 (`CY`)**, **$0.39\text{ mm}$ for `NeedleFinder 2026`**, and **$0.48\text{ mm}$ for Rater 2 (`LB/AMe`)** (classic MLE RMS $\sigma$: $0.39$, $0.56$, and $0.86\text{ mm}$). For axial tip termination, robust MAD $\sigma_z$ is $0.001\text{ mm}$ (boundary clamp, $95\%$ CI $0.001\text{--}2.02\text{ mm}$) for `CY`, **$2.98\text{ mm}$ for `NeedleFinder 2026`**, and **$3.55\text{ mm}$ for `LB/AMe`**, while 15% Winsorized axial tip $\sigma_z$ is **$3.77\text{ mm}$ (`CY`)**, **$4.94\text{ mm}$ (`NeedleFinder 2026`)**, and **$5.09\text{ mm}$ (`LB/AMe`)**. As detailed in the fourth block of Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}, **one-sided non-inferiority tests** ($H_0: E_{\text{NF},\text{CY}} - E_{\text{LB},\text{CY}} \ge \Delta$) at $\alpha = 0.05$ across all $N = 40$ dual-traced patients ($n = 432$ three-way matched catheters) confirm that `NeedleFinder 2026` is statistically non-inferior to human inter-observer discrepancy on all four endpoints: shaft mean ($\Delta = +0.25\text{ mm}$: $0.56\text{ vs. }0.72\text{ mm}$, diff $-0.16\text{ mm}$, 90% cluster bootstrap CI $[-0.31, -0.02]\text{ mm}$, patient-level Wilcoxon $p_{\text{pt}} = 1.3 \times 10^{-8}$, catheter-level $p_{\text{cath}} = 1.6 \times 10^{-41}$), shaft median ($\Delta = +0.25\text{ mm}$: $0.43\text{ vs. }0.49\text{ mm}$, diff $-0.06\text{ mm}$, 90% CI $[-0.19, +0.01]\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 8.2 \times 10^{-10}$, $p_{\text{cath}} = 1.6 \times 10^{-41}$), 3D tip median ($\Delta = +1.00\text{ mm}$: $2.33\text{ vs. }3.18\text{ mm}$, diff $-0.85\text{ mm}$, 90% CI $[-1.46, -0.52]\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 2.6 \times 10^{-7}$, $p_{\text{cath}} = 2.2 \times 10^{-14}$), and 3D tip mean ($\Delta = +1.50\text{ mm}$: $5.51\text{ vs. }6.10\text{ mm}$, diff $-0.60\text{ mm}$, 90% CI $[-1.80, +0.57]\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 1.1 \times 10^{-4}$, $p_{\text{boot}} = 0.0014$, $p_{\text{cath}} = 6.8 \times 10^{-19}$). Finally, independent adjudication against paired post-implant **Pelvic CT scans** across the $n = 70$ catheters where `CY` and `LB/AMe` disagreed by $\ge 5.0\text{ mm}$ on MRI shows that `NeedleFinder 2026` achieves a **median 3D tip error of $4.20\text{ mm}$ against CT**, compared with **$4.31\text{ mm}$ for `CY`** and **$8.00\text{ mm}$ for `LB/AMe`**, lying closer to CT than single-rater MRI on **78.6% ($55/70$)** of disputed catheters.

A systematic audit of all residual detection and tip errors for `NF 2026` (`4b`, $\tau = 50\%$) across the 54 patients identifies their exact physical causes:

- **False positives ($146$ unmatched predictions $= 1,118 - 972$ across 54 patients, $2.70$ per patient):** In mutually exclusive primary categories, **66 (45.2%, $1.22$ per patient)** are genuine peripheral or background vascular/fascial false alarms ($> 6.0\text{ mm}$ from any reference trajectory), **46 (31.5%)** are residual near-duplicate or split fragments of an already-matched reference catheter, **20 (13.7%)** match an independent second-annotator (`LB/AMe`) trajectory within $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ (plus **10 [6.8%]** within $3.5\text{--}5.0\text{ mm}$ of `LB/AMe`, totaling **30 [20.5%]** supported by Rater 2; and on the 38 patients with paired post-implant Pelvic CT, **52 of 100 [52.0%]** MRI predictions unmatched to `CY` are confirmed as real physical catheters on CT), and **4 (2.7%)** are borderline matches ($3.5\text{--}6.0\text{ mm}$) to an unmatched reference catheter.
- **False negatives ($91$ missed reference trajectories $= 1,063 - 972$, $1.69$ per patient):** Prior to Stage 2B pruning, the $1,960$-track candidate pool covers **97.4% ($1,035/1,063$)** of all reference catheters within $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ (**96.9% [$1,030/1,063$]** under 1-to-1 Hungarian matching; **97.8% [$1,040/1,063$]** closest and **97.5% [$1,036/1,063$]** 1-to-1 within $\le 5.0\text{ mm}$; and **98.8% [$750/759$]** closest / **98.6% [$748/759$]** 1-to-1 on the 759 long interstitial shafts). Of the $91$ missed catheters at $\tau = 50\%$, **28 (30.8%)** are absent from the candidate pool at $3.5\text{ mm}$ ($22$ missing beyond $5.0\text{ mm}$ due to severe mid-shaft T2W susceptibility dropout, $6$ borderline at $3.5\text{--}5.0\text{ mm}$), whereas **63 (69.2%)** are present in the candidate pool at $3.5\text{ mm}$ but pruned during Stage 2B (**46** due to low bundle confidence $p_m < 50\%$, **15** due to 1-to-1 Hungarian collision in dense adhesion clusters, and **2** during deduplication). By catheter type, **45 misses** occur on `cy_long` (**5.9% miss rate** across 759 long shafts) and **46** on `ht_obturator` (**15.1% miss rate** across 304 short obturator tracks). By adhesion status, **27 misses** occur on adhering catheters $\le 3.0\text{ mm}$ ($11.6\%$ of $233$) versus **64** on non-adhering catheters ($7.7\%$ of $830$).
- **Longitudinal versus transverse tip decomposition and tail taxonomy ($> 10$ mm 3D tip error on 162 of 972 [16.7%] matched catheters):** Decomposing every 3D tip error vector into its longitudinal component along the unit shaft tangent ($\Delta s_{\parallel}$) and its transverse component perpendicular to the shaft ($\Delta r_{\perp}$) reveals that **98.76% of total squared 3D tip error across all 972 matched catheters (and 99.32% in the $> 10$ mm tail) lies longitudinally along the catheter direction**, whereas only **1.24% is lateral displacement** ($\Delta r_{\perp}$ median $0.72$ mm, IQR $0.73$ mm). Mutually exclusive root-cause classification of the 162 tail tips ($> 10$ mm) shows that 67 (41.4%) are caudal signal-dropout premature terminations, 41 (25.3%) are cranial susceptibility overshoots into fundal air or bowel pockets, 27 (16.7%) occur on catheters with intrinsic human inter-rater tip ambiguity ($|\Delta z_{\text{CY}-\text{LB}}| > 6$ mm), 20 (12.3%) are crowded-array distal jumps, and 7 (4.3%) are top-of-FOV boundary truncations.

Moreover, propagating the out-of-fold tip uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$ through 30 Monte Carlo dose realizations per patient predicts true patient-level $|\Delta D_{90}|$ without ground truth ($\rho = 0.453, p = 5.8 \times 10^{-4}$).

**Strictly nested split-conformal 3D tip intervals and simulated clinician review-budget curves (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Parts B1–B2; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}b).** Out-of-fold tip uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$ (combining the 1D soft-argmax distribution entropy, local T2W terminal contrast drop, and slice thickness $\Delta z$) correlates strongly with true 3D tip error ($\rho = 0.532, p = 4.9 \times 10^{-72}$) and detects $> 10\text{ mm}$ tip outliers with an **AUROC of 0.805** ($95\%$ CI $[0.763, 0.841]$). Calibrating non-conformity scores $r_i = E_{\text{tip}, i} / \hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}, i}$ strictly on the inner-validation split of each fold yields **distribution-free split-conformal 3D tip intervals** at $k=0$ that achieve exact marginal empirical coverage across all 972 matched test catheters—**79.3%** at 80% nominal, **84.4%** at 85%, **90.2%** at 90%, and **95.4%** at 95%—while shrinking median 3D interval half-widths (radii in mm) by **31.5% at 90% coverage** ($12.11\text{ mm}$ adaptive vs. $17.68\text{ mm}$ fixed unscaled; $8.41\text{ mm}$ on the clearest 50% of tips) and **35.1% at 95% coverage** ($18.42\text{ mm}$ adaptive vs. $28.40\text{ mm}$ fixed unscaled). In a simulated clinician-in-the-loop verification workflow using a retrospective 1-click distal tip relocation oracle ($E_{\text{tip}} \to \sqrt{\Delta r_\perp^2 + \min(|\Delta s_\parallel|, 0.85\text{ mm})^2}$ when $E_{\text{tip}} > 2.0\text{ mm}$, $E_{\text{shaft}}$ unchanged) ranked by $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$, reviewing just **$k = 5$ flagged tips per patient** ($\sim 40\text{ s}$ at $8\text{ s/tip}$) cuts $> 10\text{ mm}$ tip outliers in half ($16.7\% \to 8.0\%$) and lowers mean tip error from **$6.23 \to 3.72\text{ mm}$** ($1.67\text{ mm}$ median, $7.79\text{ mm}$ P90); reviewing **$k = 8$ tips per patient** ($\sim 64\text{ s}$) drives the **95th-percentile tip error below $10\text{ mm}$ ($9.90\text{ mm}$ vs. $27.27\text{ mm}$ unreviewed)** and mean tip error to **$2.77\text{ mm}$** ($1.51\text{ mm}$ median, $78.5\%$ within $\le 3\text{ mm}$). Adding a simulated oracle count-guided missing-catheter check whenever $N_{\text{pred}} < N_{\text{implant}}$ ($0.65\text{ clicks/patient}$) recovers $+17$ additional catheters ($972 \to 989 / 1,063$, **93.0% 1-to-1 recall** / **94.2% closest-match recall**).

**Zero-shot cross-organ transfer to prostate MRI and multi-sequence generalization (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part C; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}c).** Evaluated zero-shot on a clinical 3T T2W SPACE **prostate brachytherapy MRI** (`Case 100 Prostate`, $0.47 \times 0.47 \times 1.6\text{ mm}^3$, 14 plastic interstitial catheters), the gynecologic-trained `Zero-Exposure No-SSL` 5-fold ensemble ($\tau = 50\%$, excluding `Case 100` from SSL pre-training) detects **13 of 14 catheters (92.9% recall) with 100.0% precision (0 false positives, 96.3% F1)**, **$0.33\text{ mm}$ median ($0.38\text{ mm}$ mean) shaft error**, and **$0.99\text{ mm}$ median ($1.64\text{ mm}$ mean, $3.96\text{ mm}$ P95) 3D tip error** under 100% reference-free `k_lim_auto = 40`, and achieves **100.0% recall ($14/14$), 100.0% precision, and 100.0% F1** ($0.34\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $1.57\text{ mm}$ median tip) under template-plane cropping (`k_lim = 47`), while `Stage 1 Full Nested` ($\tau = 50\%$) achieves **100.0% recall ($14/14$), 100.0% precision, and 100.0% F1** ($0.30\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $1.08\text{ mm}$ median tip) directly under `k_lim_auto = 40` and analytical `Stage 0` alone reaches **100.0% recall, 87.5% precision, and 93.3% F1** ($0.35\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $1.23\text{ mm}$ median tip). Ablating the Stage 0 physics channels (`X_{1:3} = 0`) causes the 99.9th-percentile Stage 1 mask probability on prostate MRI to collapse by **$985\times$ ($0.985 \to 0.001$)** and recall to drop to **$0/14$ (0%)**, whereas the 1-channel `ResUNet + clDice` baseline ($\tau = 50\%$) hallucinates **11 false-positive vascular tracks** under `k_lim_auto = 40` (**100.0% recall, 56.0% precision, 71.8% F1**, and 14 FPs / $50.0\%$ precision under `k_lim = 47`), proving that the analytical Stage 0 dark-core symmetry representation is essential for domain-invariant cross-organ transfer. Across 243 secondary MRI series (297 total series minus the 54 primary planning scans) acquired during the 54 patient sessions, zero-shot `NeedleFinder 2026` achieves **97.9% recall ($46/47$), 88.5% precision, and 92.9% F1** ($0.72\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $3.40\text{ mm}$ median tip vs. $10.62\text{ mm}$ for PMB 2019 U-Net) on **3D wbSSFP / True-FISP** ($\Delta z = 2.0\text{--}2.5\text{ mm}$), and recovers **88.4% ($780/882$) of physically present catheters** ($76.9\%$ F1, $1.07\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $4.89\text{ mm}$ median tip vs. $57.6\%$ F1 for 1-channel clDice) on **49 thick-slice 2D axial TSE T2W scans** ($\Delta z = 2.4\text{--}6.0\text{ mm}$) when Z-upsampled to $1.6\text{ mm}$.

**4D intra-procedural serial insertion monitoring, biomechanics, and paired Pelvic CT tracking (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part C; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}c–d).** Across 23 sequential intra-procedural T2W monitoring volumes in `Cases 68–71` ($5\text{--}7$ timepoints per patient as needles were progressively inserted, $108$ final `CY+HT` reference catheters, $1.31\text{ s}$ mean monitoring runtime), coupling `NeedleFinder 2026` with a **4D causal Kalman state-space filter** across timepoints improves pooled 4-case precision by **$+12.4\text{ pp}$ ($76.0\% \to 88.4\%$)** and pooled F1 from **81.6% to 82.8%** ($77.8\%$ recall, $0.75\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $3.57\text{ mm}$ median / $9.75\text{ mm}$ mean tip vs. $88.0\%$ recall, $76.0\%$ precision, $0.45\text{ mm}$ median shaft, $2.71\text{ mm}$ median / $11.23\text{ mm}$ mean tip for independent 3D tracking), with per-case F1 progressing from $77.2\% \to 83.0\%$ on `Case 068` ($n=24$), $84.7\% \to 82.7\%$ (precision $83.7\% \to 93.9\%$) on `Case 069` ($n=42$), **$85.7\% \to 93.3\%$** on `Case 070` ($n=22$), and $76.2\% \to 66.7\%$ (precision $72.7\% \to 100.0\%$) on `Case 071` ($n=20$) while rejecting transient partial-volume monitoring artifacts. Tracking $212$ persisted catheter transitions across sequential insertions provides direct in-vivo biomechanical measurements of **inter-catheter "tissue push"**: already-implanted catheters located within $\le 10\text{ mm}$ of newly inserted needles experience a **$1.44\text{ mm}$ median ($1.61\text{ mm}$ mean, $2.81\text{ mm}$ P90) non-rigid lateral displacement**, compared with **$1.10\text{ mm}$ median ($1.32\text{ mm}$ mean)** at $10\text{--}20\text{ mm}$ and **$1.00\text{ mm}$ median ($1.19\text{ mm}$ mean)** for catheters $> 20\text{ mm}$ away (Mann–Whitney $p = 9.97 \times 10^{-4}$ for $\le 12\text{ mm}$ vs. $> 12\text{ mm}$; Spearman $\rho = -0.226, p = 9.4 \times 10^{-4}$). Across $95$ tracked trajectories ($2,850$ axial stations), **non-linear shaft deflection** from the proximal insertion tangent grows monotonically with insertion depth from $0.003\text{ mm}$ median at $0\text{--}30\text{ mm}$ to $0.15\text{ mm}$ at $30\text{--}60\text{ mm}$, $1.22\text{ mm}$ at $60\text{--}90\text{ mm}$, and **$3.29\text{ mm}$ median ($4.56\text{ mm}$ mean, $10.49\text{ mm}$ P90) at $> 90\text{ mm}$ depth** ($\rho = +0.555, p = 5.55 \times 10^{-9}$). Finally, extending Stage 0 to a **dual-polarity HU tubular filter** (fusing bright-core metallic stylets and dark-core air lumens) enables **zero-shot tracking on 40 post-implant Pelvic CT scans** ($38$ paired with the 54 MRI benchmark patients, $801$ reference catheters), matching **68.9% at $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ and 79.2% ($634/801$, up to 100% on individual patients) at $\le 5.0\text{ mm}$** across the non-rigid MRI-to-CT transfer ($2.01\text{ mm}$ median shaft registration residual, $2.78\text{ mm}$ median axial tip residual) and confirming that **52.0% ($52/100$) of apparent false-positive MRI detections against `CY` are real physical catheters present on CT**.

## Where This Work Sits in the Literature

Table \ref{tbl:literature} places NeedleFinder 2026 among published catheter reconstruction studies on CT, ultrasound, and MRI. CT and ultrasound solve an easier imaging problem: metal needles, air-filled lumens, or bright specular reflections give very high contrast, so segmentation networks reach shaft errors of 0.1 to 0.7 mm on CT [@weishaupt2022; @xiao2026; @quetin2025] and 0.1 to 0.6 mm on ultrasound [@zhang2020pmb; @andersen2020]. On T2W MRI without contrast markers, a catheter is a 2 mm dark hole that looks like a vessel, on slices 1.2 to 6 mm thick; the best published MRI results are 0.74 to 0.97 mm shaft error on gynecologic implants, obtained with line markers or with CT-derived reference reconstructions [@shaaer2022; @kim2026] and 0.75 to 0.93 mm on prostate implants [@dai2020; @kostoulas2025]. NeedleFinder 2026 reaches a median shaft error of 0.47 mm ($0.59\text{ mm}$ mean; $0.45\text{ mm}$ median / $0.57\text{ mm}$ mean on long active-dwell shafts) and a median 3D tip error of 2.27 mm ($6.23\text{ mm}$ mean) on T2W MRI alone, on the largest annotated MRI cohort to date.

The head-to-head comparison in Table \ref{tbl:main} explains why neither voxel segmentation [@zaffino2019; @isensee2021; @shit2021] nor mask-driven tracking [@xiao2026] is enough on MRI, and why the three-stage architecture works. Skeletonizing a mask cannot bridge a one-slice dropout, and it turns every vessel blob into a track. Tracking bridges the gaps but still trusts the mask. NeedleFinder 2026 resolves these bottlenecks through five complementary mechanisms: Stage 0 rejects one-sided anatomical edges before learning takes place and automatically locates the template plane (`k_lim_auto`); Stage 1 combines self-supervised pre-training on external pelvic MRI (with synthetic susceptibility tubes and anatomical hard negatives) and multi-rater consensus supervision to regress sharp centerline peaks that stay separate when masks merge; Stage 2A bridges 1-to-3 slice dropouts via triplet message passing and collinear fragment stitching; Stage 2B judges each trajectory in the context of the entire 15 to 30 catheter bundle; and the 91-step dilated 1D ResNet + self-attention axial localizer (`Dedicated1DTipLocalizer`) pinpoints the distal termination directly from sub-slice multi-scale tubular profiles.

## What It Means in the Clinic, and Limitations

NeedleFinder 2026 is a one-click module in 3D Slicer [@fedorov2012] that runs in a few seconds without a GPU ($4.15\text{ s}$ mean on a 24-core workstation CPU, $1.26\text{ s}$ on standard scans, $2.67\text{ s}$ on GPU). It changes the physicist's task from tracing 15 to 30 catheters slice by slice, which takes tens of minutes, into a structured, uncertainty-guided verification step taking under a minute: click *Detect Needles*, inspect the 3D bundle, check the $k = 2\text{ to }8$ tips with the widest split-conformal uncertainty intervals ($\sim 16\text{--}64\text{ s}$, driving P95 tip error below $10\text{ mm}$ and mean tip error to $2.77\text{ mm}$ in simulated 1-click tip review), and click any missing catheter when $N_{\text{pred}} < N_{\text{implant}}$ ($0.65\text{ clicks/patient}$, raising 1-to-1 recall to $93.0\%$). Because Grubbs' three-cornered-hat analysis and one-sided non-inferiority tests show that the algorithm's intrinsic shaft error ($0.39\text{ mm}$ robust MAD SD vs. $0.35\text{ mm}$ for Rater 1 and $0.48\text{ mm}$ for Rater 2) and axial tip error ($2.98\text{ mm}$ robust MAD $\sigma_z$ vs. $3.55\text{ mm}$ for Rater 2, $4.94\text{ mm}$ vs. $5.09\text{ mm}$ 15% Winsorized $\sigma_z$; $4.20\text{ mm}$ median error against independent Pelvic CT on disputed tips) are statistically non-inferior to human inter-observer variability—and 52.0% of apparent extra MRI detections on paired cases are real physical catheters confirmed by post-implant CT—the shafts themselves rarely require manual editing, and counterfactual TG-43U1 plan optimization with Top-2 tip review holds median target $|\Delta D_{90}|$ to $2.8\%$ ($5.9\%$ mean, $\gamma_{2\%/2\text{mm}} = 74.3\%\text{--}78.2\%$).

Several limitations remain. First, while `Dedicated1DTipLocalizer` and collinear fragment stitching cut the unreviewed mean 3D tip error from $10.75\text{ mm}$ (PMB 2019) and $15.65\text{ mm}$ (Xiao 2026) to $6.23\text{ mm}$ ($2.27\text{ mm}$ median, with $58.7\%$ of tips within $\le 3.0\text{ mm}$), $16.7\%$ ($162/972$) of unreviewed matched catheters still have a tip error above $10\text{ mm}$ ($27.27\text{ mm}$ P95), of which 99.3% is longitudinal stopping ambiguity along the shaft ($41.4\%$ caudal dropout undershoots, $25.3\%$ cranial susceptibility overshoots, $16.7\%$ intrinsic human inter-rater tip ambiguity, $12.3\%$ crowded-array identity jumps, and $4.3\%$ FOV boundary truncations). Although our split-conformal uncertainty intervals ($90.2\%$ empirical coverage at $90\%$ nominal) and $k = 5\text{--}8$ tip review workflow effectively contain this tail in practice, thin-slice isotropic 3D T2W sequences ($\le 1\text{ mm}$), zero-echo-time sequences [@kaza2022; @lee2023], or multi-contrast inputs are the natural next step to reduce unreviewed longitudinal tip ambiguity further. Second, while we demonstrated zero-shot generalization across 48 external `MSD Task05_Prostate` scans ($0.125\text{--}0.33\text{ FP/scan}$), 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI (`Case 100`, $96.3\%\text{--}100.0\%$ F1), 3D wbSSFP ($92.9\%$ F1), thick-slice 2D T2W MRI ($88.4\%$ recall), 4D serial insertion scans ($82.8\%$ pooled F1, up to $93.3\%$ on `Case 070`), and 38 paired Pelvic CT patients (40 CT volumes total), all clinical brachytherapy implants come from a single institution; a prospective multi-center trial across 1.5T, 3T, and low-field systems [@major2026] from multiple vendors is still required. Third, our AAPM TG-43U1 dosimetric evaluation uses standardized target and OAR envelopes derived from the active implant geometry rather than manual physician contours from clinical DICOM RTSTRUCT/RTPLAN files (which were not archived in the de-identified research repository), and our clinician review-budget curves are quantitative simulations rather than prospective stopwatch timings in the operating room. Fourth, our ablation shows that once multi-rater SSL Stage 1, collinear stitching, and the 1D axial tip localizer are included, `SynthBrachy3T` provides its primary detection gain in the zero-label ($k=0$) regime ($+2.85\text{ pp}$ F1 overall, $+6.3\text{ pp}$ on long shafts) while still improving dwell-weighted tip error ($6.65 \to 5.72\text{ mm}$) and counterfactual target $|\Delta D_{90}|$ ($12.8\% \to 8.2\%$) at the full 43-patient training budget.

# Conclusion

Evaluated under strictly nested $5 \times 5$ patient-level out-of-fold cross-validation with 100% reference-free axial cropping (`k_lim_auto`) across 54 clinical 3T T2W MRI scans ($1,063$ catheters), NeedleFinder 2026 achieves **89.1% F1** (**91.4% recall**, **86.9% precision**; **92.3% recall** and **89.1% F1** under $5 \times 5$ inner-OOF Stage 1 cross-fitting), **$0.59 \pm 0.40\text{ mm}$ mean ($0.47\text{ mm}$ median) in-plane shaft error**, and **$6.23 \pm 10.49\text{ mm}$ mean ($2.27\text{ mm}$ median) 3D distal tip error**, executing in **$4.15\text{ s}$ on a 24-core workstation CPU** ($1.26\text{ s}$ on standard-resolution scans, $5.85\text{ s}$ on high-resolution scans) and **$2.67\text{ s}$ on a GPU**. On the 759 full-depth active-dwell shafts it reaches **91.3% recall** ($94.7\%$ at $\tau = 35\%$) and **90.0% obturator-adjusted F1** (**90.5%** with inner-OOF cross-fitting), and on the 23 patients with severe catheter adhesion it reaches **91.3%–91.8% F1**. Across all 40 doubly annotated clinical scans, Grubbs' three-cornered-hat analysis ($0.39\text{ mm}$ intrinsic shaft MAD SD vs. $0.35$ and $0.48\text{ mm}$ for the two human teams; $2.98$ vs. $3.55\text{ mm}$ tip MAD $\sigma_z$), one-sided non-inferiority tests ($p_{\text{pt}} \le 0.0085$, $p_{\text{cath}} \le 2.5 \times 10^{-11}$), and independent Pelvic CT adjudication ($52.0\%$ of apparent extra MRI tracks confirmed on CT; $4.20\text{ mm}$ median tip error vs. $4.31$ and $8.00\text{ mm}$ on disputed tips) establish that the algorithm is statistically non-inferior to human expert variability. Coupled with calibrated split-conformal 3D tip uncertainty intervals ($90.2\%$ empirical coverage at $90\%$ nominal), reviewing $2\text{ to }8$ flagged tips per patient in simulated 1-click tip review reduces P95 tip error below $10\text{ mm}$ and holds counterfactual AAPM TG-43U1 $^{192}\text{Ir}$ target $|\Delta D_{90}|$ to $2.8\%$ median ($5.9\%$ mean), while zero-shot transfer generalizes to 3T prostate MRI ($96.3\%\text{--}100.0\%$ F1), 3D wbSSFP ($92.9\%$ F1), 4D serial insertion tracking ($82.8\%$ pooled F1, $+12.4\%$ precision gain, up to $93.3\%$ F1 on `Case 070`), and 38 paired Pelvic CT patients (40 CT volumes total) without retraining.
