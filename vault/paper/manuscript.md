title: "NeedleFinder 2026: Automatic Catheter Reconstruction with Sub-Millimeter Shaft Accuracy on MRI for Interstitial Gynecologic Brachytherapy in Seconds"
date: "October 2026 (Target Journal: *Physics in Medicine & Biology*)"
abstract: |
  **Objective.** In MRI-guided interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy, a clinical team inserts 15 to 30 thin plastic catheters into a pelvic tumor and acquires a 3T T2-weighted MRI scan while the patient is under anesthesia. Before radiation can be planned, a physicist must trace the 3D path of every catheter on the scan. Doing this by hand takes about 45 to 50 minutes. Existing automatic methods based on voxel segmentation struggle on T2-weighted MRI because plastic catheters appear only as tiny dark dots that look much like blood vessels, and brief signal dropouts break single catheters into disconnected pieces. Here we present NeedleFinder 2026, an algorithm that reconstructs the catheter bundle automatically, to sub-millimeter shaft accuracy, in a few seconds.

  **Approach.** NeedleFinder 2026 combines imaging physics with three stages of machine learning. First, an analytical 3D filter (Stage 0) locates the entry plane of the perineal template automatically and measures circular dark-core symmetry on each slice—highlighting round catheter cross-sections while ignoring one-sided tissue borders. Second, a native-resolution 3D residual U-Net (Stage 1) predicts the sub-pixel center and local 3D tilt of each catheter on every slice. To prevent this network from mistaking pelvic veins for catheters, we pre-train it on 51 external pelvic MRI scans, 50 of them needle-free, injected with synthetic catheter voids and fine-tune it on consensus traces from two expert teams. Third, a graph-based tracking pipeline (Stage 2) links detections across slices, stitches fragments broken by signal dropouts, evaluates the entire 15-to-30-catheter bundle together using a graph transformer, and pinpoints each catheter's deepest tip using a 1D axial neural network equipped with calibrated uncertainty intervals. We evaluated the pipeline across 54 clinical 3T MRI scans (1,063 catheters) using strictly nested $5 \times 5$ patient-level cross-validation.

  **Main results.** NeedleFinder 2026 detected 91.4% of all 1,063 catheters with 86.9% precision and an F1 score of 89.1% (87.4% and 89.4% after adding three refinements borrowed from the literature; we quote both configurations as a range below) (and 90.0%–90.1% F1 on the 759 full-depth treatment shafts), outperforming our re-implementations of five peer-reviewed MRI/curvilinear methods and one recent tracking preprint on the identical nested splits by 6.0 to 50.7 F1 points ($p \le 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$). Along the catheter shaft, the median in-plane error was 0.47 mm (mean 0.58–0.59 mm; 0.44 mm median on full-depth shafts). At the deepest tip, the median 3D error was 2.26–2.27 mm (mean 6.23 mm), with 98.8% of the squared tip error lying longitudinally along the catheter track. Across 40 scans traced independently by two human expert teams, one-sided non-inferiority tests, with Team 1 as reference, showed that the algorithm is statistically non-inferior to human inter-observer agreement on both shafts ($p = 1.3 \times 10^{-8}$) and tips ($p = 2.6 \times 10^{-7}$). Post-implant CT scans from 38 paired patients confirmed that 52.0% of the algorithm's apparent "false alarms" on MRI were physical catheters visible on the later CT that the primary human team had not traced on MRI. In simulated high-dose-rate $^{192}\text{Ir}$ treatment planning on standardized target envelopes, moving only the two tips per patient that the algorithm flagged as most uncertain to their reference depth (an oracle correction, modeled at 18 seconds of review) lowered the median tumor dose error ($|\Delta D_{90}|$) from 4.2% to 2.8% (mean from 8.2% to 5.9%), while differences in dose to proxy bladder and rectum shells remained 13% to 14% median after review. Without retraining, the gynecologic pipeline also found 13 of 14 needles with no false alarms on a single 3T prostate brachytherapy scan, reached 92.9% F1 on balanced steady-state MRI (2 scans, 47 catheters) but only 56.1% F1 on post-contrast T1 MRI (12 scans, 217 catheters), and 82.8% F1 on 23 serial needle-insertion scans from 4 patients, and recovered 68.9% of catheters on post-implant CT. End-to-end reconstruction takes 4.15 s per patient on a workstation CPU (1.26 s on the 20 standard-resolution scans, 5.85 s on the 34 high-resolution scans) and 2.67 s on a GPU.

  **Significance.** Combining a simple physical symmetry prior with multi-stage graph learning turns a manual tracing bottleneck of 45 to 50 minutes into a 4-second automatic reconstruction (production code path, timed headless) followed by a brief, modeled uncertainty-guided check in 3D Slicer.

# Plain-Language Summary {-}

Advanced cancers of the cervix, uterus, and vagina are often treated with *interstitial brachytherapy*. While the patient is asleep under anesthesia, doctors guide 15 to 30 thin plastic tubes (catheters) into the tumor. A tiny radioactive source will later travel through these tubes for a few minutes to destroy the cancer from the inside while sparing nearby healthy organs like the bladder and bowel.

Before any radiation can be delivered, the medical team takes an MRI scan to see where the tubes landed inside the tumor. Because radiation strength drops off very rapidly with distance, being off by even a couple of millimeters can under-treat the tumor or over-irradiate a healthy organ. Currently, a physicist must sit at a computer and trace every single tube by hand across dozens of MRI slices while the patient waits with the implant in place, often still sedated—a painstaking job that takes about 45 to 50 minutes in published MRI-only workflows.

Automating this step on MRI has been difficult for a simple physical reason: plastic tubes contain no mobile water, so they do not glow on an MRI scan. Instead, each tube appears only as a tiny dark dot, two millimeters wide, on each image slice. The human pelvis is full of other dark structures—blood vessels, air pockets, and tissue borders—so standard artificial intelligence programs easily confuse blood vessels with tubes, or break a single tube into pieces when the dark dot fades on one slice.

We built **NeedleFinder 2026** to solve this problem in the way a careful human physicist does—by combining local image appearance with the overall geometry of the implant:

1. **Look for dark circles, not dark edges (Stage 0).** A simple mathematical filter checks whether each dark spot is surrounded by brighter tissue in *all* directions (like a tube cross-section) rather than dark on one side only (like a muscle border). It also automatically finds where the needle-guiding template sits at the base of the scan.
2. **Pinpoint tube centers and directions in 3D (Stage 1).** A 3D neural network—first pre-trained on 51 external pelvic MRI scans to tell artificial tubes apart from real blood vessels, and then trained on traces from two independent teams of human experts—marks the center and tilt of each tube on every slice, even when two tubes touch.
3. **Connect the dots into a coherent bundle and find each tip (Stage 2).** Two graph networks connect the marks from slice to slice, bridge gaps where a tube briefly fades, and judge whether all 15 to 30 curves make geometric sense together as a single implant. Finally, a specialized 1D network looks along the end of each tube to pinpoint its deepest tip and provides an uncertainty bar telling the physicist which tips are clear and which ones deserve a quick second look.

Tested across 54 patients (1,063 tubes), NeedleFinder 2026 found 91.4% of all tubes automatically. Along the length of each tube, its path agreed with human experts to within half a millimeter (0.47 mm median); on the same catheters traced by everyone, it was closer to one expert team (0.43 mm median) than the two teams of human experts were to each other (0.49 mm median). At the deepest tip, on the same catheters traced by everyone, its typical error was 2.3 mm, compared with 3.2 mm between the two human teams. When we checked post-implant CT scans (where tubes are easy to see), more than half (52%) of the "extra" tubes found by our software on MRI turned out to be real tubes that the primary human team had not traced on MRI.

In practice, NeedleFinder 2026 runs inside the free **3D Slicer** medical software; the reconstruction takes **4 seconds** on a 24-core workstation computer without a graphics card (1.3 to 5.9 seconds depending on scan resolution) when the same production code is run outside the Slicer window; we have not yet timed the full user interface. Instead of tracing 25 tubes from scratch, the physicist clicks one button and then checks the two tube tips the software is least sure about. In our computer simulations this check takes under half a minute, and the resulting radiation plan delivers a tumor dose within 2.8% (median) of a plan built on fully manual tracing. This is a simulation in which both corrected tips are placed exactly where the expert put them; we have not yet timed the check with clinicians in the operating room.

# Introduction

## The Clinical Bottleneck in MRI-Guided Brachytherapy

For locally advanced cervical and vaginal cancer, high-dose-rate (HDR) brachytherapy—delivering radiation directly inside the tumor—is essential for cure [@potter2018; @potter2021]. The international EMBRACE-I study demonstrated 92% five-year local tumor control by delivering a median dose of 90 Gy (EQD2) to the high-risk tumor volume [@potter2021], and the EMBRACE-II protocol prescribes 90 to 95 Gy to the target while strictly limiting dose to the adjacent bladder, rectum, and sigmoid colon [@potter2018]. When a tumor is large, asymmetric, or extends into surrounding tissues, a central uterine tandem alone cannot cover it safely. Clinicians therefore perform *interstitial* brachytherapy: alongside the central applicator, they insert 15 to 30 thin plastic catheters through a perineal template or freehand directly into the tumor [@kapur2012; @mastmeyer2017; @zaffino2019; @chino2025].

T2-weighted (T2W) MRI is the gold standard for visualizing gynecologic tumors and surrounding soft-tissue organs, and international guidelines (GEC-ESTRO and AAPM Task Group 303) recommend MRI-based treatment planning [@dimopoulos2012; @jacobsen2022; @prisciandaro2022; @richardson2025]. Ideally, the entire workflow—imaging, tumor contouring, catheter reconstruction, and radiation dose optimization—takes place on the T2W planning MRI alone [@shaaer2020; @malajovich2020; @lee2023]. In practice, however, many clinics still acquire an additional CT scan and fuse it to the MRI simply because plastic catheters are so difficult to see on MRI [@hellebust2010; @ladbury2023], or rely on specialized positive-contrast lumen markers [@zhangh2019; @ning2020; @shaaer2020] or zero-echo-time sequences [@kaza2022; @lee2023] that require extra scan time and image registration.

Why is reconstructing catheters on a standard T2W MRI so challenging, and why does its accuracy matter so much?

1. **Radiation dose changes steeply over millimeters.** The $^{192}\text{Ir}$ source steps through each catheter in 2.5 to 5.0 mm increments, and its dose falls off roughly with the inverse square of distance. A 1 mm shift along the catheter path or a 1.5 mm shift toward an organ at risk can alter bladder or rectal dose metrics ($D_{2\text{cc}}$) by 5% to 10% [@tanderup2008; @schindel2013; @kirisits2014; @singh2026], and swapping or mis-tracing catheters can shift target and urethral doses by 5% to 20% [@rylander2017; @nunez2021]. Even experienced clinicians make errors when digitizing catheters under time pressure [@haack2009; @androulakis2025].
2. **Catheters look like tiny dark voids surrounded by dark anatomy.** A 2 mm (6F) plastic catheter contains no mobile water protons. On an axial T2W MRI slice, its plastic wall and lumen produce a small proton-density signal void, 2 to 5 pixels across, accompanied by a mild magnetic susceptibility ($\Delta\chi$) transition at the catheter–tissue interface [@richart2018]. Unfortunately, the female pelvis is full of other dark structures: flow-void blood vessels, air pockets in the rectum or vagina, calcifications, and the dark connective-tissue planes (fascia) between muscles.
3. **Manual tracing takes tens of minutes while the patient waits with the implant in place.** Because each scan contains 28 to 160 axial slices (1.2 to 6.0 mm thick) and 15 to 30 catheters that bend, cross, or touch one another, tracing every catheter slice by slice takes 46 to 51 minutes on average in reported MRI-only gynecologic workflows [@shaaer2022; @shaaer2020]—nearly half of the 100-minute operating-room procedure [@roumeliotis2026].

Our goal is to replace this manual bottleneck with an automatic reconstruction algorithm that operates directly on standard clinical 3T T2W MRI, matches human expert precision along both the shaft and the tip, flags its own uncertain tips for rapid human verification, and finishes in a few seconds.

## A Quick Guide for Readers Outside Medical Physics or Machine Learning

Because this paper bridges clinical radiation oncology, MRI physics, and graph deep learning, we briefly define the core terms used throughout the text:

- **Catheter (or needle), shaft, and tip.** We use *catheter* and *needle* interchangeably for the hollow 2 mm plastic tubes inserted into the tumor. The **shaft** is the main body of the tube passing through the tissue; the **tip** (or *distal/cranial end*) is the deepest point the tube reaches inside the body, where the radioactive source stops first.
- **Obturator and tandem.** Larger central plastic guides placed in the vagina (*obturator*) and uterus (*tandem*) around which the thinner interstitial catheters are arranged.
- **Voxel, in-plane resolution, and slice thickness.** A 3D MRI scan is a stack of 2D horizontal cross-sections (*axial slices*). Within each 2D slice (*in-plane*), the image is sharp (pixel size 0.31 to 0.88 mm). Between slices (along the vertical body axis, $z$), the spacing is much coarser (slice thickness 1.2 to 6.0 mm). This anisotropy explains why finding the sideways (*in-plane*) center of a catheter is easier than judging the exact height ($z$) where its tip ends between two slices.
- **Recall, precision, and F1 score.** *Recall* is the percentage of real catheters the algorithm successfully finds. *Precision* is the percentage of algorithm-reported tracks that correspond to a real catheter (rather than a false alarm on a blood vessel). The *F1 score* is the harmonic mean of recall and precision; it is high only when the algorithm finds almost all real catheters while inventing very few false ones.
- **Shaft error ($E_{\text{shaft}}$) and tip error ($E_{\text{tip}}$).** For every detected catheter that matches an expert trace (within 3.5 mm), *shaft error* measures the sideways distance (in mm) between the automatic curve and the expert curve along their shared length. *Tip error* measures the 3D distance (in mm) between the predicted deepest tip and the expert's deepest tip.
- **Strictly nested cross-validation (out-of-fold evaluation).** To test our models on all 54 patients without ever testing on data used for training or tuning, we split the 54 patients into 5 outer groups (folds). In each round, 43 or 44 patients are used for training and tuning (via an internal 80/20 split or 5-fold inner cross-fitting), and the remaining 10 or 11 patients are held in a locked vault and evaluated *once*. Every patient is thus evaluated strictly "out-of-fold" (as an unseen patient).
- **Dosimetric metrics ($D_{90}$, $D_{2\text{cc}}$, and 3D $\gamma$).** In brachytherapy planning, $D_{90}$ is the minimum radiation dose covering 90% of the high-risk tumor volume (which clinicians want to keep high), while $D_{2\text{cc}}$ is the dose to the most-irradiated $2\text{ cm}^3$ of a nearby healthy organ like the bladder or rectum (which clinicians want to keep low). The 3D *gamma ($\gamma$) pass rate* ($2\%/2\text{ mm}$) measures the percentage of points in the 3D volume where the dose planned on automatic catheters agrees with the reference plan within 2% dose or 2 mm distance.

## Why Existing Approaches Fall Short on T2-Weighted MRI

Over the past decade, automatic catheter reconstruction has evolved through three generations (reviewed in [@sargazi2026; @vijayan2026]):

**Generation 1: Geometric and mechanical tracking from manual clicks (2012–2019).** Early work in our Brigham and Women's Hospital (BWH) program introduced image-guided catheter tracking in the Advanced Multimodality Image Guided Operating (AMIGO) suite [@kapur2012; @chen2014]. Starting from a manual click at each catheter tip, cone-search trackers [@pernelle2013] and mechanical rod-and-spring splines [@mastmeyer2017a; @mastmeyer2017] achieved ~1.4–1.5 mm accuracy on T2W MRI. Similar geometry-guided or template-registered methods work well on CT, ultrasound, or rigid applicators [@qiu2013; @dise2015; @richart2015; @hrinivich2019; @deufel2020; @zheng2021]. However, they still require a human to click every catheter tip first, and they often lose track when catheters bend sharply or cross dark vessels.

**Generation 2: 3D voxel segmentation with clustering (2019–2026).** To eliminate manual clicks, Zaffino *et al.* [@zaffino2019] trained a 3D U-Net on 50 BWH gynecologic MRI scans to classify every voxel as "catheter" or "background," then grouped the segmented voxels into tubes using density clustering (DBSCAN). Subsequent studies applied 3D U-Nets and nnU-Net [@isensee2021] across CT, ultrasound, and prostate or marker-assisted MRI [@jung2019; @jung2019b; @dai2020; @zhangd2020; @zhang2020tmi; @zhang2020pmb; @zhang2020mp; @andersen2020; @wangf2020; @li2020; @hu2021; @weishaupt2022; @liu2022; @shaaer2022; @xie2023; @aleong2024; @wang2024; @zhou2024; @goulet2025; @kostoulas2025; @moradi2025; @quetin2025; @kim2026]. Yet even when trained with topology-preserving losses such as centerline-Dice (clDice) [@hu2019; @shit2021; @kirchhoff2024], pure voxel segmentation suffers from two fundamental failure modes on T2W MRI:
1. *Over-fragmentation:* If a catheter's dark void fades on even a single slice due to partial-volume averaging, the segmented mask breaks into 2 to 4 separate pieces.
2. *Touching catheters and vascular false alarms:* When two catheters touch (*catheter adhesion*), their voxel masks merge into a single blob; meanwhile, dark pelvic veins and tissue edges are segmented as dozens of short false catheters.

**Generation 3: Structure-aware tracking, gap-bridging, and uncertainty-weighted fitting (2020–2026).** To mitigate segmentation breaks on MRI, Dai *et al.* [@dai2020] introduced a deeply supervised 3D attention-gated U-Net with axial total-variation (TV) continuity regularization, Kostoulas *et al.* [@kostoulas2025] combined a 3D residual U-Net with iterative collinear gap-bridging and false-part pruning, and Aleong *et al.* [@aleong2024] and Kim *et al.* [@kim2026] leveraged 5-model epistemic uncertainty ensembles and inverse-variance-weighted polynomial trajectory fitting. On pelvic CT, Xiao *et al.* [@xiao2026; @xiao2026jbhi] addressed catheter adhesion by combining an implant-region gating module with 3D Hough initialization and synchronous slice-to-slice tracking. In computer vision, message-passing graph networks [@braso2020; @benhaim2022; @shit2022], keypoint regression [@zhouw2026], and synthetic-to-real transfer [@arapi2023] have similarly improved curvilinear tracking. However, a mask-driven tracker applied to T2W MRI without a physical circular-symmetry prior or whole-bundle reasoning still trusts the underlying segmentation mask. Dark pelvic veins inside the implant region are tracked as catheters. The tracker has no mechanism to ask whether the resulting collection of 25 trajectories makes sense *together* as a physical implant, nor to resolve sub-slice tip terminations on thick MRI slices. (Hardware-based electromagnetic or active MR tracking avoids image ambiguity [@mehrtash2014; @wang2015; @dearcos2017; @durrbeck2022; @cantin2024; @deufel2024; @beaulieu2025; @roumeliotis2026], but requires specialized catheters and operating-room hardware.)

## How NeedleFinder 2026 Solves These Problems

NeedleFinder 2026 bridges all three generations on the complete 54-patient, 1,063-catheter BWH gynecologic MRI cohort—to our knowledge the largest annotated clinical MRI dataset for interstitial brachytherapy to date (Figure \ref{fig:overview}). Each stage of our architecture is designed to solve a specific failure mode of prior work:

1. **Physics before learning (Stage 0):** Before any neural network runs, an analytical 3D circular dark-core symmetry filter separates round dark cross-sections (catheters and vessels) from one-sided dark boundaries (fascial planes and organ walls), while an automatic template-plane locator finds the base of the implant without needing manual bounding boxes.
2. **Centerline peaks and self-supervised pelvic pre-training (Stage 1):** Instead of relying on binary masks alone, our native-resolution 3D U-Net regresses sharp sub-pixel *centerline peaks* and *3D tangent directions*—so two touching catheters still produce two distinct peaks. To prevent the network from confusing pelvic veins with catheters, we pre-train it on 51 external pelvic MRI volumes (from 50 subjects) by injecting realistic synthetic catheter voids while explicitly penalizing real pelvic vessels and fascia as hard negatives, and then fine-tune it using consensus traces from two independent expert teams.
3. **Graph linking, fragment stitching, bundle-level reasoning, and 1D tip localization (Stage 2):** A triplet message-passing graph network links slice detections while penalizing sharp kinks, and a geometric stitcher reconnects catheter fragments separated by 1-to-3-slice signal dropouts. Next, a bundle graph transformer evaluates all candidate trajectories simultaneously in the context of the whole implant. Finally, a dedicated 1D axial neural network samples a fine $0.5\text{ mm}$ multi-scale profile along the end of each trajectory to pinpoint the exact distal tip and output a calibrated split-conformal uncertainty interval, optionally fused with sub-voxel inverse-variance-weighted polynomial refinement [@aleong2024; @kim2026], axial TV continuity [@dai2020], and iterative multi-segment gap-bridging [@kostoulas2025].

Beyond geometric benchmarking against five peer-reviewed MRI/curvilinear methods and one recent tracking preprint reproduced on the identical nested splits, we evaluate NeedleFinder 2026 across four clinical translation dimensions: (i) three-cornered-hat observer adjudication against two human expert teams and paired post-implant CT scans; (ii) AAPM TG-43U1 $^{192}\text{Ir}$ radiation dosimetry; (iii) uncertainty-guided clinician tip triage; and (iv) zero-shot generalization to needle-free pelvic MRI, 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI, secondary MRI pulse sequences, serial intra-procedural insertion scans, and pelvic CT.

# Architecture of NeedleFinder 2026

A human physicist tracing catheters on MRI uses three kinds of clues in sequence: first, looking for small round dark spots on each 2D slice; second, checking whether those dark spots line up across adjacent slices; and third, verifying that the full collection of 15 to 30 curves forms a plausible implant bundle and stopping each curve where the dark void disappears at the tip. NeedleFinder 2026 mirrors this exact hierarchy in three stages (Figures \ref{fig:overview} and \ref{fig:arch}).

## Stage 0: Physical Dark-Core Symmetry and Automatic Template Cropping

**The physical intuition.** Why do standard neural networks confuse muscle borders and organ walls with catheters? Because both are dark on T2W MRI. However, their *geometry* is different: a muscle border is a dark edge with bright tissue on one side and dark tissue on the other, whereas a catheter cross-section is a tiny dark core surrounded by brighter tissue in *every* direction.

Stage 0 turns this geometric observation into a fast 3D filter $S_0(x,y,z)$ with no learned parameters, implemented in multi-threaded native Rust. At every voxel $\mathbf{p}$ on axial slice $z$, we sample six diameters passing through $\mathbf{p}$ at angles $\theta_d = d\pi/6$ ($d \in \{0, \dots, 5\}$) across four physical radii $r \in \mathcal{R} = \{1.35, 1.85, 2.5, 3.3\}\text{ mm}$ (converted to pixels using the scan's in-plane spacing). Along each diameter $d$ and radius $r$, we take the *darker* of the two opposite ring points:
$$m_{d,r} = \min\!\big(I(\mathbf{p} + r\mathbf{u}_d),\; I(\mathbf{p} - r\mathbf{u}_d)\big).$$
If $\mathbf{p}$ sits on a one-sided edge, at least one end of the diameter remains dark, keeping $m_{d,r}$ small. We then sort the six directional values ($m_{(1),r} \le \dots \le m_{(6),r}$) and compute a robust ring brightness that requires the surrounding tissue to be bright in almost all directions while tolerating a single dark neighbor (such as a touching twin catheter):
$$S_{\text{ring}}(\mathbf{p}) = \max_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \big[0.45\, m_{(2),r} + 0.55\, m_{(3),r}\big].$$
Combining this positive ring-to-core contrast with the relative darkness of the $3 \times 3$ smoothed center intensity $\bar{I}_{3\times 3}(\mathbf{p}) \in [0, 1]$ yields the raw dark-core symmetry score,
$$S_0(\mathbf{p}) \propto \max\!\big(0,\; S_{\text{ring}}(\mathbf{p}) - \bar{I}_{3\times 3}(\mathbf{p})\big)\cdot\big[1 - \bar{I}_{3\times 3}(\mathbf{p})\big],$$
which is normalized to $[0, 1]$ per volume. Because $S_0$ takes the maximum over radii up to $3.3\text{ mm}$ and uses the second- and third-darkest of the six directional values, it is, by construction, tolerant of realistic oblique catheter tilt ($\alpha \le 25^\circ\text{--}30^\circ$ relative to the slice normal, where the in-plane elliptical cross-section elongates by only $\sec\alpha \le 1.15$). On very thick slices ($\Delta z = 4\text{--}6\text{ mm}$) where steep tilt causes stronger partial-volume elongation, we first upsample the volume along $z$ to $1.6\text{ mm}$ spacing and rely on Stage 2A fragment stitching to bridge dimmed slices.

**Automatic reference-free cropping of the template plane.** Below the patient's perineum, the inferior slices of the MRI scan contain outside air and the plastic needle-guiding template. Previously, pipelines relied on manually drawn bounding boxes to crop out these bottom slices, which is unavailable in a fully automatic workflow and occasionally clipped tilted templates too high—cutting off the bases of 42 catheters across our cohort. We replaced this with a reference-free axial template locator. After standardizing the volume to caudo-cranial slice ordering via its DICOM/NRRD direction cosines, the locator scans upward from the inferior boundary and detects the step-change in 90th-percentile soft-tissue intensity alongside the emergence of clustered dark-core candidates ($S_0$) within the central field of view. Across all 54 patients, it places the crop boundary a median of $2.0$ slices deeper than the manually drawn boxes (mean $1.91$ slices deeper, IQR $1.0$; $64.1$ vs. $62.2$ active slices on average), eliminating all 42 in-field-of-view base truncations and preserving **100.0% of in-field-of-view catheter length** ($99.65\%$ of total unclipped catheter length) without ever consulting manual annotations. Stage 0 and automatic cropping run in **0.27 s** per volume on a 24-core CPU (0.07 s on standard scans, 0.38 s on high-resolution scans).

It predicts four heads at native pixel resolution: tubular mask ($P_{\text{mask}}$), sharp sub-pixel centerline peaks ($P_{\text{peak}}$), local 3D tangent slopes ($V_x, V_y$), and the central obturator ($P_{\text{obt}}$). **(b) Stage 2:** Bidirectional Kalman seed tracking and a 4-layer triplet message-passing network link slice peaks into smooth 3D candidates; a geometric stitcher reconnects collinear fragments across signal dropouts; a bundle graph transformer scores all trajectories together in the context of the whole implant; and a 91-step dilated 1D ResNet + self-attention axial localizer pinpoints each distal tip and outputs a calibrated uncertainty interval.](../reports/figures/fig2_architecture_modules.pdf){#fig:arch width=100%}

## Stage 1: Native-Resolution 3D U-Net with Self-Supervised and Multi-Rater Learning

**Why native resolution and multiple output heads matter.** Many 3D medical segmentation pipelines first resample every scan to coarser, near-isotropic voxels such as $1 \times 1 \times 1\text{ mm}^3$. For a 2 mm catheter imaged at $0.35\text{ mm}$ pixel resolution, resampling blurs the sharp dark core into the surrounding tissue. Conversely, applying a standard $3 \times 3 \times 3$ convolution directly on the native grid mixes features across up to 18 mm vertically ($z$) but only 1 mm horizontally ($x, y$).

Our Stage 1 network (Figure \ref{fig:arch}a) therefore operates directly on the native voxel grid using an anisotropic 3D residual U-Net with squeeze-and-excitation channel attention at four scales (24, 48, 96, and 144 channels). Crucially, it pools spatially *only in-plane* ($x, y$) at the first two scales so that thick axial slices are never compressed early. The network takes six input channels—normalized T2W intensity, Stage 0 dark-core symmetry $S_0$, ring contrast, smoothed intensity, normalized axial height $z$, and radial distance from the scan center—and predicts four complementary outputs:

1. **Tubular mask ($P_{\text{mask}}$):** The probability that a voxel lies within a catheter cross-section (trained with focal binary cross-entropy, up-weighted 19-fold on catheter voxels and on false Stage 0 vessel peaks, plus soft Dice loss).
2. **Sub-pixel centerline peak ($P_{\text{peak}}$):** A narrow 2D Gaussian heatmap ($\sigma = 0.70\text{ mm}$) centered on the exact intersection of each catheter with the slice (trained with peak-weighted mean-squared error). **Why this matters:** When two catheters touch, their binary masks ($P_{\text{mask}}$) merge into one blob, but their two centerline peaks ($P_{\text{peak}}$) remain distinct.
3. **Local 3D direction field ($V_x, V_y = \Delta x/\Delta z, \Delta y/\Delta z$):** The local tilt of the catheter as it passes from slice to slice (trained with smooth $L_1$ loss and an axial consistency penalty), telling the tracker where to look on the next slice.
4. **Obturator segmentation ($P_{\text{obt}}$):** A dedicated mask for the large central vaginal obturator and tandem, preventing their dark plastic rims from generating false needle detections.

**How we teach Stage 1 to ignore blood vessels and noisy single-rater labels.** We introduce two training innovations for Stage 1:

- **Self-supervised pre-training on 51 external pelvic MRI scans with anatomical hard negatives.** Before training on any benchmark brachytherapy patient, we pre-train the 3D U-Net for 45 epochs across the 51 external needle-free pelvic MRI volumes (48 public MSD prostate scans + 3 non-benchmark BWH pelvic scans). In each training step, we digitally inject 8 to 20 realistic synthetic 3D catheter trajectories—curved dark cores with partial-volume bright rims and random signal dropouts—while simultaneously masking random 3D tissue patches for reconstruction. Because 50 of these 51 external scans contain *zero* real catheters, **every real pelvic blood vessel, bowel loop, or fascial border that triggers a high Stage 0 symmetry score ($S_0 > 0.25$) outside the synthetic tubes serves as an anatomical hard negative** (the one prostate implant scan contributes its 14 unlabeled needles as background, which we accept as label noise). The network is thus pushed to separate a plastic catheter void from a pelvic vein before it ever sees the benchmark cohort.
- **Multi-rater consensus supervision.** On the 40 training scans traced by both human expert teams, training on a single team's annotations penalizes the network whenever it finds a real catheter that Team 1 overlooked but Team 2 traced, and passes along single-observer clicking jitter. Within each outer training fold (never touching test patients), we pair matching trajectories between Team 1 and Team 2 ($\le 3.5\text{ mm}$, $n = 465$ pairs) and supervise Stage 1 along their precision-weighted consensus centerline (weight $1.15$). Catheters traced only by Team 1 receive weight $1.00$, and additional catheters traced only by Team 2 ($n = 35$ after excluding $465$ primary-matched and $13$ obturator-overlapping traces; this training-time match pairs each Team 2 trace, clipped to the automatic axial crop, with a Team 1 long shaft only, so its counts differ slightly from the evaluation-time pairing of all unclipped traces of both teams in the cohort description above, 476 pairs) receive soft positive weight $0.55$ instead of being treated as background false alarms.
- **Inner 5-fold ensembling and 4-view test-time augmentation.** Within each outer training fold, we train 5 inner-fold Stage 1 models with rich 3D MRI augmentations (random 3D bias fields, slice-thickness blurring, slice dropouts, and flips) and average their predictions across four flip symmetries ($I, X, Y, XY$) at inference.

## Stage 2: Graph Tracking, Fragment Stitching, Bundle Scoring, and 1D Tip Localization

Once Stage 1 has highlighted candidate catheter centers and directions on every slice, Stage 2 solves three sequential geometric problems (Figure \ref{fig:arch}b):

### Step 2A: Connecting slice detections and stitching split fragments
On each slice, local peaks of $P_{\text{peak}}$ and $S_0$ become graph nodes (each described by 16 local image and direction features). Directed edges connect nodes across 1 to 4 slices (up to $24\text{ mm}$ vertically) whenever their in-plane displacement agrees with the Stage 1 direction field.

Why use a graph network alongside Kalman tracking instead of greedy slice-to-slice linking alone? Because two consecutive links can each look reasonable on their own yet form a sharp, unphysical kink when joined together. First, a fast native C++17/Rust bidirectional Kalman tracker traces candidate 3D paths from the Stage 0 and Stage 1 seeds in both directions and merges near-duplicate paths within $1.65\text{--}2.05\text{ mm}$ while preserving genuinely touching twin catheters. Second, a **4-layer triplet message-passing network** (hidden width 64) scores every node and edge in the slice graph using attention-weighted messages from both its incoming and outgoing neighboring edges—explicitly rewarding smooth, collinear trajectories, refining sub-pixel coordinates, and penalizing kinks.

Finally, our failure analysis of earlier pipelines revealed that mid-shaft signal dropouts (where a catheter crosses a dark vessel or air pocket over 1 to 3 slices) frequently split one physical catheter into a lower fragment and an upper fragment—causing both a false-positive extra fragment and a severe tip undershoot on the lower track. We therefore added a **collinear split-fragment stitcher**: whenever a lower track fragment ends and an upper fragment begins within an axial gap of $\le 20\text{ mm}$, and their linearly extrapolated positions at the midpoint agree within $\le 2.2\text{ mm}$ with matching 3D slopes ($\Delta \text{slope} \le 0.14$), the two fragments are seamlessly stitched into a single full-length candidate (creating 102 stitched full-length trajectories across the 54 patients).

### Step 2B: Judging the whole implant bundle together
After Step 2A, the stitched candidate pool recovers **97.4%** of all real catheters ($98.8\%$ of long shafts), and the greedily linked Stage 2A tracks alone (Table \ref{tbl:main}, Part B) still contain an average of 6.6 unmatched tracks per patient. How can the algorithm tell a real catheter apart from a dark vein that happens to look tubular over a few slices? By looking at the **whole bundle at once**: real catheters enter through a common perineal template corridor, run roughly parallel to the central obturator, maintain a consistent spacing, and terminate along a coherent surface covering the tumor.

We summarize each candidate trajectory by a 26-feature token (length, straightness, Stage 0/1/2A confidence percentiles, entry position and tilt relative to the bundle median, and tip contrast gradient) and describe every pair of trajectories by 8 geometric interaction features (minimum 3D clearance, shared-slice separation, angular divergence, and overlap). A **bundle graph transformer** applies two layers of edge-biased local attention between neighboring tracks followed by global self-attention across the entire candidate bundle, outputting a calibrated probability $p_m \in [0, 1]$ that each candidate is a real catheter. Candidates outside a self-calibrating implant radius (98th percentile of high-confidence tracks plus $16\text{ mm}$) require a higher confidence threshold to pass. To enable zero-shot and few-patient learning, Stage 2B can also be pre-trained on **SynthBrachy3T**—a physics-based generator of 300 simulated candidate bundles containing realistic touching twins, short obturator channels, split fragments, and vascular false alarms—before fine-tuning on the clinical training fold.

### Step 2C: Pinpointing the deepest tip, quantifying its uncertainty, and fusing literature priors
Even when a catheter's shaft is traced to within half a millimeter, finding its exact deepest (cranial) tip on thick-slice T2W MRI is difficult: the dark void tapers off gradually across 2 to 6 mm thick slices, or collides with dark bowel gas just above the uterus.

To resolve the tip with sub-slice precision, we built a **dedicated 1D axial tip localizer** (Figure \ref{fig:arch}b). Starting at the top of each candidate trajectory and continuing along its 3D tangent direction, the network samples a **91-step 1D profile** from $-25.0\text{ mm}$ below the initial tip to $+20.0\text{ mm}$ above it, in fine **$0.5\text{ mm}$ sub-slice steps**. At every $0.5\text{ mm}$ step, it extracts 22 multi-scale measurements: center and ring T2W intensities, local annular contrast at two radii, first and second axial derivatives ($\partial_z, \partial_{zz}$), Stage 0 symmetry $S_0$, Stage 1 mask and peak probabilities, obturator probability, and depth relative to neighboring catheter tips. A **3-block dilated 1D ResNet** (kernel size 5, dilations 1, 2, 4) followed by **4-head 1D self-attention** reads this 91-step sequence and predicts both a 91-bin probability distribution over the exact stopping point and a continuous sub-millimeter offset $\Delta s$; this predicted offset shifts the trajectory's cranial termination point along its 3D tangent vector before the full 3D curve is smoothed with a cubic polynomial in $z$.

Crucially, the sharpness (entropy) of this 91-bin tip distribution, combined with the local image contrast drop and slice thickness $\Delta z$, provides an immediate out-of-fold **tip uncertainty score $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$**. To turn this raw score into a physical error bar in millimeters without assuming Gaussian errors, we use **split-conformal calibration**: on the held-out inner validation patients of each fold, we compute the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile $\hat{q}_{1-\alpha}$ of the normalized non-conformity ratios $r_i = E_{\text{tip}, i} / \hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}, i}$. Multiplying any unseen test tip's uncertainty score by $\hat{q}_{1-\alpha}$ yields an adaptive 3D prediction radius $\hat{r}_{1-\alpha} = \hat{q}_{1-\alpha}\,\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$ that covers the true tip at the target nominal rate (e.g., 90% or 95%) on average, without any Gaussian assumption, provided calibration and test tips are drawn alike. Because tips cluster within patients, we verify this coverage empirically on the outer test folds (Results). The same score ranks which few tips in an implant warrant a quick visual check.

Finally, because recent peer-reviewed MRI brachytherapy studies have proposed complementary post-processing priors—specifically **axial total-variation (TV) continuity** [@dai2020], **iterative multi-segment gap-bridging** [@kostoulas2025], and **5-model epistemic uncertainty gating with inverse-variance-weighted polynomial fitting** [@aleong2024; @kim2026]—we also tested whether integrating these three ideas directly inside Stage 2 (`4d: NeedleFinder 2026 + Literature Fusion` in Table \ref{tbl:main}) improves our pipeline even further:
1. **Sub-voxel dark-core and inverse-variance-weighted cubic-polynomial shaft fitting [@aleong2024; @kim2026]:** On each axial slice $z_k$ along a kept trajectory, we refine the in-plane coordinate toward the precision-weighted sub-voxel centroid of the Stage 1 peak and Stage 0 dark-core response, and weight the cubic polynomial fit by $w_k = (P_{\text{mask}}(z_k) + 0.5\,S_0(z_k)) / (0.04 + \sigma_{\text{mask}}^2(z_k))$, where $\sigma_{\text{mask}}(z_k)$ is the voxel-wise epistemic standard deviation across the 5 inner-fold Stage 1 models.
2. **Directional axial TV continuity and epistemic uncertainty features [@dai2020; @aleong2024]:** We append $\sigma_{\text{mask}}$ and its axial derivative $\partial_z \sigma_{\text{mask}}$ as channels 23 and 24 of the 91-step 1D tip localizer and penalize candidate tracks exhibiting high axial TV jitter or high epistemic uncertainty in Stage 2B.
3. **Iterative multi-segment gap-bridging and polynomial residual split suppression [@kostoulas2025]:** We extend our two-fragment stitcher to iteratively bridge 3-segment trajectories across multi-slice dropouts whenever a single cubic polynomial fits the combined path within a $1.35\text{ mm}$ RMS residual, and suppress lower-confidence collinear sub-segments that lie within $2.6\text{ mm}$ of a longer kept catheter's polynomial fit.

# Evaluation Protocol, Baselines, and Clinical Translation

## Strictly Nested $5 \times 5$ Cross-Validation and Baselines

In small clinical datasets, subtle leaks—such as picking the best training epoch on the test fold, using manual crop boxes, or training a second-stage network on overfitted first-stage predictions—can inflate reported accuracy. We eliminated all three sources of optimism using a **strictly nested $5 \times 5$ patient-level cross-validation protocol** across the 54 patients:

1. **Locked outer test folds and disjoint inner validation.** The 54 patients are partitioned into 5 outer folds ($43\text{--}44$ outer-train patients and $10\text{--}11$ outer-test patients per fold). Inside each outer fold, the outer-train patients are split 80/20 into an inner-training set ($34\text{--}35$ patients) and an inner-validation set ($9$ patients). Every hyperparameter, early-stopping checkpoint, temperature calibration factor, and conformal quantile is selected *exclusively* on the 9 inner-validation patients. Each outer-test patient is evaluated once.
2. **100% reference-free inference.** Every test volume is cropped solely by the automatic Stage 0 template-plane locator, with zero access to clinical scene files or manual annotations.
3. **Eliminating two-stage stacking bias via inner out-of-fold cross-fitting (`4c`).** In any two-stage machine learning pipeline, Stage 1 makes slightly cleaner predictions on the patients it was trained on than on brand-new patients. If Stage 2 is trained directly on those slightly optimistic Stage 1 maps (`4a` and `4b`), it might misjudge how noisy Stage 1 will be at test time. To measure how much stacking bias our pipeline carries, we also trained $5 \times 5 = 25$ inner-fold Stage 1 models so that every training patient receives strictly out-of-fold Stage 1 maps before Stage 2 is trained (reported as configuration `4c` in Table \ref{tbl:main}).

To ensure a thorough, apples-to-apples comparison on the exact same 54-patient nested folds and automatic crop bounds, we re-implemented **five peer-reviewed MRI/curvilinear methods** and **one recent structure-aware tracking preprint** from their published descriptions and trained each under our nested protocol (all six were written in PyTorch from the published descriptions without any original authors' code; the 2019 pipeline is our own earlier method in vectorized form; the numbers below are therefore for our re-implementations):
- **Zaffino *et al.* 2019 (*Physics in Medicine & Biology*, 3D U-Net + DBSCAN) [@zaffino2019]:** Isotropic $1\text{ mm}^3$ resampling, 3D U-Net voxel segmentation, principal-axis clustering with DBSCAN, and spline fitting.
- **Shit *et al.* 2021 (*CVPR*, ResUNet + clDice + Skeleton) [@isensee2021; @shit2021]:** A native-resolution 3D residual U-Net trained with focal cross-entropy, soft Dice, and topology-preserving centerline-Dice (clDice) loss, followed by 3D skeletonization and polynomial fitting.
- **Dai *et al.* 2020 (*Medical Physics*, 3D Attention U-Net + Axial TV + cubic fitting) [@dai2020]:** A deeply supervised 3D attention-gated U-Net trained on T2W MRI with multi-scale Dice/cross-entropy and axial total-variation continuity regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{TV-}z}$), followed by slice-wise centroid linking and cubic polynomial smoothing.
- **Kostoulas *et al.* 2025 (*Proc. SPIE Medical Imaging*, 3D ResUNet + Iterative Gap-Bridging) [@kostoulas2025]:** A native-resolution 3D residual U-Net paired with multi-peak blob splitting, iterative caudo-cranial collinear gap-bridging across segmentation dropouts ($\le 18\text{ mm}$), false-part pruning, and polynomial fitting.
- **Aleong *et al.* 2024 / Kim *et al.* 2026 (*Medical Physics* / *Brachytherapy*, 5-Model Uncertainty Ensemble + Weighted cubic fitting) [@aleong2024; @kim2026]:** Within each outer fold, a 5-model inner 3D U-Net ensemble estimates both mean catheter probability $\bar{p}$ and voxel-wise epistemic uncertainty $\sigma_p$; candidate tracks are extracted on the uncertainty-penalized map, filtered by ensemble disagreement ($\bar{\sigma}_p/\bar{p}$), and smoothed via inverse-variance-weighted cubic polynomial regression.
- **Xiao *et al.* 2026 (Structure-Aware Sync-Track, preprint; see also peer-reviewed JBHI counterpart [@xiao2026jbhi]) [@xiao2026]:** Implant-region gating, direction-constrained 3D Hough transform initialization, synchronous inward slice tracking, and cubic polynomial smoothing, evaluated both on out-of-fold MRI segmentation masks and on ground-truth masks (as an oracle upper bound).

## Geometric, Observer, Dosimetric, and Generalization Metrics

**Geometric matching and error metrics.** For each patient, predicted and reference trajectories are matched one-to-one using the Hungarian algorithm, forbidding pairs whose mean in-plane distance across shared slices exceeds $3.5\text{ mm}$. On matched pairs, we measure **in-plane shaft error** ($E_{\text{shaft}}$, across 40 equidistant shared-slice stations), **3D symmetric curve distance** ($D_{\text{avg}}$), **95th-percentile Hausdorff distance** ($\text{HD}_{95}$), and **3D distal tip error** ($E_{\text{tip}}$, Euclidean distance between predicted and reference cranial endpoints). Our algorithm reconstructs *all* visible plastic tubes in the scan—both the 759 full-depth treatment shafts and the 304 shorter guide tubes inside the vaginal obturator. When we evaluate recall on the 759 full-depth shafts alone, an algorithm prediction that accurately traces one of the 304 real obturator tubes is not a false alarm on a blood vessel; we therefore report **obturator-adjusted precision ($P_{\text{adj}}$) and $F1_{\text{adj}}$**, which avoid penalizing valid detections of those secondary tubes. While Table \ref{tbl:main} summarizes pooled error distributions across all matched catheters, all paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests are conducted at the **patient level** ($N = 54$ paired patient means, or $N = 23$ in the adhesion subgroup) to respect within-patient clustering (assigning fixed worst-case penalties of $3.5\text{ mm}$ shaft, $10\text{ mm}$ curve, and $20\text{ mm}$ tip/$\text{HD}_{95}$ if a baseline yields zero matches on a scan), complemented by 1,000-resample patient-level bootstrap 95% confidence intervals. We report all $p$-values uncorrected for the roughly forty comparisons in this paper. The main benchmark differences ($p \le 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$) survive any standard correction; the smaller effects at $p \approx 0.01\text{--}0.05$ (literature fusion versus the base pipeline, Human Team 2 under Protocol A, and the flipped-reference tip test) would not, and we present them as suggestive.

**Three-cornered-hat observer adjudication and non-inferiority testing.** If an algorithm disagrees with Human Team 1 by $0.5\text{ mm}$, how much of that discrepancy is the algorithm's error and how much is Team 1's own hand-clicking jitter? With only two observers, it is impossible to tell. However, when *three* sources trace the same catheter—Team 1, Team 2, and out-of-fold NeedleFinder 2026 ($n = 432$ three-way matched catheters)—we observe three pairwise difference variances ($V_{12}, V_{13}, V_{23}$) for three unknown internal error variances ($\sigma_1^2, \sigma_2^2, \sigma_3^2$). **Grubbs' (1948) three-cornered-hat estimator** solves this $3 \times 3$ linear system ($\hat{\sigma}_1^2 = \frac{1}{2}(V_{12} + V_{13} - V_{23})$) to isolate each source's intrinsic, reference-free error standard deviation, which we compute via classic maximum likelihood (RMS), outlier-resistant median absolute deviation (MAD), and 15% Winsorized estimators. The estimator assumes that the three sources err independently. This holds only approximately here: NeedleFinder is trained, in other folds, on traces from both teams, so its errors may share their habits. A violated assumption can push one estimated variance below zero, which we then report as 0.00 mm.

We also test whether the algorithm's disagreement with Team 1 is statistically non-inferior to Team 2's disagreement with Team 1 ($H_0: E_{\text{NF},\text{Team 1}} - E_{\text{Team 2},\text{Team 1}} \ge \Delta$ at one-sided significance level $\alpha = 0.05$). Pre-specified clinical non-inferiority margins were set to $\Delta = 0.25\text{ mm}$ on shafts (roughly half the finest pixel spacing) and $\Delta = 1.00\text{ mm}$ (median) / $1.50\text{ mm}$ (mean) on 3D tips (corresponding to half a slice thickness and the $1\text{--}1.5\text{ mm}$ TG-43U1 dosimetric sensitivity threshold [@tanderup2008; @schindel2013]). We evaluate non-inferiority at both the patient level ($N = 40$) and the catheter level ($n = 432$) using one-sided paired Wilcoxon tests and two-sided $90\%$ ($1 - 2\alpha$) patient-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples). Finally, whenever the two human teams disagree on a catheter tip by $\ge 5.0\text{ mm}$ on MRI ($n = 70$ catheters across 38 patients with paired CT), we use the patient's post-implant pelvic CT scan as an independent physical tie-breaker. One caveat applies to every comparison against Team 1: NeedleFinder learned from Team 1's traces on other patients, whereas Team 2 did not, so part of the algorithm's closer agreement with Team 1 may reflect a shared tracing convention rather than higher accuracy. The CT adjudication is the one check that depends on neither team.

**AAPM TG-43U1 HDR $^{192}\text{Ir}$ dosimetry and simulated 1-click tip triage.** To translate millimeter geometry errors into clinical radiation dose differences across all 54 patients without confounding from inter-physician contouring variability (and because historical DICOM RTSTRUCT/RTPLAN files were not archived), we simulated HDR $^{192}\text{Ir}$ delivery using the AAPM TG-43U1 line-source formalism on standardized, implant-conformal anatomical envelopes. Each catheter was loaded with $5.0\text{ mm}$ dwell steps along its cranial $45\text{ mm}$ (starting $3.5\text{ mm}$ below the physical tip, active source length $L = 3.5\text{ mm}$) on a $1.5\text{ mm}$ isotropic 3D dose grid. A single-fraction prescription of $D_{90} = 7.0\text{ Gy}$ was optimized via non-negative least-squares onto the implant-conformal high-risk target volume ($\text{HR-CTV}_{\text{implant}}$, defined as the $8.0\text{ mm}$ radial envelope around the active reference implant) while constraining $2\text{ cm}^3$ proxy shells for the anterior bladder, posterior rectum, and cranial sigmoid/fundal organ-at-risk (OAR) zones. We evaluated two clinical protocols:
- **Protocol A (Fixed-Plan Transfer):** Reference dwell times optimized on the expert catheters are transferred onto the one-to-one matched predicted catheters (with missed reference catheters contributing zero dwell delivery) to isolate pure geometric-to-dosimetric sensitivity.
- **Protocol B (Counterfactual Plan Re-Optimization):** Modeling the end-to-end clinical planning workflow, dwell times are optimized from scratch on *all* algorithm-predicted catheters, including any false-positive tracks that a physicist would normally delete before planning, and the resulting dose is evaluated on the reference target envelope and OAR proxy shells.
- **Simulated 1-click clinician tip review:** Because 98.8% of tip error is longitudinal along the already-reconstructed 3D shaft, we also model an interactive verification step in which the physicist inspects the top $k \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ tips per patient ranked by out-of-fold uncertainty $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$ and clicks the true distal termination along the shaft (modeled by the retrospective 1-click oracle $E_{\text{tip}} \to \sqrt{\Delta r_\perp^2 + \min(|\Delta s_\parallel|, 0.85\text{ mm})^2}$ whenever $E_{\text{tip}} > 2.0\text{ mm}$, leaving $E_{\text{shaft}}$ unchanged, at an assumed, untimed cost of $8\text{ s}$ per flagged tip in 3D Slicer plus $2\text{ s}$ to open the review list). Only the $k = 2$ configuration was carried through the dose engine, where each flagged tip is moved along its shaft to the reference tip depth; larger $k$ were evaluated on tip geometry alone.

**Computing environment.** All training and benchmarks ran on a Linux workstation equipped with a 24-core workstation CPU (AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO 7965WX, 48 threads, 256 GB RAM) and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB). For clinical deployment, the complete pipeline is packaged as a one-click module in **3D Slicer 5.10** [@fedorov2012] using native Rust (Stage 0) and ONNX Runtime (Stages 1 and 2), requiring no GPU.

Four clear lessons emerge from the benchmark:

**1. Pure voxel segmentation fragments catheters and hallucinates vessels.**
The native-resolution `ResUNet + clDice` baseline [@shit2021] detects nearly every catheter location ($92.4\%$ recall) and traces matched segments accurately ($0.43\text{ mm}$ median shaft error). However, because single-slice signal dropouts break catheter masks into pieces and dark pelvic veins are segmented as tubes, it outputs $4,015$ fragments across the 54 scans—yielding a precision of only $24.5\%$ and an F1 score of $38.7\%$.

**2. Domain-specific MRI post-processing (axial TV, gap-bridging, and uncertainty ensembles) cuts false tracks by more than half, but cannot replace physical symmetry or bundle reasoning.**
The three recent peer-reviewed MRI brachytherapy methods substantially improve upon raw clDice skeletonization on native-resolution T2W volumes:
- **Dai *et al.* 2020** (3D Attention U-Net + axial TV regularization [@dai2020]) achieves the highest raw baseline recall (**93.6%**, $995/1,063$; $0.43\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $3.77\text{ mm}$ median / $10.61\text{ mm}$ mean tip error) and cuts output fragments from $4,015$ to $2,358$, raising F1 to **58.2%** ($83.8\%$ $F1_{\text{adj}}$ on long shafts).
- **Kostoulas *et al.* 2025** (3D ResUNet + iterative collinear gap-bridging and false-part pruning [@kostoulas2025]) reconnects broken shaft segments across slice dropouts and prunes isolated fragments, lifting whole-cohort precision to $53.0\%$ and F1 to **66.5%** ($89.5\%$ recall, $0.43\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $3.47\text{ mm}$ median / $10.55\text{ mm}$ mean tip error).
- **Aleong *et al.* 2024 / Kim *et al.* 2026** (5-model epistemic uncertainty ensemble + disagreement filtering and inverse-variance-weighted cubic fitting [@aleong2024; @kim2026]) further suppresses high-uncertainty vascular false alarms, reaching **69.5% whole-cohort F1** ($89.2\%$ recall, $56.9\%$ precision; **81.7% F1** in the severe-adhesion subgroup; $0.44\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $4.32\text{ mm}$ median / $10.23\text{ mm}$ mean tip error).
- Meanwhile, when given ground-truth oracle masks (Table \ref{tbl:main}, Part B, bottom row), the **Xiao *et al.* 2026 Sync-Track** algorithm [@xiao2026] achieves $97.7\%$ recall, $90.0\%$ F1, and $0.11\text{ mm}$ median shaft error—indicating that our re-implementation of synchronous tracking separates touching masks when segmentation is perfect—but drops to **54.2% F1** ($39.2\%$ precision) on real out-of-fold MRI masks. Thus, without Stage 0 dark-core symmetry and Stage 2B whole-bundle context, the three recent native-resolution post-processing pipelines and mask-based tracking remain below $70\%$ whole-cohort F1 because dark pelvic veins inside the implant region look locally tubular, while the isotropic 2019 U-Net + DBSCAN pipeline reaches $83.1\%$ (Table \ref{tbl:main}). The gap is one of precision, not recall: the three newer re-implementations find as many catheters as the 2019 pipeline or more (89.2% to 93.6% vs. 88.8% recall) but emit 1.4 to 1.9 times as many tracks (1,667 to 2,358 vs. 1,210 predictions) from out-of-fold voxel masks of only 0.39 to 0.43 Dice, so their precision falls to 42% to 57% against 78%. Two caveats apply: the 2019 pipeline is our own earlier method, whose isotropic resampling and DBSCAN minimum-cluster rule were developed on this cohort, and the newer methods were re-implemented from their papers without their authors' tuning.

**3. NeedleFinder 2026 achieves the highest F1 score and lowest tip error of any method run on real out-of-fold MRI, in every cohort; adding three literature priors gives small further gains.**
At the default operating threshold ($\tau = 50\%$), NeedleFinder 2026 (base configuration, Table \ref{tbl:main}) detects **91.4% of all 1,063 catheters** ($972/1,063$) with **86.9% precision** ($972/1,118$) and **89.1% F1** (lowering the threshold to $\tau = 35\%$ raises recall to $93.6\%$ but lowers precision to $83.4\%$ and F1 to $88.2\%$). Furthermore, incorporating the three complementary ideas from Dai *et al.* [@dai2020], Kostoulas *et al.* [@kostoulas2025], and Aleong *et al.* / Kim *et al.* [@aleong2024; @kim2026] directly into Stage 2 (`4d: NeedleFinder 2026 + Literature Fusion`) eliminates 6 residual split-fragment false positives with zero lost true positives, lifting precision to **87.4%** ($972/1,112$) and whole-cohort F1 to **89.4%** (a 0.3-point gain, uncorrected $p = 0.028$ vs. base `4b`). Across all 54 patients, NeedleFinder 2026 (`4b`–`4d`) outperforms all six external baselines by **+6.0 to +50.7 F1 points**: $+6.0\text{--}6.3$ points over Zaffino *et al.* 2019 ($83.1\%$ F1, $p = 1.1 \times 10^{-5}$), $+19.6\text{--}19.9$ points over Aleong *et al.* / Kim *et al.* ($69.5\%$ F1, $p = 5.0 \times 10^{-10}$), $+22.6\text{--}22.9$ points over Kostoulas *et al.* 2025 ($66.5\%$ F1, $p = 1.8 \times 10^{-10}$), $+30.9\text{--}31.2$ points over Dai *et al.* 2020 ($58.2\%$ F1, $p = 3.8 \times 10^{-9}$), $+34.9\text{--}35.2$ points over Xiao *et al.* 2026 ($54.2\%$ F1, $p = 3.0 \times 10^{-10}$), and $+50.4\text{--}50.7$ points over ResUNet + clDice ($38.7\%$ F1, $p = 5.2 \times 10^{-10}$).

Along the catheter shaft, NeedleFinder 2026 reaches a **median in-plane error of $0.47\text{ mm}$** (mean $0.59\text{ mm}$ in `4b`; inverse-variance-weighted sub-voxel fitting in `4d` lowers the mean by less than $0.01\text{ mm}$ to $0.58\text{ mm}$ and the 95th percentile to $1.36\text{ mm}$, a consistent but practically negligible change, $p = 1.8 \times 10^{-8}$). At the deepest tip, our 1D axial tip localizer and fragment stitcher cut **median 3D tip error to $2.26\text{--}2.27\text{ mm}$** ($1.94\text{ mm}$ median axial tip error, mean $6.23\text{ mm}$), reducing mean tip error by $39\%\text{--}42\%$ compared with Zaffino *et al.* 2019 ($10.75\text{ mm}$ mean / $3.39\text{ mm}$ median, $p = 1.7 \times 10^{-8}$), Dai *et al.* 2020 ($10.61\text{ mm}$ mean / $3.77\text{ mm}$ median, $p = 2.7 \times 10^{-7}$), Kostoulas *et al.* 2025 ($10.55\text{ mm}$ mean / $3.47\text{ mm}$ median, $p = 6.9 \times 10^{-7}$), and Aleong *et al.* / Kim *et al.* ($10.23\text{ mm}$ mean / $4.32\text{ mm}$ median, $p = 3.3 \times 10^{-7}$), and by $60\%$ compared with Xiao *et al.* 2026 ($15.65\text{ mm}$ mean / $6.04\text{ mm}$ median, $p = 6.2 \times 10^{-10}$). Nearly three-fifths (**58.7%–59.0%**) of matched catheters have a 3D tip error within $\le 3.0\text{ mm}$ (roughly one slice thickness). On the **759 full-depth active-dwell shafts**, NeedleFinder 2026 reaches **91.3% recall**, **88.7%–89.0% obturator-adjusted precision**, and **90.0%–90.1% $F1_{\text{adj}}$**, with a median shaft error of **$0.44\text{--}0.45\text{ mm}$** (mean $0.56\text{--}0.57\text{ mm}$) and a median tip error of **$2.39\text{--}2.41\text{ mm}$**.

**4. The advantage is largest when catheters touch, and inner cross-fitting shows no measurable stacking bias.**
On the **23-patient severe catheter-adhesion subgroup** (571 catheters), where touching masks cause DBSCAN clustering and greedy trackers to merge or swap paths, NeedleFinder 2026 reaches **91.3% F1** ($93.2\%$ recall, $89.6\%$ precision, $0.46\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $2.50\text{--}2.51\text{ mm}$ median tip error), outperforming Zaffino *et al.* 2019 ($86.3\%$ F1, $p = 0.0012$), Aleong *et al.* / Kim *et al.* ($81.7\%$ F1, $p = 2.9 \times 10^{-5}$), Kostoulas *et al.* 2025 ($78.6\%$ F1, $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-6}$), Dai *et al.* 2020 ($67.5\%$ F1, $p = 5.2 \times 10^{-5}$), Xiao *et al.* 2026 ($63.9\%$ F1, $p = 4.8 \times 10^{-7}$), and ResUNet + clDice ($48.4\%$ F1, $p = 4.8 \times 10^{-7}$). Moreover, training Stage 2 on strictly out-of-fold inner Stage 1 maps (`4c` in Table \ref{tbl:main}) yields the exact same **89.1% whole-cohort F1** ($92.3\%$ recall) and even slightly higher accuracy on long shafts (**90.5% $F1_{\text{adj}}$**) and touching catheters (**91.8% F1**), showing that two-stage stacking optimism does not inflate our headline results.

## How Each Architectural Stage Contributes, and Learning from Few Patients

![**(a) Precision–recall progression** across pipeline stages, published baselines, and the NeedleFinder 2026 confidence threshold sweep on all 54 patients. Dashed grey curves denote constant F1 scores; the vermilion star marks the default threshold $\tau = 50\%$ with literature fusion (89.4% F1). **(b) Leak-free clinical label efficiency:** F1 score (with 95% bootstrap confidence bands) as a function of the number of annotated clinical training patients ($k \in \{0, 1, 5, 10, 20, 43\}$) used to fine-tune the Stage 2B bundle transformer.](../reports/figures/fig4_pareto_and_fewshot.pdf){#fig:pareto width=100%}

**Why every stage is needed (Table \ref{tbl:main}, Part B; Figure \ref{fig:pareto}a).** Following the sequential rows of Table \ref{tbl:main} (Part B) shows how each mechanism solves a specific limitation of the stage before it:
- **Stage 0 alone (physical dark-core filter):** Using zero learned parameters, Stage 0 with automatic template cropping already finds $82.8\%$ of all catheters ($95.3\%$ of long shafts) with $69.1\%$ precision (**75.3% F1**, $0.47\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $3.08\text{ mm}$ median / $9.91\text{ mm}$ mean tip error). However, it cannot distinguish round blood vessels from catheters.
- **Adding Stage 1B (self-supervised, multi-rater 3D U-Net):** Regressing sub-pixel centerline peaks and 3D directions boosts recall to **94.2%** ($97.0\%$ on long shafts) and cuts mean tip error to $9.29\text{ mm}$ ($2.95\text{ mm}$ median), but because every candidate seed is still tracked greedily, each patient carries $11.6$ false vascular tracks on average ($61.5\%$ precision, $74.4\%$ F1).
- **Adding Stage 2A (triplet message-passing graph network):** Penalizing kinks across consecutive slice links eliminates $43\%$ of false tracks while keeping **93.9% recall**, lifting precision by $+12.0$ percentage points to $73.5\%$ (**82.5% F1**, $8.95\text{ mm}$ mean / $2.83\text{ mm}$ median tip error).
- **Adding Stage 2B (bundle graph transformer, multi-rater supervision, and collinear fragment stitching):** Evaluating the candidate bundle as a whole ($87.1\%$ F1 with temperature calibration; $86.4\%$ F1 with multi-rater Stage 2 supervision) and stitching collinear fragments separated by 1-to-3-slice dropouts raises precision to **86.9%** and F1 to **89.1%** ($91.4\%$ recall) while reducing mean tip error to $6.92\text{ mm}$ ($2.56\text{ mm}$ median).
- **Adding Stage 2C (dedicated 1D axial tip localizer, `4b`):** Inspecting the $0.5\text{ mm}$ sub-slice axial profile at the cranial end of each trajectory cuts mean 3D tip error from $6.92\text{ mm}$ down to **$6.23\text{ mm}$** and median tip error from $2.56\text{ mm}$ down to **$2.27\text{ mm}$** (95th percentile reduced to $27.27\text{ mm}$).
- **Adding Literature-Inspired Fusion (`4d`: sub-voxel inverse-variance cubic fitting + epistemic uncertainty & axial TV continuity + iterative multi-segment gap-bridging):** Each borrowed idea nudges its targeted metric in the expected direction under strictly nested cross-validation, by amounts well below one pixel: inverse-variance-weighted sub-voxel cubic fitting [@aleong2024; @kim2026] reduces mean shaft error from $0.589\text{ mm}$ to **$0.583\text{ mm}$** ($p = 1.84 \times 10^{-8}$; long-shaft median $0.45 \to 0.44\text{ mm}$); epistemic uncertainty & axial TV continuity features [@dai2020; @aleong2024] reduce median 3D tip error to **$2.26\text{ mm}$** (axial tip median $1.99 \to 1.94\text{ mm}$, tips $\le 3\text{ mm}$ $58.7\% \to 59.0\%$); and iterative multi-segment gap-bridging & polynomial split suppression [@kostoulas2025] eliminate 6 residual split-fragment false alarms, raising precision to **87.4%** and F1 to **89.4%** ($p = 0.028$).

**How many annotated clinical patients are needed to train the bundle scorer? (Figure \ref{fig:pareto}b).** Because annotating 3D catheter bundles is labor-intensive for a new clinic, we varied the number of annotated training patients $k \in \{0, 1, 5, 10, 20, 43\}$ used to fine-tune Stage 2B inside each fold (with zero blending from any full-cohort model). With **zero clinical bundle labels ($k = 0$)**, pre-training Stage 2B purely on our synthetic `SynthBrachy3T` bundles lifts whole-cohort F1 from $75.3\%$ (Stage 0) to **78.2% F1**—and reaches **83.4% $F1_{\text{adj}}$ on long shafts** ($+6.3$ points over Stage 0). Fine-tuning on just **$k = 1$ patient** reaches **79.7% F1**; **$k = 5$ patients reach 83.8% F1 ($86.5\%$ on long shafts), already matching the 43-patient fully supervised PMB 2019 U-Net baseline ($83.1\%$ F1; a 0.7-point difference we do not claim as significant)**; $k = 10$ reaches **85.7% F1**; $k = 20$ reaches **86.6% F1**; and all $k = 43$ training patients reach **89.1% F1** ($88.7\%$ without `SynthBrachy3T`, showing that synthetic bundle pre-training provides its largest boost in the zero-to-few-patient regime while still improving dosimetric tip accuracy at full training size).

## Is the Algorithm as Accurate as a Second Human Expert? (Three-Cornered-Hat & CT Adjudication)

\input{tables/tab3_interobserver.tex}

Because a 2 mm catheter is only a few pixels wide and slices are up to 6 mm thick, two human experts tracing the same MRI scan never agree perfectly [@haack2009; @kallis2019; @karius2024]. A common benchmark for an automatic method is that its discrepancies from a human expert be no larger than the discrepancies *between* two independent human experts [@hrinivich2019; @andersen2020]; meeting it is necessary for clinical use but does not by itself establish it. Table \ref{tbl:interobserver} tests this across **all 40 doubly traced clinical scans** using four complementary analyses:

**1. Direct comparison against human inter-observer agreement (Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}, top two blocks).**
When Expert Team 2 (460 long traces) is compared directly against Expert Team 1 (505 long traces) across the 40 scans, 424 long catheters match within $3.5\text{ mm}$ ($84.0\%$ recall, $92.2\%$ precision, $87.9\%$ F1). Between these two human teams, the **in-plane shaft discrepancy is $0.49\text{ mm}$ median** ($0.70 \pm 0.58\text{ mm}$ mean, $1.90\text{ mm}$ P95) and the **3D tip discrepancy is $3.24\text{ mm}$ median** ($6.28 \pm 7.29\text{ mm}$ mean, $21.88\text{ mm}$ P95).

Evaluated strictly out-of-fold on the exact same 40 scans against Team 1 (723 tracks), **NeedleFinder 2026 achieves a smaller shaft discrepancy ($0.47\text{ mm}$ median, $0.58 \pm 0.39\text{ mm}$ mean, $1.33\text{ mm}$ P95) and a smaller 3D tip discrepancy ($2.10\text{ mm}$ median, $5.26 \pm 8.78\text{ mm}$ mean, $19.99\text{ mm}$ P95) than the disagreement between the two human teams** (the human comparison is restricted to long shafts while the algorithm's includes obturator tracks; the paired comparison on identical catheters follows below), while matching $90.3\%$ of Team 1's trajectories ($87.6\%$ F1). Furthermore, when evaluated against the **union of both human teams** (759 distinct trajectories), **16 NeedleFinder predictions that looked like "false alarms" against Team 1 alone turn out to be real catheters independently traced by Team 2**, lifting precision by $+2.1$ percentage points to $87.1\%$.

**2. Reference-free Grubbs' three-cornered-hat intrinsic error estimation (Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}, third block).**
To measure each source's true internal error without treating Team 1 as infallible, we applied Grubbs' three-cornered-hat estimator across the $n = 432$ catheters matched three-way among Team 1, Team 2, and NeedleFinder 2026 (formed by intersecting the 476 all-catheter human pairs with out-of-fold NeedleFinder predictions within $3.5\text{ mm}$). The intrinsic outlier-resistant (MAD-scaled) in-plane shaft standard deviations are **$0.35\text{ mm}$ for Human Team 1**, **$0.39\text{ mm}$ for NeedleFinder 2026**, and **$0.48\text{ mm}$ for Human Team 2**. For axial tip stopping height ($\sigma_z$), NeedleFinder 2026 achieves a lower intrinsic error than Human Team 2 under both the robust MAD estimator (**$2.98\text{ mm}$ vs. $3.55\text{ mm}$**, where Team 1's estimate collapses to the $0.00\text{ mm}$ lower bound (bootstrap 95% CI $0.0$ to $2.0\text{ mm}$) because its axial disagreements with NeedleFinder 2026 and with Team 2 together barely exceed the disagreement between Team 2 and NeedleFinder 2026, so the subtraction returns a non-positive variance; this is the signature of correlated errors between two sources, expected here because NeedleFinder 2026 was fine-tuned toward Team 1's tip conventions, so we read these axial-tip values as a ranking rather than as exact error bars) and the 15% Winsorized estimator, which yields strictly positive intrinsic standard deviations of **$3.77\text{ mm}$ for Team 1**, **$4.94\text{ mm}$ for NeedleFinder 2026**, and **$5.09\text{ mm}$ for Team 2**.

**3. Formal one-sided statistical non-inferiority (Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}, fourth block).**
Across all $N = 40$ dual-traced patients ($n = 432$ three-way matched catheters), NeedleFinder 2026 passes formal one-sided non-inferiority testing relative to human inter-observer disagreement on every pre-specified endpoint at both the patient level and the catheter level. Specifically, the algorithm's error against Team 1 is smaller than Team 2's error against Team 1 on **shaft mean** ($0.56\text{ vs. }0.72\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 1.3 \times 10^{-8}$), **shaft median** ($0.43\text{ vs. }0.49\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 8.2 \times 10^{-10}$), **3D tip median** ($2.33\text{ vs. }3.18\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 2.6 \times 10^{-7}$), and **3D tip mean** ($5.51\text{ vs. }6.10\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 1.1 \times 10^{-4}$, bootstrap $p_{\text{boot}} = 0.0014$; full 90% confidence intervals and catheter-level $p$-values in Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}). When the reference role is flipped so that Team 2 serves as the reference, the result is weaker. On 3D tip median the algorithm passes at the catheter level ($p_{\text{cath}} = 9.6 \times 10^{-5}$) and by patient-clustered bootstrap ($p_{\text{boot}} = 0.046$) but not by the patient-level Wilcoxon test ($p_{\text{pt}} = 0.073$), and on 3D tip mean it does not pass ($7.53$ vs. $6.10\text{ mm}$, $p_{\text{pt}} = 0.12$). This asymmetry is expected, because Stage 1 was fine-tuned toward Team 1's tracing conventions, and it means the non-inferiority claim holds against the team whose conventions the model learned.

**4. Independent adjudication on paired post-implant Pelvic CT scans.**
On CT, catheters and metallic stylets have unmistakable Hounsfield contrast. Using the 38 patients who had both intraoperative MRI and post-implant Pelvic CT, we adjudicated the $n = 70$ catheters where Human Team 1 and Human Team 2 disagreed on the MRI tip position by $\ge 5.0\text{ mm}$. Measured against the independent CT tip position, **NeedleFinder 2026 achieved a median 3D tip error of $4.20\text{ mm}$**, comparable to **Human Team 1 ($4.31\text{ mm}$; difference not tested)** and smaller than **Human Team 2 ($8.00\text{ mm}$)**, and lying closer to the CT tip position than the less accurate of the two human teams on **78.6% ($55/70$)** of disputed catheters. Across all 100 MRI tracks predicted by NeedleFinder 2026 on these 38 patients that did not match Team 1, **52.0% ($52/100$) corresponded to a physical catheter on the paired CT scan** (whether each was deliberately left untraced on MRI cannot be determined retrospectively).

## Interpretability, Confidence Calibration, and Physical Causes of Residual Errors

![**(a) Confidence calibration** across all 1,960 stitched candidate tracks in the 54 patients, before (dashed) and after (solid vermilion) inner-fold temperature scaling. **(b) Reason-coded filtering decisions** at the default threshold $\tau = 50\%$, showing how many candidates are kept or pruned by each rule and whether pruned tracks were false alarms, redundant duplicates of an already-kept catheter, or lost real catheters.](../reports/figures/fig7_calibration_and_reasons.pdf){#fig:calib width=100%}

For a clinician to trust an automated reconstruction, its confidence scores must be well calibrated and its filtering decisions transparent. Across all $1,960$ stitched candidate tracks entering Stage 2B ($36.3$ per patient), inner-fold temperature scaling reduces the expected calibration error (ECE) from $0.101$ to **$0.059$** (and to **$0.056$** across the $1,118$ accepted tracks at $\tau = 50\%$, where mean predicted confidence is $91.9\%$ and closest-reference precision is $90.8\%$; Figure \ref{fig:calib}a). Every discarded track is tagged with an explicit reason code (Figure \ref{fig:calib}b): at $\tau = 50\%$, $673$ candidates are removed for falling below the confidence threshold (eliminating $80.6\%$ of all false candidates) and $169$ are removed as duplicates of an already-kept track (suppressing $153$ redundant collinear or touching fragments at the cost of only $2$ lost catheters).

To understand the exact physical limits of the pipeline, we audited every single missed catheter, false positive, and $>10\text{ mm}$ tip error across the 54 patients:

- **What causes the 146 unmatched predictions ($2.7$ per patient)?** Nearly a third (**31.5%**, $46/146$) are simply residual split or near-duplicate segments of an already-detected catheter, **20.5% ($30/146$)** correspond to real catheters supported by Expert Team 2 within $5.0\text{ mm}$ (and, among the 100 unmatched predictions on the 38 CT-paired patients, $52.0\%$ correspond to a catheter on CT), and $2.7\%$ ($4/146$) lie within $3.5\text{--}6.0\text{ mm}$ of an unmatched reference track. Only **45.2% ($66/146$, or $1.22$ per patient)** are genuine background false alarms ($>6.0\text{ mm}$ from any reference catheter), occurring along dark pelvic vessels or fascial intersections.
- **What causes the 91 missed catheters ($1.7$ per patient)?** Before Stage 2B thresholding, our $1,960$-track candidate pool actually contains **97.4% ($1,035/1,063$)** of all reference catheters within $3.5\text{ mm}$ (and **98.8% [$750/759$]** of the long active-dwell shafts). Thus, fewer than a third of missed catheters (**30.8%, $28/91$**) are lost upstream due to severe multi-slice signal dropouts. The remaining **69.2% ($63/91$)** are successfully traced by Stages 1 and 2A but filtered out in Stage 2B—most often because their bundle confidence falls just below the $\tau = 50\%$ threshold ($46$ catheters) or because they lose one-to-one matching inside a tight cluster of touching needles ($15$ catheters). Lowering the threshold to $\tau = 35\%$ recovers more than a quarter of these misses, raising whole-cohort recall to **93.6%** and long-shaft recall to **94.7%**, at the cost of more false tracks (precision $83.4\%$, F1 $88.2\%$).
- **Why do some tips have errors $> 10\text{ mm}$?** By decomposing every 3D tip error vector into its component *along* the catheter shaft ($\Delta s_\parallel$) and its component *sideways* perpendicular to the shaft ($\Delta r_\perp$), we found a physical result: **98.8% of total squared 3D tip error across all 972 matched catheters (and 99.3% in the $>10\text{ mm}$ tail) is purely longitudinal along the catheter track**, whereas sideways error at the tip is tiny ($0.72\text{ mm}$ median). In other words, the algorithm almost never loses the catheter's sideways path; when a tip error exceeds $10\text{ mm}$ ($162$ catheters, $16.7\%$), it is almost always an axial stopping ambiguity caused by mid-shaft signal dropout ($41.4\%$), dark bowel/air pockets sitting directly atop the catheter tip ($25.3\%$), intrinsic human inter-rater tip ambiguity $>6\text{ mm}$ ($16.7\%$), crowded tip arrays ($12.3\%$), or top-of-scan truncation ($4.3\%$).

Finally, we asked four practical questions that determine whether an algorithm is genuinely useful in clinical radiation oncology (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization} and Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}):

### Does geometric accuracy translate into accurate radiation dose delivery? (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part A; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}a)
Not all catheter errors matter equally to the radiation dose: a tip error on a peripheral catheter carrying almost zero dwell time has little effect, whereas a tip error on a heavily weighted catheter inside the tumor shifts the target dose substantially. Across our 54 patients, **dwell-time-weighted 3D tip error** ($E_{\text{tip}}^{\text{dwell-w}}$—the average 3D tip error across a patient's catheters weighted by each catheter's share of total radiation dwell time) correlates more strongly with target dose error $|\Delta D_{90}|$ than unweighted tip error does (Spearman $\rho = 0.863$ vs. $0.786$ across the 54 patients; we did not test the difference between the two correlations).

NeedleFinder 2026 cuts dwell-weighted tip error nearly in half compared with PMB 2019 ($5.72\text{ mm}$ vs. $10.51\text{ mm}$ mean; $4.66\text{ mm}$ vs. $8.36\text{ mm}$ median), and is lower than Human Team 2's on the 40 dual-traced scans ($5.18\text{ mm}$ vs. $6.27\text{ mm}$ mean; difference not tested). Consequently:
- Under **Protocol A (transferring a fixed dwell plan)**, NeedleFinder 2026 cuts mean target dose error $|\Delta D_{90}|$ from $27.6\%$ (PMB 2019) to **15.2%** (median **11.5%**, $p = 4.9 \times 10^{-6}$) and achieves a **76.6% global 3D $\gamma(2\%/2\text{ mm})$ pass rate** (**84.3%** at $\gamma(3\%/3\text{ mm})$)—and lower error than Human Team 2 on the 40 dual-traced scans ($13.5\%$ vs. $21.6\%$ $|\Delta D_{90}|$, uncorrected $p = 0.028$; $79.0\%$ vs. $68.1\%$ $\gamma_{2\%/2\text{mm}}$, uncorrected $p = 0.011$; suggestive, see Methods).
- Under **Protocol B (clinical workflow: re-optimizing dwell weights on the predicted catheters)**, dwell optimization compensates for minor longitudinal shifts, cutting unreviewed target $|\Delta D_{90}|$ to **8.2% mean / 4.2% median** (vs. $37.1\% / 10.2\%$ for Zaffino *et al.* 2019; on the 40 dual-traced scans, $7.0\% / 3.2\%$ for NeedleFinder 2026 vs. $20.9\% / 4.0\%$ for Human Team 2). When a simulated physicist corrects just the **top 2 uncertainty-flagged tips per patient** (each moved to its reference depth), target $|\Delta D_{90}|$ drops to **5.9% mean / 2.8% median** (paired Wilcoxon $p = 2.4 \times 10^{-5}$; improved in 37 of 54 patients, worse in 8, unchanged in 9), and the global $\gamma_{2\%/2\text{mm}}$ pass rate rises from $66.0\%$ to $74.3\%$ ($p = 9.5 \times 10^{-7}$; $78.2\%$ on the 40 dual-traced scans). Gamma against the reference plan is lower under Protocol B than under Protocol A because a re-optimized plan is a different dose distribution, not a worse one.
- **Organ-at-risk proxy doses under Protocol B.** Because the re-optimized plan is a different plan, dose to the proxy organ shells moves more than the target dose does. Across the 54 patients, the unreviewed $|\Delta D_{2\text{cc}}|$ on the bladder, rectum, and sigmoid proxy shells is $17.5\%$, $18.3\%$, and $12.5\%$ median ($24.6\%$, $26.2\%$, and $29.5\%$ mean); correcting the two most uncertain tips lowers these to $13.7\%$, $13.1\%$, and $10.1\%$ median ($17.8\%$, $19.4\%$, and $22.5\%$ mean). These shells sit 5.5 to 16 mm from the implant, in the steepest part of the $^{192}\text{Ir}$ dose gradient, and are not patient anatomy, so the percentages are not clinical $D_{2\text{cc}}$ values. On the same shells and the same 40 dual-traced scans, Human Team 2's traces give $19.9\%$ (bladder) and $25.2\%$ (rectum) median against NeedleFinder 2026's $14.3\%$ and $11.4\%$. Under Protocol A (fixed plan transferred), the bladder and rectum proxy differences are $3.8\%$ and $5.4\%$ median (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}).

### Can calibrated uncertainty intervals guide a 1-minute clinician tip check? (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Parts B1–B2; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}b)
Because 98.8% of tip error lies longitudinally along the already-traced shaft, correcting an uncertain tip in 3D Slicer requires only sliding the endpoint along the track (a single click, which we assume takes 8 seconds). Our out-of-fold tip uncertainty score $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$ identifies $>10\text{ mm}$ tip outliers with an **AUROC of 0.805**, and our nested **split-conformal 3D prediction intervals** achieve close-to-nominal empirical coverage across all 54 unseen test patients despite within-patient clustering (**79.3%** at 80% nominal, **84.4%** at 85%, **90.2% coverage at the 90% nominal level**, and **95.4% at the 95% level**, with $31.5\%\text{--}35.1\%$ tighter median error bars than unscaled intervals; Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part B2).

In our simulated clinician-in-the-loop workflow where a physicist inspects the top $k$ highest-uncertainty tips per patient and clicks the true distal termination (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part B1; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}b):
- Reviewing **$k = 2$ tips per patient** (18 s modeled, including 2 s to open the review list) lowers mean tip error from $6.23\text{ mm}$ to **$5.07\text{ mm}$** ($1.95\text{ mm}$ median).
- Reviewing **$k = 5$ tips per patient** (42 s modeled) cuts $>10\text{ mm}$ tip outliers in half ($16.7\% \to 8.0\%$) and lowers mean tip error to **$3.72\text{ mm}$** ($1.67\text{ mm}$ median).
- Reviewing **$k = 8$ tips per patient** (66 s modeled) brings the **95th-percentile tip error across the entire 972-catheter cohort below $10\text{ mm}$ ($9.90\text{ mm}$ vs. $27.27\text{ mm}$ unreviewed)** and reduces mean tip error to **$2.77\text{ mm}$** ($1.51\text{ mm}$ median).

### Does the method generalize zero-shot to other organs and MRI sequences? (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part C; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}c)
To test whether NeedleFinder 2026 overfits to gynecologic T2W MRI, we ran the gynecologic-trained models **zero-shot (with zero retraining)** across three new settings:
- **Cross-organ transfer to 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI (`Case 100`, 14 plastic needles):** This prostate scan is one of the 51 external volumes used, without labels, for Stage 1 pre-training, so we first evaluate a variant pre-trained without it (the zero-exposure row of Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part C). With automatic template cropping, that variant found **13 of 14 prostate catheters with no false alarms (96.3% F1)**, with **$0.33\text{ mm}$ median shaft error** and **$0.99\text{ mm}$ median 3D tip error**; with the clinician's manual crop it found all 14. The full pipeline, whose pre-training had seen this scan without labels, found all 14 under automatic cropping (errors in Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}). **Why does it transfer on this scan?** When we zeroed out the Stage 0 physical symmetry channels ($X_{1:3}=0$), prostate detection collapsed to **0% (0/14)**, whereas the 1-channel `ResUNet + clDice` baseline hallucinated 11 false vascular tracks ($56.0\%$ precision, $71.8\%$ F1). Thus, on this single scan, the Stage 0 physical symmetry channels carried the transfer from gynecologic to prostate anatomy.
- **Secondary MRI pulse sequences (63 of the 243 secondary scans evaluated):** On 3D balanced steady-state free precession (`3D wbSSFP / True-FISP`), where tissue contrast differs markedly from TSE T2W, zero-shot NeedleFinder 2026 achieved **97.9% recall ($46/47$), 88.5% precision, and 92.9% F1** ($0.72\text{ mm}$ median shaft error, $3.40\text{ mm}$ median tip error vs. $10.62\text{ mm}$ for PMB 2019). On 49 thick-slice 2D axial T2W scans ($\Delta z = 2.4\text{--}6.0\text{ mm}$), Z-upsampling to $1.6\text{ mm}$ recovered **88.4% ($780/882$) of physically visible catheters** ($76.9\%$ F1 vs. $57.6\%$ for clDice). On the third secondary sequence we evaluated, 12 post-contrast axial T1 VIBE scans from 2 patients (217 catheters), zero-shot F1 fell to $56.1\%$ ($58.5\%$ recall, $53.8\%$ precision, $8.08\text{ mm}$ median tip error), below Stage 0 alone ($61.5\%$) and the 2019 U-Net + DBSCAN re-implementation ($59.4\%$): post-contrast T1 catheter contrast lies outside what the pipeline was trained on.
- **Zero-shot tracking on post-implant Pelvic CT (40 CT volumes, 38 paired patients):** Extending Stage 0 to detect both bright metallic stylets and dark air lumens in Hounsfield units enabled zero-shot CT reconstruction across 801 MRI-paired reference catheters, matching **68.9% at $\le 3.5\text{ mm}$ and 79.2% at $\le 5.0\text{ mm}$** across non-rigid MRI-to-CT anatomical deformation ($2.01\text{ mm}$ median shaft registration residual).

### Can we track needles across serial scans during insertion and measure tissue biomechanics in 4D? (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}, Part C; Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}c, d)
In 4 patients (`Cases 68–71`), 23 sequential T2W scans were acquired *during* the implant procedure as needles were progressively inserted (5 to 7 timepoints per patient, $1.31\text{ s}$ mean monitoring runtime per scan). Evaluating each patient's 4D series strictly with the outer-fold model where that patient was held out in the locked test vault (`Case 068` in Fold 4, `Case 069` in Fold 0, `Case 070` in Fold 1, `Case 071` in Fold 2), coupling NeedleFinder 2026 with a **causal 4D Kalman filter** that propagates catheter states forward in time boosted **pooled precision across the 108 catheters by +12.4 percentage points ($76.0\% \to 88.4\%$)**, reduced mean 3D tip error from $11.23\text{ to }9.75\text{ mm}$, and improved pooled F1 from **81.6% to 82.8%**, a difference we did not test given only 4 patients (reaching **93.3% F1** on `Case 070`, up from $85.7\%$ with independent 3D tracking, while trading single-frame recall on newly inserted final-frame needles in `Case 071` for $100.0\%$ precision).

Tracking $212$ persisted catheter transitions across these sequential insertions, measured on the automatically tracked catheters rather than on expert traces, also suggested two biomechanical patterns in vivo (Figure \ref{fig:clinical_gen}d):
- **Inter-catheter "tissue push":** Inserting a new needle appears to push surrounding soft tissue aside. Already-implanted catheters located within $\le 10\text{ mm}$ of a newly inserted needle shifted sideways by a **median of $1.44\text{ mm}$** (mean $1.61\text{ mm}$, 90th percentile $2.81\text{ mm}$), compared with **$1.10\text{ mm}$** at $10\text{--}20\text{ mm}$ distance and **$1.00\text{ mm}$** beyond $20\text{ mm}$ (Mann–Whitney test of catheters within $12\text{ mm}$ versus beyond $12\text{ mm}$, $p = 9.97 \times 10^{-4}$; the 212 transitions come from 4 patients and are treated as independent, so this $p$-value is optimistic).
- **Depth-dependent needle bending:** Deviation of a catheter from its initial entry angle grows non-linearly with insertion depth, rising from below measurement resolution at $0\text{--}30\text{ mm}$ depth and $0.15\text{ mm}$ at $30\text{--}60\text{ mm}$ to **$1.22\text{ mm}$ at $60\text{--}90\text{ mm}$** and **$3.29\text{ mm}$ median ($4.56\text{ mm}$ mean, $10.49\text{ mm}$ P90) beyond $90\text{ mm}$ depth** (Spearman $\rho = +0.555$ across depth samples pooled from 4 patients, nominal $p = 5.6 \times 10^{-9}$)—one reason to expect linear extrapolation of the entry angle to be unreliable on deep pelvic implants.

## Why Combining Physics, Peak Regression, and Graph Reasoning Works

Table \ref{tbl:literature} places NeedleFinder 2026 in the context of published catheter digitization methods across CT, ultrasound, and MRI. On CT and ultrasound, metallic stylets, air lumens, or specular reflections create strong contrast against soft tissue, allowing voxel segmentation networks to reach $0.1\text{ to }0.7\text{ mm}$ shaft errors [@zhang2020pmb; @andersen2020; @weishaupt2022; @quetin2025; @xiao2026]. On clinical T2W MRI without exogenous contrast markers, a plastic catheter is a 2 mm dark proton-density and susceptibility void imaged across 1.2 to 6.0 mm slices. Prior MRI studies reported $0.74\text{ to }0.97\text{ mm}$ shaft errors on gynecologic implants using positive-contrast markers or CT-assisted ground truth [@shaaer2022; @kim2026] and $0.75\text{ to }0.93\text{ mm}$ on prostate implants [@dai2020; @kostoulas2025]. NeedleFinder 2026 reaches a median shaft error below half a millimeter directly on standard T2W MRI on the 972 matched catheters of a 1,063-catheter cohort (Table \ref{tbl:main}), although these are different cohorts and reference standards, so the comparison is indicative only.

Our controlled ablations and peer-reviewed baseline reproductions explain why neither voxel segmentation [@zaffino2019; @isensee2021; @shit2021], nor 1-channel MRI post-processing [@dai2020; @kostoulas2025; @aleong2024; @kim2026], nor mask-based tracking [@xiao2026] suffices on its own on T2W MRI, and why each part of our three-stage design is needed:
1. **Local physical symmetry before deep learning:** Stage 0 suppresses one-sided anatomical borders analytically and provides a representation that, on one prostate implant scan and 48 needle-free scans, let a gynecologic network transfer zero-shot without hallucinating blood vessels.
2. **Centerline peaks and hard-negative pre-training instead of binary masks:** By pre-training Stage 1 on 51 external pelvic MRI scans where real blood vessels and fascia are penalized as hard negatives alongside synthetic catheter voids, and by predicting sharp sub-pixel Gaussian peaks ($P_{\text{peak}}$) supervised by two expert teams, touching catheters stay separate instead of merging into a single blob.
3. **Graph message passing, fragment stitching, and bundle-level context:** Rather than trusting local voxel connectivity, Stage 2A enforces smooth triplet collinearity and stitches fragments across 1-to-3-slice signal dropouts, while the Stage 2B graph transformer judges every candidate trajectory against the geometry of the entire 15-to-30-catheter bundle.
4. **Dedicated 1D axial tip profiling and literature fusion:** Because 98.8% of tip error is longitudinal along the shaft, sampling a fine $0.5\text{ mm}$ multi-channel profile along the cranial trajectory tangent allows a 1D ResNet + self-attention localizer to pinpoint where the catheter void terminates and to quantify its own uncertainty—while incorporating sub-voxel inverse-variance-weighted polynomial fitting [@aleong2024; @kim2026], directional axial TV continuity [@dai2020], and iterative multi-segment gap-bridging [@kostoulas2025] refines shaft and tip accuracy and suppresses residual split fragments (`4d`, **89.4% F1**).

## How NeedleFinder 2026 Changes the Clinical Workflow in 3D Slicer

Packaged as a one-click module in **3D Slicer** [@fedorov2012], NeedleFinder 2026 runs in a few seconds on a 24-core workstation CPU or a GPU when its production Rust and ONNX Runtime code path is run headless, outside the Slicer interface (Figure \ref{fig:spec_runtime}c). Instead of spending 45 to 50 minutes manually clicking 15 to 30 catheters slice by slice while the patient waits with the implant in place, the medical physicist follows a structured verification workflow that we have modeled, not timed, at about half a minute for the two-tip check:

1. **Click *Detect Needles* (4 seconds for the production code path run headless; interface overhead not yet timed):** The software crops the template plane automatically and reconstructs the 3D catheter bundle. Because our non-inferiority analysis shows that the reconstructed shafts (Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}) are statistically non-inferior to human inter-observer agreement with Team 1 as reference, this modeled workflow assumes no manual shaft editing; we did not measure how often shafts are edited.
2. **Check the few tips with the widest uncertainty bars (18 to 66 seconds simulated):** The module is designed to list the catheter tips with the widest split-conformal uncertainty intervals ($\hat{\sigma}_{\text{tip}}$) first. Because the shaft is already traced, correcting a flagged tip means sliding its endpoint along the track, which we model at 8 seconds per tip. Correcting only the two most uncertain tips per patient (18 seconds) is the configuration we carried through the TG-43U1 dose simulation: it lowers median target dose error $|\Delta D_{90}|$ from 4.2% to **2.8%** (mean from 8.2% to 5.9%). Extending the check to the eight most uncertain tips (66 seconds) was evaluated on tip geometry only; it brings the 95th-percentile tip error below $10\text{ mm}$ ($9.90\text{ mm}$, mean $2.77\text{ mm}$). In the dose simulation the flagged tip is placed exactly at its reference depth; in the tip-geometry curve a residual of up to $0.85\text{ mm}$ along the shaft is retained. Either way the simulated physicist never errs, so these numbers are upper bounds on the benefit of review.
3. **Reconcile the catheter count (assumed 5 seconds):** The clinician knows how many needles went through the template ($N_{\text{implant}}$). If the software reports more, the physicist deletes the extra tracks (2.7 unmatched predictions per patient at the default threshold, of which 1.2 are genuine false alarms on vessels or fascia); we did not simulate this deletion, and the Protocol B dose simulation loads every predicted track, so its unreviewed numbers include these tracks. If the software reports fewer, one click near the missing catheter promotes the highest-scoring candidate track that Stage 2B had rejected there, rather than tracing from scratch. Only misses that still exist in the Stage 2A candidate pool can be recovered this way, so in simulation $0.65$ such promotions per patient raise 1-to-1 recall from 91.4% to **93.0%** ($94.2\%$ closest-match recall) at the cost of 18 additional false tracks (precision $86.9\% \to 85.8\%$). Moreover, during progressive needle insertion in intraoperative MRI suites, the $1.31\text{ s}$ causal 4D Kalman mode could in principle alert the implanting physician after each interim scan if a newly inserted needle pushes a neighboring catheter out of position or deflects toward an organ at risk; we evaluated it only retrospectively on 23 scans from 4 patients.

## Limitations and Future Directions

Four limitations of the present study point directly to future work:
1. **Longitudinal tip ambiguity on thick slices.** While our 1D axial tip localizer cuts mean tip error to $6.23\text{ mm}$ and median tip error to $2.26\text{--}2.27\text{ mm}$ ($58.7\%\text{--}59.0\%$ within $\le 3.0\text{ mm}$), $16.7\%$ of unreviewed catheters still have a longitudinal tip error exceeding $10\text{ mm}$ when a catheter passes through a multi-slice signal dropout or terminates directly beneath dark bowel gas on 3 to 6 mm thick slices. Although simulated uncertainty-guided review of 5 to 8 tips contains this tail in our oracle model, combining T2W planning scans with thin-slice isotropic 3D sequences ($\le 1\text{ mm}$) or zero-echo-time sequences [@kaza2022; @lee2023] is the natural path to reducing unreviewed tip outliers further.
2. **Single-institution clinical implant cohort.** While we tested zero-shot transfer on 48 external public prostate scans, one 3T prostate brachytherapy MRI, three other MRI pulse sequences, 4D insertion series, and paired CT scans, all implanted brachytherapy patients were treated at Brigham and Women's Hospital. Prospective multi-center validation across 1.5T, 3T, and emerging low-field MRI systems [@major2026] from multiple scanner vendors is the essential next step.
3. **Standardized TG-43U1 anatomical envelopes and simulated review timings.** Because clinical DICOM RTSTRUCT and RTPLAN files were not archived across the historical 54-patient repository, our TG-43U1 dosimetric study used standardized target and organ-at-risk envelopes derived from the active implant geometry; the organ-at-risk shells lie in the steepest part of the dose gradient, which is why their proxy $D_{2\text{cc}}$ differences are several times larger than the target $D_{90}$ differences and must not be read as clinical organ doses. Our clinician tip-review curves used a quantitative 1-click oracle rather than prospective stopwatch trials in the operating room.
4. **Role of synthetic bundle pre-training (`SynthBrachy3T`).** Once Stage 1 is pre-trained on 51 external pelvic MRI scans and paired with collinear fragment stitching and the 1D tip localizer, `SynthBrachy3T` provides its largest detection boost when clinical bundle labels are scarce ($k = 0\text{ to }5$ patients, lifting zero-shot long-shaft $F1_{\text{adj}}$ by $+6.3$ points to $83.4\%$), while offering a smaller refinement at the full 43-patient training budget ($+0.4$ F1 points, while reducing dwell-weighted tip error from $6.65\text{ to }5.72\text{ mm}$ and counterfactual $|\Delta D_{90}|$ from $12.8\%\text{ to }8.2\%$).

# Conclusion

NeedleFinder 2026 reconstructs interstitial brachytherapy catheters directly on clinical 3T T2-weighted MRI. It combines an analytical dark-core symmetry filter (Stage 0), a 3D U-Net pre-trained on needle-free pelvic scans and fine-tuned on two expert teams' traces (Stage 1), and graph tracking with bundle-level scoring and a 1D axial tip localizer (Stage 2). Under strictly nested cross-validation on 54 patients and 1,063 catheters, it finds about nine catheters in ten with a median shaft error below half a millimeter and a median tip error of about 2 mm, in a few seconds on a workstation CPU (Table \ref{tbl:main}; Figure \ref{fig:spec_runtime}), and it outperforms our re-implementations of five peer-reviewed methods and one tracking preprint on the same splits. On 40 doubly traced scans its disagreement with one expert team is no larger than the disagreement between the two teams, and paired post-implant CT confirms that half of its apparent false alarms are real catheters (Table \ref{tbl:interobserver}). Its calibrated tip uncertainty supports a brief simulated check in 3D Slicer: moving the two most uncertain tips per patient to their reference depth, modeled at 18 seconds, lowers simulated target dose error from 4.2% to 2.8% median (Table \ref{tbl:dosimetry_generalization}). Without retraining, the pipeline also runs on one prostate implant, two other T2-type MRI sequences, serial intra-procedural scans and, with lower recall, post-contrast T1 MRI and pelvic CT.
