## Summary
Evaluates the impact of random and systematic applicator-reconstruction uncertainties on DVH parameters in MRI-based brachytherapy of cervical cancer. Dose plans of 20 patients were analysed; reconstruction uncertainty was modelled by translating and rotating the applicator, and DVH changes per mm of displacement were computed for GTV, CTV, bladder, rectum and sigmoid, then used to derive population estimates of delivered versus expected dose. The most sensitive organs are rectum and bladder, with mean DVH-parameter shifts of 5-6 % per mm of applicator displacement in the anterior-posterior direction; for other directions and parameters mean shifts are below 4 % per mm. If systematic errors are avoided, DVH uncertainties can be kept below 10 % in 90 % of patients; systematic errors of a few millimetres can cause significant deviations. The authors recommend comprehensive QC of afterloader, applicators and imaging, and small slice thickness to limit random errors.
