## Summary
Kaza et al. (Brigham and Women's Hospital) optimized a pointwise encoding time reduction with radial acquisition (PETRA) sequence to detect empty interstitial HDR catheters with positive signal. In a gelatin phantom, empty catheters displayed positive signal on PETRA and more accurate cross-sections than on clinical T2-weighted sequences, differing by 0.4 mm on average from their nominal 2 mm diameter, with minimal susceptibility differences and a symmetric metal artifact. PETRA-CT catheter tip differences assessed in a treatment planning system were below 1 mm, and the interstitial catheter positional difference between PETRA and CT was below 1 mm on average, increasing with distance from isocenter. In a first clinical implementation on an endometrial adenocarcinoma patient, all interstitial catheters and the template were detected and the empty needles were traceable with higher spatial resolution and more favorable contrast than on the T2-weighted contouring images.

## Key facts for our paper
- Zero-TE-type (PETRA) sequence gives positive signal from empty 2 mm catheters; cross-section error 0.4 mm vs nominal diameter.
- PETRA vs CT tip differences < 1 mm; shaft positional difference < 1 mm on average.
- First clinical implementation on one gynecologic patient; precedes [@lee2023] from the same BWH group.

## Suggested use in our manuscript
Discussion, 'What It Means in the Clinic, and Limitations', where zero-echo-time sequences are proposed to reduce tip error. Suggested sentence: 'PETRA imaging shows empty 2 mm catheters as positive signal with sub-millimeter agreement to CT for both shafts and tips [@kaza2022; @lee2023], so adding such a series as a second input channel is a natural extension.'
