## Summary
Huang et al. evaluated the impact of CT slice thickness on reconstruction of stainless-steel tandem-ovoid applicators in 17 cervical cancer patients by reconstructing 0.625, 1.25, 2.50 and 5.00 mm image sets from the same raw data. The partial-volume effect caused reconstruction uncertainties predominantly as superior-inferior ovoid displacements, with maximum uncertainties approaching the slice thickness. Each 1 mm superior-inferior displacement caused HR-CTV D90 deviations of 1.23% (right) and 2.47% (left), and each 1 mm anterior-posterior displacement caused rectal D2cc deviations of 1.71% and 2.34%; thicker slices (2.50 and 5.00 mm) overestimated HR-CTV D90 by 2.86% and 11.11% on average (maximum 7.06% and 25.55%). The authors recommend reconstructing 0.625 mm images for applicator reconstruction.

## Key facts for our paper
- Reconstruction uncertainty along the applicator axis approaches the slice thickness; 17 patients, CT.
- 1 mm along-axis displacement -> 1.2 to 2.5% HR-CTV D90; 5 mm slices overestimate D90 by 11% on average.
- Supports interpreting our tip error relative to the 1.2 to 6.0 mm slice thickness.

## Suggested use in our manuscript
Discussion, 'What It Means in the Clinic, and Limitations', in the sentence placing the median tip error within one slice thickness. Suggested sentence: 'Tip uncertainty along the catheter axis is inherently bounded by the slice thickness, and on CT each millimeter of along-axis displacement changes HR-CTV D90 by 1.2 to 2.5% [@huang2026], which argues for thinner slices in the T2W protocol.'
