## Summary
Singh et al. assessed the dosimetric impact of systematic source position uncertainty along the source path in MRI-only cervical HDR brachytherapy and evaluated robust re-planning strategies. Treatment plans of 22 cervical cancer patients (11 intracavitary, ICBT; 11 interstitial, ISBT) were retrospectively analyzed by shifting the source position within the applicators by ±1 to ±5 mm in 1 mm steps (Oncentra Brachy offset feature) and recomputing HRCTV D90 and OAR D2cc. In ICBT, D2cc changes were significant at all shift positions for bladder and sigmoid (p ≤ 0.001) and for positive shifts (1 to 5 mm) for rectum (p ≤ 0.04), while bowel was not significant; HRCTV D90 was significantly affected only from -2 to -5 mm (p ≤ 0.042). In ISBT all OARs were significantly affected at all shifts (p ≤ 0.003) and HRCTV D90 at all shifts except -1 and -2 mm (p ≤ 0.033). Within ±5 mm the mean (maximum) HRCTV D90 drop was -2.0 ± 1.9 (-5.4)% for ICBT and -6.2 ± 1.4 (-11.9)% for ISBT, and bladder D2cc rose by up to 6.0 ± 3.4 (13.6)% (ICBT) and 5.2 ± 4.6 (17.2)% (ISBT). Targeted re-optimization of dwell times/gradients within 1 cm of the affected OAR improved robustness.

## Key facts for our paper
- 22 patients (11 ICBT, 11 ISBT), FIGO IB2-IVA, MRI-only cervical HDR (Methods).
- Shifts of ±1 to ±5 mm in 1 mm steps along the source path; 0 mm = reference (Methods, Fig. 1b).
- ICBT: bladder and sigmoid D2cc significant at all shift positions (p ≤ 0.001), rectum at +1 to +5 mm (p ≤ 0.04), bowel not significant (p ≥ 0.197); HRCTV D90 significant at -2 to -5 mm only (Results, Figs. 2-3).
- ISBT: all OARs significant at all shifts (p ≤ 0.003); HRCTV D90 significant at all shifts except -1 and -2 mm (p ≤ 0.033) (Results, Fig. 4).
- Mean ICBT D2cc gradients: -85 cGy/mm bladder, 10 cGy/mm bowel, 18 cGy/mm rectum, 23 cGy/mm sigmoid (Results).
- Within ±5 mm: HRCTV D90 up to -2.0 ± 1.9 (-5.4)% ICBT and -6.2 ± 1.4 (-11.9)% ISBT; bladder D2cc up to 6.0 ± 3.4 (13.6)% ICBT, 5.2 ± 4.6 (17.2)% ISBT (abstract).
