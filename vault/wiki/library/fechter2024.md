## Summary
Fechter, Sachpazidis and Baltas review the role of deep learning across all processes of interventional radiotherapy (brachytherapy), with explanations of key terms and a second focus on the availability of open source code, open data and open models needed for reproducibility. They find deep learning already plays a major role in some areas (notably segmentation and registration) but is hardly present in others, with influence growing partly through related fields. Open source, data and models are growing but remain scarce and unevenly distributed: in the full text, all 6 public datasets identified (Table 2) contain solely MR images intended for prostate tissue segmentation/classification, 20 manuscripts came with public code and 6 with trained models (Table 3), for 25 manuscripts in total with code, data or models, and brachytherapy-specific code or models are described as scarce. The authors conclude that reluctance to publish code, data and models limits reproducibility and restricts evaluation to mono-institutional datasets. Published online 12 Nov 2022; print issue May 2024.

## Key facts for our paper
- All 6 public datasets found "contain solely MR images and are intended for segmentation and/or classification of prostate tissue" (Section on open data, Table 2); no public needle, catheter or applicator dataset is listed.
- 20 papers with public code, 6 with trained models; 25 with code, data or models in total (Table 3).
- "The reluctance in publishing code, data and models limits reproducibility and restricts evaluation to mono-institutional datasets" (abstract).

## How our manuscript cites it
- "No public needle or catheter dataset for brachytherapy exists [@fechter2024]": SUPPORTED as of the review's literature search (all public datasets found are MR prostate segmentation/classification sets). Consider "existed as of the 2022 review" or "no public ... dataset has been reported", since the review's search predates 2023.
