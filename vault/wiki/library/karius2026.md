## Summary
Karius et al. studied in-situ needle bending and its implications for external needle tracking (e.g. optical tracking) in combined intracavitary/interstitial gynecologic brachytherapy. They developed a semi-automated needle reconstruction on planning CT (accuracy 0.17 ± 0.08 mm, maximum 0.46 mm, versus a manual intra-observer variability of 0.21 ± 0.11 mm measured on 53 needles of 10 patients) and used it to reconstruct 679 needles of 89 cervical cancer patients treated 2018 to mid-2023. Bending, measured as the Euclidean distance between the actual tip and the tip predicted by extrapolating the distal straight segment, rose with needle length from 3.6 ± 2.1 mm (100-120 mm needles) to 7.9 ± 3.0 mm (200-220 mm), averaging 5.9 ± 3.0 mm (range 0.7-25.7 mm); tip deviations exceeded 1, 5 and 10 mm for 99%, 58% and 21% of needles. Transverse deflections (5.1 ± 3.0 mm) were much larger than longitudinal ones (1.3 ± 0.4 mm), and inter-needle spacing deviated from the intended equidistance by 1.4 ± 1.2 mm transversally. The authors conclude that transverse bending must be corrected for in tracking-based prediction approaches.

## Key facts for our paper
- 89 patients, 679 needles, planning CT, combined IC/IS gynecologic brachytherapy (Methods, Results).
- Semi-automatic CT needle reconstruction accuracy 0.17 ± 0.08 mm (max 0.46 mm); manual intra-observer variability 0.21 ± 0.11 mm (Results).
- Tip deviation from straight-line prediction: 5.9 ± 3.0 mm overall; 3.6 ± 2.1 mm (100-120 mm) to 7.9 ± 3.0 mm (200-220 mm needles); >1 mm in 99%, >5 mm in 58%, >10 mm in 21% of needles (Results, table of bending by length).
- Transverse deflection 5.1 ± 3.0 mm vs longitudinal 1.3 ± 0.4 mm; spacing deviation 1.4 ± 1.2 mm (Results).

## How our manuscript cites it
- "First, tips are harder than shafts, here as in every modality [@wangf2020; @karius2026]": NOT SUPPORTED. The paper does not compare the reconstruction accuracy of tips with that of shafts; its tip "deviations" are the displacement of the actual tip from a straight-line (tracking) prediction caused by needle bending, not a localization error. Suggested fix: either drop karius2026 from this sentence, or cite it for what it shows, e.g. "needles bend in situ by 3.6 to 7.9 mm depending on length, so tip position cannot be inferred from the shaft direction [@karius2026]".
