## Summary
Wang et al. (BWH: Viswanathan, Damato, Schmidt, Cormack) evaluated an active MR tracking (MRTR) system in which a stylet with shaft-mounted microcoils is inserted into each brachytherapy catheter and tracked at 40 Hz by a dedicated sequence during withdrawal, giving 3D positions along the trajectory to which a polynomial curve is fitted. The authors state that a major obstacle to intraprocedural dosimetry is the time needed for catheter trajectory reconstruction. In a phantom with fifteen catheters, the mean 3D error of MRTR trajectories relative to MRI-based digitization was 1.5 +/- 0.5 mm, with an in-plane error of 0.7 +/- 0.2 mm and a tip error of 1.7 +/- 0.5 mm; tip localization error increased off-center and as the stylet orientation deviated from B0. MRTR resolved catheter-assignment ambiguity due to crossed catheter paths, described as a common problem in image-based digitization, and in three gynecologic patients the MRTR trajectories were consistent with MRI- and CT-based digitization.

## Key facts for our paper
- Active MR tracking of a microcoil stylet for catheter reconstruction: in-plane 0.7 mm, tip 1.7 mm, 3D 1.5 mm vs MRI digitization (15 phantom catheters, 3 patients).
- Explicitly identifies crossed catheter paths (adhesion) as a common problem of image-based digitization.
- Hardware-based alternative from the same BWH program.
