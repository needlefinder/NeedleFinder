## Summary
Nunez et al. used automated simulations to quantify the clinical severity of HDR prostate brachytherapy physics errors selected from an internal FMEA. Twenty-nine failure modes were scored by 8 physicists (RPN 6 to 108, mean 46 ± 23, high inter-rater variability). On six idealized plans they simulated single (N = 1722) and systematic (N = 126) catheter shifts (craniocaudal, -1 to 1 cm), single-catheter digitization errors (tip and connector end displaced independently, 0.1 to 0.5 cm; N = 44,318) and swaps of two catheters (N = 528), evaluating prostate D90%, urethra D20% and rectum D1cm3 against 5-20% (possible clinical impact) and >20% (potentially reportable) thresholds. Potentially reportable events occurred for systematic shifts >0.4 cm (prostate) and digitization errors >0.3 cm (urethra) and >0.4 cm (rectum). Possible clinical impact (5-20%) was observed for catheter swaps in all organs, systematic shifts >0.2 cm (prostate) and >0.4 cm (rectum), and digitization errors >0.2 cm (prostate) and >0.1 cm (urethra, rectum). Swaps did not produce deviations >20%; the paper states digitization errors are more likely than swaps to result in medical events.

## Key facts for our paper
- Catheter swaps (all pairwise swaps in 6 plans, N = 528) produced 5-20% deviations in prostate D90%, urethra D20% and rectum D1cm3, but no deviations >20% (abstract; Results, Fig. 2D / Appendix 2; no mean/max values given in text).
- Digitization errors >0.2 cm (prostate) and >0.1 cm (urethra, rectum) reached the 5-20% band; >0.3 cm (urethra) and >0.4 cm (rectum) exceeded 20% (abstract).
- Single catheter shifts exceeded 5% only above 0.7 cm; shifts up to 1 cm never exceeded 20% (Results).
- Perceived severity score for swaps 5.6 ± 1.3 vs 5.5 ± 2.1 for digitization errors; RPN for swaps 58.7 ± 34.2 (Table 2).

## How our manuscript cites it
- "Swapping two catheters is worse still [@nunez2021]" (following sentences on 1-1.5 mm shifts changing OAR dose by 5-10%): NOT SUPPORTED as a comparative. The source reports that swaps cause possible clinical impact (5-20% deviations) in all three dose metrics but never >20%, and explicitly states digitization errors are more likely to produce medical events than swaps; it does not rank swaps as worse than millimeter-scale shifts. Suggested wording: "Swapping two catheters during digitization changes prostate, urethra and rectum dose metrics by 5 to 20% [@nunez2021]."
