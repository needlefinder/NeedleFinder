## Summary
Proposes an MRI-only method to digitize both the applicator and the interstitial catheters for cervical cancer brachytherapy, removing the need for CT and MRI-CT registration. Applicator digitization uses applicator mesh reconstruction, ring identification and alignment of the mesh model to the MRI; catheter digitization uses an uncertainty-aware deep-learning model that segments catheters and estimates prediction uncertainty, which drives initial localization and refinement. Data: 35 T2-weighted MRIs from 30 cervical cancer patients treated with the Venezia applicator, split 80% development / 20% test. All applicators were digitized with mean translation and rotation errors of 1.13 +/- 0.26 mm and 2.19 +/- 2.09 degrees; all catheters except one were digitized, with shaft and tip errors of 0.74 +/- 0.32 mm and 2.52 +/- 2.04 mm. Plans from automatic digitization showed no significant differences from clinical plans (p > 0.05).
