## Summary
Richart et al. review strategies for MRI-based reconstruction of endocavitary and interstitial applicators in cervical cancer brachytherapy, covering MR-compatible applicators, MR sequences, patient preparation, and applicator commissioning. They recommend performing reconstruction on the same T2-weighted study used by the physician for contouring, but note that clear identification of the source path inside catheters and applicators is a challenge when only T2W sequences are used. Reviewed strategies include filling intracavitary catheters with high-signal substances (not feasible for every interstitial component), library models of the intracavitary applicators with recent extensions to the interstitial component, MR markers such as small pellets used as anchor points, supporting CT with carefully assessed CT-MR registration uncertainty, and ongoing work on ultrasound and electromagnetic tracking.

## Key facts for our paper
- Review of MRI-based applicator reconstruction in cervix brachytherapy (2018).
- Recommends reconstructing on the T2W contouring sequence; explicitly states source-path identification on T2W alone is a challenge.
- Lists the mitigation strategies: signal-producing fillers, library models, MR pellet markers, CT support with registration, US and EM tracking.

## Suggested use in our manuscript
Introduction, 'The Clinical Problem' or 'Previous Work', to frame why T2W-only catheter identification is hard. Suggested sentence: 'Reconstruction is recommended on the same T2W series used for contouring, yet identifying the source path inside the catheters on T2W images alone remains a recognized challenge, which clinics address with marker fillers, library models, pellet markers, or a supporting CT [@richart2018].'
