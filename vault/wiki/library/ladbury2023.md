## Summary
Ladbury et al. provide a practical guide to hybrid intracavitary/interstitial brachytherapy for locally advanced cervical cancer, in which transvaginal and/or perineal needles are added to a tandem and ring/ovoids either through a specialized applicator or freehand. They state that during treatment planning it can be helpful to obtain both MRI and CT, because plastic MRI-compatible needles do not show up well on MRI. Hybrid brachytherapy typically extends dose coverage by an additional 1 to 2 cm beyond intracavitary-only techniques and is preferred for bulky residual tumor, lateral parametrial extension, vaginal extension, and asymmetric targets.

## Key facts for our paper
- Expert practical guide (2023) explicitly notes that plastic MRI-compatible needles 'do not show up well on MRI', motivating a supplementary CT.
- Hybrid IC/IS extends coverage by 1 to 2 cm beyond intracavitary-only brachytherapy.

## Suggested use in our manuscript
Introduction, 'The Clinical Problem', where the manuscript explains why clinics historically acquired both MRI and CT.
