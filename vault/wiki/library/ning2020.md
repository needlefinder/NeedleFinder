## Summary
Ning et al. evaluated an MRI-positive line marker made of C4:S (a cobalt chloride-based contrast agent) encapsulated in high-density polyethylene tubing in nine sequential gynecologic brachytherapy procedures (six tandem-and-ovoid, three interstitial). The markers showed the strongest positive signal on 3D CISS/FIESTA-C followed by T1-weighted sequences, permitting delineation of the applicator lumen and source path; these images can be fused with T2-weighted sequences for contouring so that planning can be done directly on MRI. Using time-driven activity-based costing, the authors argue that eliminating the postoperative CT for fusion and applicator registration could reduce workflow time and cost.

## Key facts for our paper
- Positive-contrast MRI line marker (C4:S in HDPE tubing) tested in 9 procedures (6 T&O, 3 interstitial).
- Best visibility on 3D CISS/FIESTA-C and T1-weighted sequences; requires fusion with the T2W contouring series.
- Rationale: eliminating the postoperative CT and registration step.
