## Summary
de Arcos et al. (BWH) prospectively used dedicated actively MR-tracked (MRTR) stylets in eleven gynecologic cancer patients to optimize interstitial catheter placement, with catheter tip location and orientation overlaid on in-room monitors at 6 to 16 frames per second. Coarse navigation repositioned catheter tips by 16 mm (mean) relative to baseline in 14 +/- 5 s per catheter, fine-tuning by a further 12 mm in 24 +/- 17 s per catheter, and a pullback mode provided catheter trajectories for treatment planning with about 1.5 mm point resolution in 1 to 9 s per catheter. In pullback mode the catheter trajectories matched CT precision, enabling their use for radiation treatment planning.

## Key facts for our paper
- Prospective clinical trial, 11 patients, active MR tracking for catheter navigation and trajectory reconstruction (pullback mode, ~1.5 mm resolution, 1 to 9 s per catheter).
- Trajectories matched CT precision; requires custom tracked stylets and a dedicated sequence.
