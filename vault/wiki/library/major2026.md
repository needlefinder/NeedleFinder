## Summary
Major et al. evaluated the geometric accuracy of applicator and needle reconstruction for gynecological brachytherapy on a 0.55 T low-field MR scanner. In a phantom study, seven plastic needles inserted into three phantoms were scanned with MR and CT and five observers marked tip and entry points; needle lengths and tip coordinates were compared. In patients, inter-observer variation of applicator and needle tip localization was evaluated on MR images of 20 fractions from 11 patients treated with a Venezia applicator plus needles, with MR markers in the tandem and ovoid tubes and only air in the needles. The mean difference in needle depth between MR and CT was 1.16 ± 0.8 mm, inter-observer SD of tip position was below 1.0 mm for both modalities, and tip coordinates on MR and CT correlated with R^2 > 0.99. In patients, the SD of applicator tips with MR markers was smaller than for needle tips without markers (X 0.18 vs 0.27 mm, Y 0.24 vs 0.58 mm, Z 0.21 vs 0.34 mm), with the largest variability in the Y direction corresponding to the larger slice thickness; the authors conclude that 0.55 T MRI provides clinically acceptable reconstruction accuracy comparable to CT.

## Key facts for our paper
- 0.55 T MR scanner; 3 phantoms with 7 plastic needles; 5 observers; 11 patients, 20 fractions, Venezia applicator with needles (abstract).
- MR-vs-CT needle depth difference 1.16 ± 0.8 mm; inter-observer tip SD < 1.0 mm; R^2 > 0.99 (abstract).
- Patient tip SD, marker applicator vs unmarked needles: X 0.18 vs 0.27 mm, Y 0.24 vs 0.58 mm, Z 0.21 vs 0.34 mm (abstract).
