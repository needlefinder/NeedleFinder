## Summary
Androulakis et al. used an afterloader-integrated electromagnetic tracking (EMT) system to detect errors in TRUS-based implant reconstructions in 14 prostate HDR patients treated intraoperatively, measuring dwell positions at 5 mm intervals in all implanted needles before treatment. The median Euclidean distance between EMT-measured and clinically reconstructed dwell positions was 1.0 mm (range 0.1 to 9.4 mm), with larger errors in the anterior and lateral prostate. Of 265 needle reconstructions, 23% (61/265) had at least a minor error (minimum 2 mm) and 9% (24/265) had major or severe errors; severe errors were mostly caused by incorrect needle or depth selection, and major, moderate and minor errors by artifacts, shadowing and user errors, respectively.

## Key facts for our paper
- 14 patients / 265 needle reconstructions on intraoperative TRUS; median dwell-position error 1.0 mm (max 9.4 mm).
- 23% of needle reconstructions had errors >= 2 mm; 9% major or severe; artifacts and shadowing are leading causes.
- Quantifies how often manual reconstruction is wrong in routine practice.
