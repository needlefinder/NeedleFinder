## Summary
Part B of AAPM Medical Physics Practice Guideline 13.a sets minimum practice standards for Ir-192 HDR brachytherapy clinical processes, from patient arrival through site-specific imaging, planning, treatment delivery, emergency procedures and error mitigation (Part A covered program infrastructure). The full text states that CT is the most commonly used imaging modality and that "other modalities such as US, MRI, orthogonal x-rays, fluoroscopy, mammography, or cone beam computed tomography (CBCT) can also be used for patient and applicator imaging and planning"; Table 1 gives site-specific imaging recommendations, and the prostate treatment table lists "US (+/- MR informed)", "CT (+/- MR informed)", "MR" and "MR guided" as treatment-planning imaging modalities. It also notes that MRI leaves large signal voids where applicators lie, so applicator geometry must be understood before insertion.

## Key facts for our paper
- MRI is listed among the modalities that "can also be used for patient and applicator imaging and planning" (full text, Imaging section) and as a prostate treatment-planning modality (prostate treatment table).
- Timelines quoted: 5 to 8 h implant-to-release for MRI-based prostate; 9 h for interstitial GYN reducing to 4-6 h in an image-guided suite (full text, timelines paragraph).
