## Summary
Kallis et al. evaluated inter- (IOV) and intra-observer (IAV) variability of catheter reconstruction on CT and its dosimetric impact for multi-catheter interstitial breast brachytherapy. Three medical physicists reconstructed the catheter traces of 13 patients (compared also with the planning system's automatic reconstruction and one deliberately imprecise manual reconstruction); IAV used repeated reconstructions by two physicists on 13 patients; 426 catheters were considered in total. With dwell times, dwell positions, optimization and normalization kept constant, the geometric deviation between corresponding dwell positions was 0.60 ± 0.35 mm for IOV (first observer 0.54 ± 0.32 mm, second 0.58 ± 0.37 mm), catheter trace length varied by 0.51 ± 0.45 mm, and IAV was 0.49 ± 0.30 mm (IOV and IAV significantly different, p < 0.01). Dosimetric indices changed little (CI 0.27 ± 0.31%, DNR 0.0027 ± 0.0025, COIN 0.0036 ± 0.0033, mean heart dose 0.024 ± 0.019%, mean lung dose 0.05 ± 0.11%); skin D0.2cc changed up to 8.52%. The authors conclude repeated reconstruction does not cause large geometric or dosimetric deviations but note the lack of ground truth.
